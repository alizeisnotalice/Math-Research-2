# A local Fourier obstruction for a Cantor set of variance zeros on the line

Let the input have a smooth positive probability density g near a bounded interval I (and any finite input mass outside), let v be bounded continuous nonnegative, and suppose the output has a smooth density in I. Inside I suppose a compact affine copy E of a digit Cantor set has a neighborhood on which its points are variance zeros and

    v(x)=dist(x,E)^α (c(x)+o(1)),   0<α<2,

uniformly as dist(x,E)↓0, with c continuous positive there. Assume v is positive off E in this neighborhood. Here the Cantor set comes from a base b≥3 and a fixed digit set D containing 0,b−1 with 2≤m=|D|<b. Then these assumptions are incompatible. Only local data near E enter; arbitrary other variance zeros and nonsmooth variance are allowed elsewhere. In particular the conclusion applies to the reduced Gaussian-output variance v=sup f−f. When α≥2 a one-sided quadratic upper contact alone does not in general contradict the nonuniform Gaussian output on the line; the present theorem concerns α<2.

**Localization in the physical output.** Choose x₀∈E in the interior of its convex hull and take a small open interval J about it, compactly inside the neighborhood. Let ψ be a smooth nonnegative cutoff supported inside J, strictly positive near x₀. Shrink J so that |c(x)/c₀−1|≤η there, c₀=c(x₀); decreasing the distance threshold if necessary ensures the analogous bound with 2η for v/dist^α. For any frequency n→∞ (in radians per unit x), smoothness of the exact output gives a rapidly decreasing Fourier transform against ψ. The part with input outside J does too: it is smooth near the support of ψ by positive separation and the elementary bound |p_s^(j)(z)|≤C_j|z|^(−j−1), uniformly for s>0; deterministic sources outside J give zero there.

For input inside J, Fourier inversion of ψ expresses the conditional expectation of ψ(Y)e^{inY} as the integral of its rapidly decreasing Fourier amplitudes at ξ times exp[i(n+ξ)x−(n+ξ)²v(x)/2]. On replacing the exponential damping by exp(−n²v(x)/2), the integral error is O(n⁻¹∫_J exp(−n²v/16)g)+O_H(n^(−H)) for all H: on |ξ|≤n/2 the mean value bound is C|ξ|n⁻¹exp(−n²v/16); use rapid decay outside. Thus, writing Z for any comparable scale of local Gibbs mass,

    ∫ ψ(x)g(x)e^{inx} exp(−n²v(x)/2)dx=o(Z)

provided Z decays at most polynomially and the local mass at the weaker damping is O(Z). Constants may depend on the fixed J and ψ.

**Frozen variance on the Cantor set.** Write [A,A+S] for the convex hull of E and z=(x−A)/S. Put d=log_b m. In these coordinates consider the weights W_e=exp(−(dist(z,C)/e)^α), e→0. On [0,1], their masses Z_e obey Z_e≍e^(1−d). Indeed cylinders of length b^(−k) comparable to e give matching upper and lower bounds for the e-neighborhood (finite endpoint overlaps do not affect length). Integrating the tube bound gives the same two-sided estimate for W_e. The probability W_e/Z_e converges weakly to the equal-digit Cantor probability μ: for any fixed level k, the m^k cylinders have equal masses by rescaling, and the remaining finitely many intervals have total weighted mass O_k(e). The endpoints of each cylinder belong to its Cantor copy, so the nearest point can always be found in that copy. The mass outside [0,1] near its two endpoints is O(e).

Choose a nonzero integer ℓ with μ̂(ℓ)≠0 (otherwise μ would be Haar by trigonometric polynomial density). Set n=2πℓ b^k/S, taking ℓ>0 by conjugation. For every continuous test a on the hull, if b^k e→0 then

    ∫ a(A+Sz) e^{inSz} W_e(z)dz / Z_e → μ̂(ℓ) ∫ a(A+Sz)dμ(z).

To prove this, discard the gaps before level k, of total weighted mass O(m^k e)=O((b^k e)^d Z_e). Freeze a at one endpoint of each remaining cylinder with error o(Z_e). At those endpoints the phase is 1; inside each cylinder the same ratio of integrals occurs, namely the fixed ℓ Fourier coefficient at the changed width b^k e, which tends to μ̂(ℓ). The unweighted cylinder averages of a approach its μ integral. This argument holds for every e comparable to n^(−2/α), in particular for c₀ and for any fixed weaker damping.

**Comparison with the actual variance.** Use e=S⁻¹(n²c₀/2)^(−1/α), Z=Z_e. For a=ψg, its mean against μ is strictly positive. On its support away from small distance to E, both kernels are exponentially small. Elsewhere the pointwise difference between real and frozen damping is ≤Cη exp(−n²c₀ dist(x,E)^α/4) for η≤1/8. Its weighted integral is bounded, relative to Z_e times the μ mean of ψg, by C'η in the limit: the unmodulated test converges with that very same mean at the weaker damping, and Z_{2^(1/α)e}/Z_e is bounded independently of J,ψ,η,c₀. Jacobians and the harmless common phase e^{inA} are identical for the two comparisons. This dependence on the local mean permits choosing η small before shrinking J. The unweighted local mass at damping n²v/16 is O(Z_e) by the tube bound, so the localization error is o(Z_e) for this fixed cutoff. Taking C'η<|μ̂(ℓ)|/2 leaves a nonzero normalized Fourier amplitude, contradicting smoothness. No arithmetic alignment of any zero outside J was required.

**Finite local regularity obstruction.** In fact the output measure cannot belong to the local Sobolev space H^β in a neighborhood of the indicated Cantor copy, where β=2(1−d)/α. In the proof retain the measured Fourier transform of ψ times the actual output rather than setting it rapidly decreasing. The exterior-input term alone is rapidly decreasing, regardless of the regularity of the full output. For frequencies n+s on a fixed interval |s|≤s₀, the localization error estimates are uniform. Replacing n+s by n in the dampings incurs O(Z_e/n) by the same estimate; the extra phase multiplies ψg by exp(isx). At s=0 the frozen limit is nonzero, and it stays bounded away from zero for all sufficiently small s₀ since this modified test differs uniformly by O(s₀) times ψg in absolute value on J. This bound relative to its mean can also be seen directly inside the Fourier integral from |exp(isx)−1|≤|s|sup_J|x|. The perturbation η is taken as above. Thus for some C>0, every large k, and all |s|≤s₀, the absolute Fourier transform of the cutoff output at n+s is ≥C |n|^(−β). These frequency intervals are disjoint eventually. Each contributes at least a fixed positive amount to the integral of (1+|ξ|²)^β times the squared Fourier transform. This proves the asserted endpoint Sobolev failure for the output measure in the distributional sense.

The fixed-frequency self-similar measure and its classical Cantor Fourier products have earlier references; see [Jessen–Wintner, §6, Example 1](https://www.ams.org/tran/1935-038-01/S0002-9947-1935-1501802-5/S0002-9947-1935-1501802-5.pdf#page=14). The proof above supplies the moving-frequency localization and variable-coefficient estimate on the line.

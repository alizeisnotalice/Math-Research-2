# Isolated terminal layers and deterministic spatial sets

Suppose X∼γ and F is a bounded nonnegative mark satisfying the exact transform for all real z. Suppose F has a highest level H of positive mass and all lower marks are at most H−δ, δ>0. If the conditional law of F given X is deterministic on a set of positive Gaussian measure, then F=1 almost surely.

Indeed analytic continuation to imaginary z gives the final spatial heat identity p_H=ρ_H+∫_{F<H}p_{H−F}(·−x)dμ, for H>0, where μ is the marked law and ρ_H its terminal-layer spatial measure. The degenerate case H=0 is incompatible with EF=1 from the second transform coefficient. The integral defines an entire spatial function by the uniform positive variance gap. Thus ρ_H has a real analytic density, between 0 and g. On a deterministic region ρ_H(g−ρ_H)=0 almost everywhere. A real analytic function vanishing on a positive-measure set is identically zero, hence one factor vanishes identically. Positive terminal mass excludes ρ_H=0. Therefore F≡H and H=1. In particular no finite collection of levels can localize its mixing to a proper closed set with open complement.

There is also an obstruction with no gap assumption. If F is constant c throughout a half-line X≤a, its contribution to the tilt z=−t is exp((1−c)t²/2)Φ(a+t); the remaining contribution is at most exp(−ta)γ(X>a), which alone need not vanish if a<0. For c<1 the displayed contribution already diverges. When c=1, subtract the constant graph identity: on X>a the integral of exp(−tX+t²(1−F)/2) equals that of exp(−tX), at most exp(−ta). Any positive mass of F≤1−δ in a fixed bounded part of this ray violates the equality for large t. Hence F≥1 everywhere and the mean forces F≡1. For a≥0 the contribution on X>a tends to zero by Gaussian domination, so c>1 is also impossible. Reflection gives the opposite half-line. Only the stated cases of this half-line argument are asserted.

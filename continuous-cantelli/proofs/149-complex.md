# Simultaneous finite complex jets with uniform error on unbounded horizontal strips

In round 145 the approximating graphs can be chosen smooth with range exactly [1−ε,1+ε] and the same two anchors, weak mixed limit, and negative heat witness. For any increasing sequence B_n<∞ and positive tolerances e_n one may require

    sup_{|Im z|≤B_n} |∫exp(zx−z² f_n(x)/2)γ(dx)−1| ≤ e_n.

At each n one may also prescribe *exact* agreement of any finite list of derivatives of the transform with the constant function 1 at finitely many real or complex parameters, including any prescribed finite number of Gaussian moment equations at zero. Conjugate data are taken consistently. This is an existence result with degenerating local moduli, proved by finite-dimensional calibration, not a convergence claim for a uniformly regular sequence.

**Strip approximants.** The target three-layer law has entire transform 1 by analytic continuation. Truncation to mark 1 outside a fixed radius R>3 changes only the late layer P_εη. On a strip |Im z|≤B, both absolute kernels with mark at least 1 are bounded by exp((1+ε)B²/2)exp(x²/2), by maximization in Re z. The integrated truncation error tends to zero with R, since the late Gaussian convolution has variance ε<1 and compact original source. Within a fixed truncation radius all kernels with the marks in [1−ε,1+ε] are uniformly bounded and equicontinuous in x for all z on the strip, by the quadratic damping in Re z. Thus the cell matching, smoothing and anchoring in round 145 approximate uniformly on each such strip, as finely as desired before increasing the truncation radius. Cauchy's formula on a slightly larger strip gives all specified derivative convergence. Weak convergence of the joint laws holds as in round 145.

**An invertible derivative matrix on one fixed interval.** Choose any bounded open interval I with closure in the negative axis. Its limiting conditional middle-layer probability p₀(x) is strictly positive. Let τ be a smooth cutoff supported strictly inside the mark range and equal to one at mark 1. For a baseline graph g, use the corrections

    g(x)+τ(g(x))Σλ_i h_i(x),        h_i∈C_c^∞(I).

In the limiting joint law only the middle layer contributes to the linearization. At a parameter z the spatial derivative row against h_i is the integral of h_i p₀ times −(z²/2)exp(zx−z²/2) against γ. Parameter derivatives of any order are obtained by differentiating this row. At nonzero z the rows of orders 0 through k are exp(zx) times polynomials of respective degrees 0 through k, with nonzero leading coefficients. At zero the rows of orders 0 and 1 vanish identically (these conditions hold for every graph); rows of orders 2 through k are polynomials of consecutive degrees 0 through k−2 with nonzero leading coefficients. Include the missing intermediate rows if desired. For distinct complex exponents the resulting polynomial-exponential systems are linearly independent on I: applying sufficiently high powers of the constant-coefficient operators (∂x−w) for all exponents w other than the selected one isolates a nonzero exponential times polynomial. Taking real and imaginary parts for one member of each conjugate pair gives real-linear independence, while at real parameters only a real row is needed. The positive factor p₀ does not affect independence. Consequently finitely many bumps supported on the same I give an invertible real matrix.

For each fixed finite system the smooth approximants' Jacobians converge to this matrix by weak convergence, and second derivatives in the coefficients are locally uniformly bounded by compact spatial support. The quantitative inverse-function argument from round 145 supplies coefficients tending to zero that enforce every equation. The cutoff ensures all corrected values stay inside the original closed range for small coefficients, and both anchors remain fixed. The uniform change on the strip is arbitrarily small by boundedness of |z|² times the damped kernel on I. Impose also the mean equation via the second row at zero. Diagonal selection gives the advertised e_n and preserves weak convergence, the common negative witness and the local failure of equicontinuity.

If an explicitly nonexact sequence is desired, include the next previously unconstrained derivative at zero and prescribe a nonzero residual tending to zero sufficiently fast for each fixed finite system. The same invertible Jacobian gives this perturbation: first make the coefficient neighborhood small enough to preserve the required tolerance and weak-approximation error, and then choose the residual within its attainable neighborhood. Thus passing all of these recalibrated tests is compatible with a known failure on an omitted equation.

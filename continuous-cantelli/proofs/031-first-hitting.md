# First-hitting radii convert potential propagation to a local rigidity theorem

Throughout, x₀=0 in translated coordinates, and the Gaussian input density becomes ρ(x₀+u), bounded above and positive near zero. Assume v≥0 is locally Hölder of positive order α≤1 there. Under exactness the full positive potential is analytic, as shown in 031-flat-potential.md. Write H_s=½∫₀ˢp_t and S_R(y)=∫_{|u|<R}ρ(x₀+u)H_{v(x₀+u)}(y−u)du. On the real axis this is nonnegative. On every disk |z−c|≤3(R−|c|)/8 with c∈(−R,R) real it extends holomorphically and is bounded by a common constant B: the full analytic potential minus the exterior gives this extension since exterior means are at distance at least R−|c| from c, implying Re[(z−u)²]>0. B is uniform for small R; zero exterior variances contribute zero.

**Propagation chain.** Suppose |S_R|≤e≤B on |y|≤R/32. For any 0≤x<R,
S_R(±x) ≤ C B (e/B)^{c (1−x/R)^p},
with absolute constants C,c,p>0, p>2. For the positive side let b=15/16, d_l=Rb^l and c_l=R−d_l. Use the short-segment interpolation lemma in 031-flat-potential.md at each c_l with scale d_l/8 and normalized segment [−1/4,1/4]. The bound E_l on |y−c_l|≤d_l/32 propagates to |y−c_l|≤d_l/8 as 4B(E_l/B)^θ, θ=log2/log48. That larger interval contains both [c_l,c_{l+1}] and |y−c_{l+1}|≤d_{l+1}/32. For c_l≤x<c_{l+1}, repeated exponents give θ^{l+1}=θ(d_l/R)^p≥θ(1−x/R)^p, with p=logθ/log b>2. The product of the factors 4 is bounded by 4^{1/(1−θ)}. When an intermediate bound exceeds B, its trivial replacement by B only improves the estimate. Reflection gives the other sign.

**Hitting geometry.** Suppose every neighborhood of the origin contains a positive value on the side(s) under consideration. Take a sufficiently small realized m₀>0, set m_j=2^−j m₀ and let r_j be the first radius at which the maximum envelope reaches m_j. The domains for this envelope are [0,r] for a left plateau and [−r,r] for the bilateral assertion. Continuity and v(0)=0 give 0<r_j<r_{j−1}, r_j→0; the latter uses the supposition above. Put R=r_{j−1}; throughout |u|<R, v≤2m_j (in the one-sided case the other side is a plateau). At one of x_j=±r_j the variance equals m_j (take + for a left plateau). With Hölder constant K, take the interval of length ℓ=(m_j/(2K))^{1/α} immediately inward from x_j. It lies within the core, since r_j≥(m_j/K)^{1/α}. On it v≥m_j/2 and |u−x_j|≤ℓ≤√m_j for large j. Consequently H_{v(x₀+u)}(x_j−u)≥c√m_j, by restricting the heat integral to m_j/4≤t≤m_j/2. Hence S_R(x_j)≥c m_j^{1/α+1/2}. In particular −log S_R(x_j)≤C(j+1).

**A. Plateau boundary.** Suppose v=0 for −r₀<u≤0 and the positive side is not identically zero in any neighborhood. For y∈[−R/32,−R/64], all nonzero local input means u∈(0,R) have distance at least R/64. Since v≤2m_j, monotonicity and the integrated Gaussian tail give S_R≤C exp[−c R²/m_j] on this segment. Propagate to |y|≤R/32 using the interpolation lemma with scale R/8, initial normalized interval [−1/4,−1/8] and fixed exponent 1/7. The chain above yields
S_R(x_j)≤C exp[−c (1−r_j/R)^p R²/m_j].
Together with the Hölder lower bound this gives (R−r_j)/R ≤ C[(j+1)m_j/R²]^{1/p}.
It implies R^{2/p}−r_j^{2/p}≤ C[(j+1)m_j]^{1/p}: if r_j≥R/2 use the mean-value bound on [R/2,R]; if r_j<R/2 the displayed estimate gives R²≤C(j+1)m_j. Telescope toward 0, using ∑_{l≥j}[(l+1)m_l]^{1/p}≤C_p[(j+1)m_j]^{1/p}. Thus r_{j−1}²≤C(j+1)m_j. For every small r∈[r_j,r_{j−1}] we have U(r)≥m_j and r²≤C(j+1)m_j; the latter implies j+1≤C'log(1/r). Therefore
max_{0≤u≤r}v(x₀+u) ≥ c r²/log(1/r)
for all sufficiently small r>0. A plateau with Hölder positive-side emergence cannot have v=o(u²/log(1/u)); in particular no O(u^{2+η}), η>0, can emerge at its endpoint. Reflection gives the other orientation.

**B. Bilateral exponentially flat upper envelope.** Suppose on both sides v(x₀+u)≤C exp(−d/|u|^γ), d,γ>0. Then v vanishes on a neighborhood of x₀. Indeed, in the contrary hitting geometry R≥c(j+1)^−1/γ from the envelope at radius R. Set b_j=m_j^{1/(γ+2)}, so b_j≪R. For |y|≤b_j, local means |u|≤2b_j contribute at most C exp[−d/(2b_j)^γ] by ∫H_U=U/2. The other means have distance ≥b_j and variance ≤2m_j, so contribute ≤ C exp[−b_j²/(4m_j)]. Thus S_R(y)≤C exp[−c m_j^−s], s=γ/(γ+2)>0. On the disk centered at 0 use scale R/8 and input segment [−8b_j/R,8b_j/R] in normalized coordinates. Its interpolation exponent is ≥c/(j+1) since log(R/b_j)≤C(j+1). It yields S_R≤C exp[−c m_j^−s/(j+1)] on |y|≤R/32. The chain and hitting lower bounds now imply
1−r_j/r_{j−1} ≤ C[(j+1)² m_j^s]^{1/p}.
The right side is summable. Thus the product of the successive positive ratios r_j/r_{j−1} has a positive limit, contradicting r_j→0. No pointwise or averaged lower envelope is needed.

In both assertions the Hölder lower bound may be replaced by a polynomial-mass lower bound for the potential at each hitting point. This proof does not show that an arbitrary modulus of continuity supplies such a bound.

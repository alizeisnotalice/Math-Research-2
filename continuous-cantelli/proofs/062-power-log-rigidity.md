# Continuous finite sums of real powers and logarithms

Consider the real algebra generated by globally subanalytic real functions on the line, arbitrary fixed real powers of positive globally subanalytic functions, and logarithms of positive globally subanalytic functions. The conclusion of round 61 holds for any continuous nonnegative φ in this algebra. It suffices that φ² belong to it. All sums and products here are finite. In particular this includes continuous constructible functions in the sense of [Cluckers and Miller](https://arxiv.org/abs/0911.4373). Real powers have real exponents throughout this theorem; for context on the larger transform algebras see [Cluckers, Comte and Servi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.15958).

**Local forms and tails.** On every one-sided germ the one-variable Puiseux lemma expresses a positive generator as t^b U(t^(1/q)), t the distance to the endpoint, U analytic with positive constant term. Taking a fixed real power produces t^(bλ) times an analytic unit; taking a logarithm gives b log t plus an analytic germ. Apply this also at infinity after t=1/|x|. Thus the normalized f and v=M−f on each side are finite sums

    t^d (log(1/t))^k A(t^(1/q))

for real d, integer k≥0 and analytic A convergent near 0. A common positive integer q suffices within each comparison. Expanding A gives exponents in a finite union of translates of the discrete lattice (1/q)N. Combine coincident powers into polynomial coefficients in log(1/t). If any survive, the smallest exponent and the largest logarithm degree there give a genuine leading equivalent: tails of the finitely many analytic factors are bounded geometrically at any smaller positive radius, and fixed powers of the logarithm are dominated by any positive power gap. If all coefficients vanish, the function vanishes on a neighborhood by absolute convergence before regrouping. Boundedness at infinity yields a finite limit on each end. At a finite zero, continuity implies either an identically zero side or a leading power p>0, times a nonnegative integer power of log(1/t). There is no negative power of log in the form under consideration.

**Zero points of f and its maximum.** At a nonzero zero the local power bound with slightly reduced exponent contradicts the round 6 mass budget. At the origin any side of order p>2 is excluded by the interval argument of round 61, using any smaller exponent still greater than two. A zero interval is excluded by the nonzero-point bound. All remaining positive branches dominate a positive constant times t², including p=2 with positive logarithm degree. Thus compact tilted mass vanishes, both tail limits equal 1 and any nonconstant f has a rightmost global maximum as in round 61.

**Comparison at the rightmost maximum.** For v, mark each side as sharp if its leading p<2 or p=2 with positive logarithm degree; then v/u²→∞ on that side. The comparable case is precisely p=2 with degree zero; it is excluded by round 16. The remaining sides, including zero germs, are flat relative to u² and have an O(|u|^β) bound for some β>2. Two sharp sides violate round 17 since the positive leading powers imply a divergent local negative moment. One sharp and one flat side are excluded by round 20. Therefore both sides obey a superquadratic bound.

Use the common two-sided lattice union and subtract the polynomial P of previously matched signed-coordinate coefficients. At the first remaining exponent α, select the largest log degree k on either side. The normalized residuals on the two sides are (c_±+o(1))t^α (log(1/t))^k with at least one nonzero coefficient. Every α here exceeds two. The signed residual theorem of round 24 requires k=0, α an integer and c₋=(−1)^α c₊. Its hypothesis on P holds initially with P=0 and thereafter because the first matching coefficient is positive and has even degree ≥4, giving a nonnegative local reference P=O(u⁴). At k=0 no lower log degree at this exponent remains. Iterate successively through the locally finite exponent set. Higher log terms and fractional or irrational powers can never survive. The remaining signed integer-power series has positive radius: its coefficients grow at most exponentially by the finite analytic representations above and Cauchy estimates for each A. Absolute convergence and the matching make it an actual two-sided analytic germ for v. The chosen rightmost maximum guarantees it is nonzero on the right. Round 23 yields the contradiction.

# The time-resolvent measure including plateaus, and the first sign on every cut

Use the convention of the November 18 research guide: f=1+E a−a, h=1−f. Suppose f is bounded nonnegative, with Gaussian spatial marginal and exact exponential identity. For a graph put μ(dx,ds)=γ(dx)δ_{f(x)}(ds); the following resolvent calculation also works with a general bounded-time joint law μ satisfying the same transform. Write z=√(2q). In every time slice subtract sources at s≤t, using the original measure at t=s. The resulting signed Q_t has total variation ≤2. For t≥H its Fourier transform at ξ is exp(−tξ²/2)[1−∫exp(iξx+sξ²/2)dμ], zero by entire continuation. The measure e^(−qt)Q_t(dy)dt is absolutely integrable in variation, so integrating in t first gives density
[e^(−z|y|)−∫exp(−qs−z|y−x|)dμ]/z.
This uses the elementary Laplace transform of the heat kernel; the start point of the time integral has Lebesgue measure zero even at a platform time. For y>0 substitute ∫exp(zx−qs)dμ=1 in the first term to cancel x≤y. The difference equals (2/z)∫_{x>y}exp(−qs)sinh(z(x−y))dμ. Reflection handles y<0. Although platform slices do not alter this time integral, they must be included in Q_t: on any bounded continuous spatial test the right limit at t uses the source at t, by weak heat continuity and dominated convergence. Hence the Bernstein-polynomial argument for the time variable e^(−t) recovers positivity of *every* slice if all nonnegative smooth tests pass all lattice differences. This is the reduction of §6 of the supplied guide.

**First-order theorem.** Suppose f−1 has at most two changes of sign on the line. Then on both wings
−∂_q R(0,y) ≥ −∂_q R_const(0,y)>0 for every cut. This includes nonmonotone graphs with oscillations that do not induce another crossing. In particular, it proves the guide's first-order test for the full two-crossing class.

Indeed ∫(f−1)dγ=∫x(f−1)dγ=0, while ∫x²(f−1)dγ=Var(f)/2, by the second through fourth Gaussian moment identities. Unless f=1 almost everywhere, a constant sign is impossible, and a single crossing contradicts the first moment by subtracting the crossing abscissa from x. With two crossings c<d, the product (x−c)(x−d)(f−1) must have positive integral. Thus f−1 is nonnegative outside and nonpositive inside (allow zero intervals). Subtract the affine chord through c,d from any convex test φ. The residual is nonpositive between them and nonnegative outside, so ∫φ(f−1)dγ≥0. Taking φ(x)=(x−y)_+ or its reflection proves the comparison, since with u=x−y the derivative of the right-hand resolvent at zero is 2∫_{x>y}u(u²/3−f)dγ. Termwise differentiation is valid by bounded f and Gaussian tails. Strict positivity for f=1 is ∫₀¹t p_t(y)dt>0. Equivalently for the right-wing half of −R', its first spatial derivative equals ∫_{x>y}(u²−f)dγ and its second is f(y)g(y)−2∫_{x>y}u dγ; both signed-affine terms cancel by the two moment conditions above.

The exact first-order requirement on both wings also admits a shape-free restatement. Let U be uniform on [0,1], independent of a standard normal Z. Then it is equivalent to Law(√U Z) being below fγ in convex order. Both have mass one and zero mean. The density of the former at y≥0 is 2∫_y^∞(x−y)g(x)dx, by the time-integrated heat equation (or differentiating on y>0 and using the tail values); hence its right call at y is ∫_{x>y}(x−y)^3dγ/3. Evenness and equal means handle puts on the negative half-line. This equivalence alone does not establish convex order for unrestricted graphs.

**Nonzero q uniformly in the cut.** For the same two-crossing class the first derivative stays strictly negative at every cut at least throughout 0≤q<log(3/2)/(H+1), H=sup f. Write S_q=sinh(√(2q)u)/√(2q). The positive term of −R'/2 is ≥exp(−qH)∫u f dγ≥exp(−qH)∫u dγ on the right wing. The subtracted term is ≤∫∂_q S_q dγ by the nonnegative series coefficients. For f=1, 2∫S_q dγ=∫₀¹exp(q(1−t))p_t(y)dt, hence ∫∂_q S_q≤exp(q)∫u³dγ/3. The ratio ∫u³/∫u is ≤2: the probability on u>0 proportional to u exp(−u²/2−yu) shifts stochastically left as y increases from zero. These bounds prove the claim. No step asserts complete monotonicity itself.

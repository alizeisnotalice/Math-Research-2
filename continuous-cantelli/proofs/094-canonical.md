# Comparison at the split with the continuing Gaussian first-exit boundary

Assume a nonconstant exact normalized f is single-well with unequal wing limits H₊>H₋=1+T>1, as in the early connected theorem. Write b for the common critical radius, F_±(r)=f(±r). The Gaussian first-exit embedding supplies a continuous strictly expanding symmetric interval with radius R(t) and inverse F_*(r); it continues past the finite time T. It agrees with the actual early boundary through T by local enthalpy uniqueness. These standard existence and uniqueness statements are for the Brownian first-exit embedding, not for arbitrary exact Cantelli graphs beyond T. See [Cox and Peskir, Theorems 1, 10](https://doi.org/10.1214/14-AOP941), and the finite-time [Volterra uniqueness theorem of De Angelis and Kitapbayev](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1508.05858). The graph F_* need not be bounded. Only survival to finite times is used below. All endpoint limits are from the interior of a fixed finite time range, so the canonical boundary is locally Lipschitz by the fixed-wall reflection argument.

**Low wing.** At points arbitrarily close on the right of b, F₋(r)>F_*(r). More quantitatively, define h(u)=F_*(b+u)−T, Δ(u)=(F₋(b+u)−F_*(b+u))_+, and θ=limsup r[H₋−F₋(r)] as r→∞. Then

    sqrt(2 liminf_{u↓0} h(u) log(1/Δ(u))) + θ/2 ≤ b,                 (1)

where a zero Δ contributes +∞ in the liminf. This identifies which side of the actual Brownian continuation the low wing must visit. No analyticity condition on either crossing is needed.

**Proof.** Fix a small η>0, compare the critical survivor's real transforms for parameters −z, z→+∞, and set q=z²/2. For the canonical process the contribution after time T up to the left radius b+η is ∫₀^η exp[(b+u)z−qh(u)] g(b+u)du. The remaining sum is O(exp(−bz))+O(exp(−δq+Cz)): the opposite collar lies at radius at least b and the later canonical survivor at T+δ, δ=F_*(b+η)−T, is compactly supported. This decomposition follows by the strong Markov property or by the bounded-time exponential martingale. For the true graph, subtract the corresponding actual left collar from the same transform. All other terms are nonnegative, and the far interval of left radii [z−1,z] contributes at least c exp[q(H₋−F₋(z))], using monotonicity and the Gaussian shift. This interval is disjoint from the collar. Consequently the canonical-minus-actual positive collar difference must pay at least this amount less the two errors. If Δ≡0 near 0 this is already impossible. For any k below the liminf in (1), shrink η so Δ≤exp(−k/h). Using 1−exp(−qΔ)≤qΔ gives an upper bound C q η exp[(b+η)z−sqrt(2k)z] by AM–GM. Compare along a sequence attaining the tail limsup and let η↓0 and k increase to the liminf. When that liminf is zero, the same bound without exponential gain follows by simply bounding the positive integrand by exp[(b+η)z]. This yields (1).

**High wing.** Write R₊(T+s)=sup{r:F₊(r)≤T+s}. Both R₊ and R start at b; the absence of a critical plateau follows from the terminal survivor bound. If R₊−R has one fixed weak sign on (T,T+d₀), then, for small h>0,

    ∫₀ʰ |R₊(T+s)−R(T+s)|ds ≤ C h² exp(−2b²/h).                    (2)

For nonmonotone deviations this assertion is about the one-signed case; infinitely many crossings remain possible.

To prove (2) direct the parameter +z toward the high wing. In both true and canonical decompositions the opposite wing contributes at most C exp(−bz) up to any fixed small time collar. For the true graph its entire opposite wing satisfies this same bound since f≥T there. On the high wing restrict both spatial integrals to [b,b+η], choosing η so the time sign holds wherever the two times there lie below a fixed d₀ (and shrink η to ensure both do). The true high tail beyond b+η contributes O(exp(−δ'q+Cz))+O(exp(−δ'q)) for some δ'>0: use F₊(b+η)>T on a fixed compact segment and H₊>1+T at infinity. For the canonical graph use the stopped decomposition at F_*(b+η); its compactly supported survivor costs O(exp(−δq+Cz)). Therefore the absolute difference of the two collar integrals is O(exp(−bz)). The fixed sign of the inverse curves implies the same order for the integral of the absolute difference. Express that difference as the integral over the times between F₊ and F_* of q exp(−qs). For 0<s<d with d sufficiently small the spatial cross-section is precisely the strip between the two radii and lies in this collar. On it g is bounded below and exp(zr)≥exp(bz). Hence q∫₀ᵈ exp(−qs)|R₊(T+s)−R(T+s)|ds ≤ C exp(−2bz). Bounding exp(−qs) below on [0,h] and taking z=2b/h proves (2).

This implies liminf_{s↓0} s log(1/|R₊(T+s)−R(T+s)|)≥b². Here are details that need no prior upper Lipschitz bound on R₊. For a positive gap d_s, monotonicity of R₊ and local Lipschitz continuity of R with constant K ensure a gap ≥d_s/2 on [s,s+d_s/(2K)]. For a negative gap use [s−d_s/(2K),s]; this is positive since d_s≤R(T+s)−b≤Ks (in this sentence d_s denotes the absolute gap). The gap tends to zero by right continuity. The positive case first implies d_s=o(s), since along d_s≥c s the integral lower bound is of order d_s², impossible in (2) with h=s+d_s/(2K)=O(d_s). The same conclusion in the negative case follows using h=s. Substituting h=s(1+o(1)) into (2) and taking logarithms yields the stated exponent. No existence of a bounded nonconstant exact graph realizing these bounds is asserted.

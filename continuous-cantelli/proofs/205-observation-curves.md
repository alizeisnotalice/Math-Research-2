# Arbitrary interior observation curves, including unbounded valleys

Let I be a component of {E>f} for a continuous exact graph, σ its constant roof. Let J be any open subinterval of I, possibly unbounded, with a continuous observation height h satisfying f<h<σ throughout J and tending to σ at each finite endpoint. Then x↦q(h(x),x) takes arbitrarily many strictly alternating signs in J. In particular for each τ>0 this holds **in every component** of {x∈I: f(x)<σ−τ}, on taking h=f+τ. This extends [round 204](204-delayed-valley.md) to unbounded intervals, nonconstant delays and individual sublevel components. The curves can be chosen inside arbitrarily small neighborhoods of any active roof point with f<σ.

Start a reverse Brownian path at z∈J, at time t∈(h(z),σ], and stop it on the curve h. No path can leave J before its first hit. A hit occurs by reverse time t because h>0 and Brownian paths stay in a bounded spatial range on finite time intervals. Write K^h_{t,z} for its endpoint law. We claim
```
∫ |q(h(x),x)| K^h_{t,z}(dx)<∞,       ∫ q(h(x),x) K^h_{t,z}(dx)=q(t,z).
```
On bounded J with a uniform strict separation from the original graph this is the ordinary bounded caloric representation. For the full statement apply bounded optional stopping separately to the starting kernel p_t(z) and to each nonnegative original source at u, whose spacetime kernel is p_{t−f(u)}(z−u) at positive age and zero at nonpositive age away from its pole. For fixed u with f(u)≥σ its positive-age contribution on the observation domain is zero. For f(u)<σ, if u is in the closure of J, the continuity of h there and h(u)>f(u) (using h=σ at finite endpoints) give a neighborhood of u on which all eligible ages are bounded below by a positive constant. If u lies outside the closure it has positive spatial distance from J. In either case the kernel is bounded throughout the observation domain for this fixed source, since at positive distance from u one can maximize the Gaussian over all ages. It is caloric across zero age away from its pole. The initial kernel obeys the same argument at u=0, with height zero. Optional stopping for each bounded kernel, then Tonelli for the source integral, proves the claim. The summed source at the starting point is finite by the same local positive-age and distant-source split, now using t>f(z) and the continuity of f near z. This avoids any assumption of a uniform continuity modulus at infinity. Possible instantaneous source atoms at fixed times have no support in the observation domain, which is strictly above f.

At t=σ the right hand side vanishes throughout J. The ordered-minor proof of [round 202](202-graph-trace-alternation.md) applies with h in place of f. Its determinant expansion also holds for the possibly unbounded weights |q(h(x),x)| on sign blocks: every matrix entry is integrable by the claim, the finite product terms are integrable by independence, and the same collision argument applies (or truncate each weight and use the no-collision integral and monotone convergence). If the entire trace vanished, the claim for all starting times would make q zero in the open strip h(x)<t<σ over J. This contradicts E=σ throughout J exactly as in round 204. Thus infinitely many alternations are required. On an unbounded J the alternating points can escape to infinity; the theorem does not force a finite accumulation point there.

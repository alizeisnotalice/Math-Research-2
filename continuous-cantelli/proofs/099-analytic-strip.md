# One analytic high wing suffices in the unequal-tail single-well model

Use the normalized continuous single-well setting and early identification of rounds 88 and 94. Reflect so the right tail is higher. Suppose the high wing agrees on an outward collar of the critical radius b=R(T) with the genuine Gaussian first-exit curve. This follows if that wing is real analytic on a neighborhood of b, by round 96 and the early identification. The entire graph is then excluded, without any modulus for the low inverse. Write R for the canonical radius, A for the actual low radius (right-continuous generalized inverse of the monotone spatial wing). The absence of a plateau at critical height gives A(T+h)→b as h↓0. Disregard the at most countably many later jump times of A in almost-everywhere slice arguments. The heat sources are the pushforwards of g(x)dx under x↦(f(x),x), so a plateau deposits its full spatial density at its time.

**Canonical extension.** In z=x+R(t) coordinates construct an analytic function U(t,z) across (T,0) by the Cauchy–Kowalevski theorem for U_zz=2U_t+2R' U_z, with U(t,0)=0 and U_z(t,0)=2g(R(t))R'(t). The coefficients and data are analytic by round 96 and the surface is noncharacteristic for the second z derivative. In the original coordinates the truncation U(t,x+R(t)) times the indicator x>−R(t) has distributional heat operator equal locally to −g(R(t))R'(t) times the unit point mass at −R(t). The identical source occurs for the canonical heat remainder. Their difference is therefore caloric on a full neighborhood and zero on the exterior open side. Interior spatial analyticity of caloric distributions at each fixed time makes the difference vanish on a smaller cylinder. This identifies U with the interior canonical survivor; U also satisfies |U(t,z)|≤C|z| throughout a fixed smaller neighborhood.

**Strip sign.** Set D=q_actual−q_canonical and S=1_actual swept interval−1_canonical swept interval. The high sources cancel. For almost every short time t>T, D is spatially analytic for x>−min(A(t),R(t)): at every point there the recent low sources have positive spatial separation and the old sources have positive heat age. Continuity of A at t suffices for the recent sources even if earlier plateaux occurred. The high exterior support theorem gives D=0 on the right exterior, hence on that entire half-line. If A(t)>R(t), the actual remainder is itself spatially analytic on the local interval from −A(t) to the right of −R(t), bounded away from the positive high source locations. It equals the canonical interior on an open part of this interval and hence equals the analytic continuation U throughout the strip. Since q_canonical vanishes there, |D|≤C|A(t)−R(t)|=o(1). Thus D+gS>0. If A(t)<R(t), positivity of the individual heat kernels gives D≤the canonical new low source potential, which is O(√(t−T)) uniformly by bounded R' and the heat kernel supremum. Here S=−1, so D+gS<0. These signs use g bounded below near −b.

**Energy.** Every finite new source measure has heat potential in space-time L² on a finite slab by Minkowski, since ∫₀ʰ‖p_v‖₂²dv=O(√h). With W=∫_T^t D(s)ds we have W_t=D and W_xx=2(D+gS) distributionally, hence W(t) belongs to H² for almost every t. At each such time W is affine and square integrable on the common right half-line, where its second derivative vanishes, so W=W_x=0 there. Integrating twice on the strip yields WS≥0 by the sign just established. The heat energy identity for W_t−W_xx/2=−gS with zero initial data gives W=0. Thus q_actual=q_canonical on almost every time slice beyond T, contrary to the growing real transform on the low tail proved in round 97. The general continuous problem, and a high wing with a nonzero flat difference from the canonical germ, remain open.

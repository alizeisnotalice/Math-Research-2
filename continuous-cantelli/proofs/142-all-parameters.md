# Finite orders can miss a negative heat slice at every Laplace parameter

For every N≥1 there is an explicit one-parameter family of exact three-layer joint models for which, once ε is sufficiently small depending on N,

    (−∂q)^n R(q,y)>0     for all 0≤n≤N, all q≥0 and all y∈ℝ,

yet some time slice of the signed heat flow is strictly negative on a spatial interval. Consequently every difference of order k≤N at every integer base j≥0 is positive for every nonzero nonnegative spatial test. The smallest possible **difference order k itself**, with j and the spatial test free, diverges along the family. The models have a conditional distribution of time marks at a fixed position.

**Uniform large-q estimate.** A sufficient mark range is 0<m≤F≤H with m≤1, m>√(1−m) and H−m<(m−√(1−m))². Choose 0<a<1, 0<r<m such that 2a−a²<m, r+a>1 and (r+a−1)²>H−m. Such a choice follows by taking a just below 1−√(1−m) and r just below m. For each fixed n the signed derivative has the strict sign simultaneously on all cuts for large enough z=√(2q), uniformly in the exact joint law. It suffices to consider y≥0, then reflect.

For y≤az use the free-minus-marked derivative representation in round 132. Multiply by z^(2n+1). The free term is at least exp(−zy) since P_n(zy)≥1. In the marked term exp(−qF)≤exp(−qm) and every (z²F)^j contributes at most a fixed polynomial in z. In the convolution use g(x)≤g(y) exp(y|x−y|) and integrate the fixed polynomials P_i(z|x−y|) against exp(−(1−a)z|x−y|). Thus the ratio to the free term is at most a polynomial times exp(−qm+zy−y²/2). Its exponent is ≤−q(m−2a+a²), strictly negative uniformly.

For y≥az use the exact wing kernel exp(−qF)sinh(zu)/z, u=x−y>0. By the derivative estimates proved in round 135, when u≤rz its order n signed derivative is at least a fixed positive multiple of the kernel for large z, uniformly in F. Indeed use the product bound near u/z=0 and the uniform approximation (F−u/z)^n+O(z⁻²) away from zero. On the remainder u>rz the absolute derivative is at most the kernel times (H+u/z)^n. All these pointwise comparisons apply before conditioning on x, so they allow mixed marks.

If az≤y≤z integrate on z+1≤x≤z+2, where 1≤u≤(1−a)z+2<rz. This supplies at least c z⁻¹exp(−zy−q(H−1)). The bad region requires x−z≥(r+a−1)z. Gaussian square completion bounds its absolute integral by a polynomial times z⁻¹exp(−zy−q(m−1+(r+a−1)²)). The bad/positive ratio tends to zero uniformly. If y≥z use the round 135 packet 1/(1+y)≤u≤2/(1+y). Its lower bound is c g(y)exp(−qH)/(1+y)². The bad upper bound is a polynomial in z times g(y)exp(−q(m+r²)−rz(y−z)). Because r²>(r+a−1)² and (1+y)² exp(−rz(y−z))≤C(1+z)², the ratio again vanishes uniformly. The case n=0 also follows from the exact wing formula.

**Removing the parameter cutoff.** Fix m=19/20, H=21/20, a=9/20 and r=9/10. Even the coarser central comparison m−2a>0 works here. Take the maximum of the uniform cutoffs just proved for n≤N to get a finite Q, before choosing the model. Round 128 supplies δ(N,Q)>0 for the complementary compact range 0≤q≤Q, uniformly over all cuts and every exact law with sup F≤1+δ. Choose 0<ε<min(1/20,δ). In the explicit construction of that round the three marks are 1−ε,1,1+ε, the spatial marginal is standard Gaussian, and the time slice at 1−ε is at most −εγ/2 on [2,2+ε^(3/2)/100]. All hypotheses hold simultaneously. Integration over the translated unit cube gives every order-k difference from the derivative inequality, independently of j. Right-continuity against a test supported inside the negative interval ensures an open interval of negative test values in time; the Hausdorff criterion in round 123 ensures some negative integer difference exists. Thus increasing only the base j while keeping the number of differences bounded cannot suffice. No numerical construction or compactness passage of deterministic functions is used.

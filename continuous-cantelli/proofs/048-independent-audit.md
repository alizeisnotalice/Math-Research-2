# Independent audit of the three sextic-contact notes

Reviewed r043-sextic-sector-fold.md, r043-general-analytic-sextic-sector.md and r043-sextic-real-critical.md. I found no gap that invalidates their stated exclusions, including the subsequence lower bound for general analytic reference germs. The following checks distinguish the sectorial conclusion, the real-error theorem and the critical endpoint.

**One real outer branch.** For the negative-b branch from U=1, the barrier Re U=1/√3 maps outside |W|<ρ, because Re F₀(1/√3+it)=ρ+√3 t². Thus Re U≥1/√3 on its limiting closed-disk image; the relation |1−U²|≤2/3 implies |arg U|≤arcsin(2/3)/2. The root at −ρ on this branch is simple. On compact subsets away from the +ρ fold the branch is isolated from other roots. Local implicit branches therefore patch on a slightly enlarged compact region. The remaining fixed neighborhood of the +ρ fold lies inside the stipulated sector, so local square-root coordinates patch to both lips of a single short outward radial slit. The same argument holds for the reflected outer branch. Strictly larger open sector angle leaves room for the neighborhoods. A bounded remainder on these uniformly chosen slit disks has coefficients O(ρ_e^(−N)/N): this follows by integrating along the two cut lips, plus an indentation of radius ρ_e/N. Since C_N is of order N^(−1/2), the positive main term controls the error uniformly in b. The negative outer branch sign is unchanged at even N.

**Cutoffs and positive source mass.** First choose r sufficiently small that u²H(u) increases with |u| on each side, then choose B large so the outer spatial endpoints are away from u²H=1/B, and only then fix the inner cutoff r'≪B^(−1/2). For |b|≤B the central map in |u|≤r' has derivative bounded below, its real inverse for small w remains strictly inside, and its increment differs from the analytic reference to arbitrarily high order uniformly in b. On the two compact annuli integrate b first. The remaining Gaussian integrand is analytic there because the sectorial H is analytic away from zero; at w=0 the only cutoff crossings are the two simple nonzero roots of u²H=1/B, with all fixed endpoints outside equality. The moving endpoints are analytic. For B<|b|≤|w|^(−1/2) all real roots lie in the listed central/outer parts: shrinking r gives H≥1/2 and an equation |b||u|³/2≤|w|+|u| that localizes them; on the real rays the derivative has exactly the indicated critical behavior. The tail |b|>|w|^(−1/2) cannot be discarded pointwise for the density on a discriminant, but it can be discarded for the cumulative increment as done in the note, since its absolute mass is bounded by the Gaussian tail. Restoring physical variables divides the Nth density coefficient by N+1 for increments, without affecting supergeometric growth. In the separate real-error note the rough perturbation uses a kernel difference and therefore does not use the annular analyticity of a nonanalytic error.

**General analytic central branches.** For the analytic reference, the same compact-root and local-fold patch works with both critical values. Their radial slits lie in disjoint fixed neighborhoods, even if their arguments rotate by O(e). The two positive leading amplitudes at e=0 follow by reflection in the real case and by the rotation W=iT, U=iV in the positive-b case. The two-slit Darboux bound is uniform; it is safe even when the two amplitudes cancel, as a separate bound on the absolute error. For H₀'(0)≠0, the favorable real fold changes its modulus linearly in e, giving an exp(cN^(3/4)) gain on the unit window around b_*=√(cN/2); the imaginary pair modulus changes at worst quadratically, costing only exp(CN^(2/3)) for b≥N^(1/3). Lower b cannot compete on the Nth-root scale. If the linear coefficient vanishes, the phase is p₃e³+O(e⁵) and the log modulus is O(e²), even in e. The announced 4-divisible frequency subsequence aligns the two conjugate terms uniformly on |b−b_*|≤N^η with η<1/4. The derivative bound |d[N L(b^(−1/2))]/db|≤C on b≥b_*/2 guarantees the discarded tails under the positive magnitude measure remain Gaussian beyond this window, compared with its unit window. Thus the selected positive-b integral is positive, rather than merely bounded in magnitude. Other positive-b frequencies need not share this sign. Overall the reference bound B_N exp(−o(N)) follows along the selected subsequence. In the pure cubic-map case the central Lagrange coefficient also gives the sharper c₀ B_N.

**Real error.** Substitution t=A/u⁴ in the Hermite majorant gives Γ(3N/4+2−K/4) for an additional error prefactor |u|^K. Direct Stirling comparison gives the claimed exponential ratio [(32/27)/(1+4cd)^3]^(1/4) and the critical prefactor N^(3−K/4). For the pure case the o(|u|¹²) prefactor strictly defeats the constant-order ratio at the endpoint. There is no inference here for an arbitrary flat real remainder.

A small additional endpoint extension holds if H₀(u)=1+O(u⁴). On the negative-b unit window, W_j=±ρ+O(e⁴), so N times the modulus correction is bounded; the positive-b subsequence remains nonnegative by the preceding argument. Therefore A_N≥c B_N on that subsequence. For the critical Hermite comparison on a fixed sufficiently small core write t=|u|^(−4). Intermediate variances are cu⁶(1+O(1/t)); the Gaussian action −u²/(4s)=−t/(4c)+O(1), and the variance-power loss is at most exp(CN/t). For t≥εN this is a constant. For fixed T≤t≤εN, the Gamma density with shape 3N/4+O(1) and fixed rate 1/(4c)+d_H has lower-tail probability ≤exp(−K_ε N), where K_ε→∞ as ε↓0 by the elementary exponential moment bound. This absorbs exp(CN/T), and the fixed t<T annulus is analytic. The little-o factor can be made arbitrarily small on t≥T by increasing T, or on t≥εN as N→∞. Hence the same real endpoint exclusion holds for these references too.

**Evidence.** The independent script r043_sextic_independent_audit.py checks the pure Lagrange coefficients, signs of all first-order critical-value changes and the first imaginary phase coefficient when the linear term vanishes. Its 65-digit calculations confirm the critical power, with K=12 bounded and K=16 decaying as 1/N up to N=4096. The archive claims depend on the analytic estimates, not the finite sample.

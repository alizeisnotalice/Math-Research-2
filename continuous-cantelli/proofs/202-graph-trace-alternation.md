# Every active roof component with nonzero accumulated graph trace contains arbitrarily many spatial sign alternations

Assume the setting of [round 199](199-graph-trace.md). Write w(z)=W(f(z),z). Let I be any component of {E>f}; its roof E is a constant σ. For every y in I start reverse Brownian motion at (σ,y), stop on the graph, and denote its endpoint distribution by K_y. Then
```
K_y(I)=1,                    ∫_I w(z)K_y(dz)=W(σ,y)=0.
```
The first statement uses f=σ at every finite end of I; at positive reverse time no live path can reach such an endpoint. The second uses caloric optional stopping and local extinction. In particular it holds at σ=H. We show that either w vanishes identically on I, or for every n there are ordered points in I at which w alternates strictly in sign n times. Only the second option is possible if I contains any point with f<1, since formula (3) of round 199 makes w strictly positive there.

**Ordered hitting measures.** Fix y₁<⋯<y_m in I and ordered disjoint Borel intervals B₁,…,B_m in I. For nonnegative bounded endpoint weights b_j supported in B_j set A_ij=∫b_j dK_{y_i}. The determinant is nonnegative. It is strictly positive if for each j some open interval in B_j has b_j bounded below by a positive constant.

To prove this, take independent reverse Brownian paths from the m starting points and freeze each spatial path after its graph hit. Expand the determinant over all assignments of target intervals. Contributions from paths colliding before the final reverse time σ cancel in pairs. Indeed at their earliest meeting choose the lexicographically first meeting pair. For configurations contributing a nonzero product the meeting paths must both be live up to this meeting: if one had stopped strictly earlier at x, then the new time height at x would be strictly less than f(x), so the other path would already have hit the graph before arriving there. Two paths with the same stopping endpoint cannot occupy distinct target intervals. Thus on relevant collisions both paths are live and their Brownian futures can be exchanged. More formally, use their independent underlying Brownian paths continued past all stops, reflect their future increments by transposition at this joint stopping time, and then apply the deterministic hitting rule afresh. The strong Markov property preserves probability. The first-collision pair and time stay fixed, the assignment is transposed, and its product of endpoint weights stays fixed. On no-collision configurations, order is preserved so only the identity assignment can contribute.

For strict positivity take small intervals interior to the indicated positive windows. Over a short initial time move each start linearly toward its distinct ordered target center. Synchronous interpolation preserves a positive minimum separation. A compact interval inside I contains all these trajectories and has supremum of f strictly below σ. Thus the travel time and disjoint tube radii can be chosen small enough to keep all paths above the graph until their arrivals. Require the paths then to stay within their respective target windows until at least their graph hits, which must happen by reverse time σ. Each Brownian path has strictly positive probability to stay in its prescribed open tube about this continuous piecewise linear path; alternatively subdivide time, use the strictly positive Gaussian transition densities and the positive Brownian bridge confinement probabilities. Independence yields a strictly positive no-collision contribution.

**Sign consequence.** A nonzero continuous real function on an interval with only finitely many strict sign alternations has a finite ordered partition of its nonzero set into nonempty single-sign blocks, alternating in sign. To construct it, order the nonzero points and group two points of the same sign if all nonzero points between them have that sign. Distinct consecutive groups alternate, and arbitrarily many groups would give arbitrarily long alternating sequences (equivalently assign to each point the maximum alternation length of a chain ending there). The groups admit disjoint ordered interval hulls, assigning any common zero boundary arbitrarily. Every block has an open subinterval on which |w| is bounded below. Use one arbitrary starting point per block, in increasing order, and choose b_j=|w| restricted to the jth block. If e_j is its sign then Ae=0 by the reverse hitting identity at all starts, whereas strict positivity of det A gives a contradiction. This proves the assertion.

Thus one strictly positive early portion forces arbitrarily many negative AND positive graph intervals in its own roof component. Each negative interval lies in {1<f<σ}. On a bounded component there is an accumulation point of sign changes in its closure. This argument permits accumulation at an endpoint and amplitudes tending to zero; it gives no common positive lower bound for the infinitely many sign amplitudes. The oscillating function is the explicit graph potential w in round 199, a functional of the whole continuous graph.

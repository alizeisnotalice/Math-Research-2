# An interval mass expansion at a superquadratic variance contact

Center coordinates at a finite zero of the bounded continuous nonnegative reduced variance v. Let g(u)=p₁(a+u). Assume the exact Gaussian output law. Let w≥0 be a smooth bounded reference, with w=O(|u|^q), and assume v=O(|u|^q), |v−w|=O(|u|^α) near zero, where q>2 and α>2. The zero reference is allowed. Extend a local smooth reference by a cutoff. Write B=α+q/2. There is a single polynomial Q, containing only integer degrees j with α+1≤j<B, such that, for signed h of either sign,

    ∫₀ʰ g(u)(v(u)−w(u))du = Q(h) + O(|h|^B).

All constants can depend on the graph. The true variance needs no derivatives. This is a quantitative necessary condition on the actual, unsmoothed interval masses.

**Heat primitive.** Put K_b(x)=∫₀ᵇ p_s(x)ds with K₀=0 and U_v(y)=∫g(u)K_{v(u)}(y−u)du, where the subscript v(u) denotes the variance at u. Distributionally U_v''=2(output density minus g), by the heat equation, including the identity contribution at variance zero. Exactness makes U_v real analytic near zero. The reference U_w has Peano expansions of every finite order there: its output density has the smooth-reference coefficient expansion of §I of the round 51 proof for a finite even leading order ≥4. The same follows directly even at flat zeros: write the reference output as the pushforward under u↦u+Z√w(u) when a smooth signed square root exists. For general smooth nonnegative references, the following argument avoids this regularity issue altogether.

**Smooth reference via weak heat expansion.** At a nonflat zero the leading degree is even and the positive smooth factor has a smooth square root on a neighborhood. The local coefficient expansion of round 51 §I applies also for the quartic case (m=2). A flat zero may instead be compared directly with w=0 by the near/far estimates below, using arbitrarily large finite exponents for the near region. Contributions separated from the center have finite Taylor expansions with a remainder of every specified order. Thus for a nonflat reference the output has a full density expansion in the sense of measures (the density representative off zero has these asymptotics); integrating its second primitive twice and then once more gives the needed Peano coefficients. For a flat reference the near/far estimates directly give them for the cumulative heat potential. No polynomial replacement is needed in either case.

**Comparison on an interval.** Use first h>0, I=[0,h]. In ∫_I(U_v−U_w), the contribution from |u|≤2h is exactly the integral of g(u) times the signed time integral from w(u) to v(u) of P(u+√s Z∈I). Replacing this probability by 1_I(u) costs O(h^B). Indeed the pointwise discrepancy away from endpoints is at most 2 exp[−dist(u,{0,h})²/(2C h^q)], uniformly over those times. Integrate in u and bound the length of the time interval by C h^α. Reflection and orientation give the same estimate for h<0.

For |u|>2|h| expand the kernel difference at y=0 before integrating in y. In the local region its jth derivative at |y|≤|h| is bounded by C_j |u|^(−j−1) exp(−c/|u|^(q−2)) times the time interval length. This follows by absorbing powers of |u|/√s in the Gaussian. Away from zero use the same separation without the exponential, with bounded times and integrable source g. Thus Taylor remainders of any fixed order integrate uniformly; each coefficient integral extends to all u at a cost smaller than any fixed power of h. For each required order this yields one polynomial in signed h, with the same coefficients on both sides. Combined with the expansions of the two full potentials this gives a polynomial modulo O(|h|^B). Since the interval mass is O(|h|^(α+1)), all lower coefficients vanish. Degrees at or above B can be discarded. For the flat smooth-reference argument just apply this near/far calculation to U_w itself with lower time endpoint zero; its near contribution is smaller than any fixed power, independently of any prior expansion for U_w.

**Shrinking windows.** For intervals J⊂[−2r,2r] of length ℓ, subtracting the endpoint expansions gives a uniform O(r^B) discrepancy between ∫_J g(v−w) and ∫_J Q'. After division by ℓ r^α it tends to zero whenever ℓ/r^(q/2)→∞. If α is noninteger the normalized average tends uniformly to zero on these windows. For integer α it reads the single signed monomial coefficient of Q' of degree α up to o(1) there (higher degrees are o(r^α)). For w=0 and v=O(|u|^α) take q=α. In particular, if α is not an even integer, nonnegativity forces any term of degree α in Q' to vanish, by using fixed relative intervals on both sides. One thereby rules out a nonzero-height nonnegative mass fraction on any sequence of intervals above the intrinsic noise width. This says nothing about intervals comparable to, or shorter than, that width.

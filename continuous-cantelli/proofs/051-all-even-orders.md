# Every controlled even-order contact is incompatible with an exact Gaussian output

Fix an integer m≥3, d=m−1, s=1/(2d), c>0, and a point x₀. Suppose a bounded continuous v≥0 has, from both sides, the complete value expansion of cu^{2m}H(u)^2 at x=x₀+u, where H is smooth near zero and H(0)=1. Precisely, their difference is O_J(|u|^J) for every finite J. Let H_j=H^(j)(0)/j!. No regularity of the actual remainder is required. Any one of the following implies that the Gaussian-source mixture cannot have an exact Gaussian law.

* H has a convergent Taylor series (H itself may differ from it).
* |H_j|≤C^{j+1}(j!)^b for fixed b<s, encompassing divergent formal series.
* At the critical pointwise Gevrey exponent, τ=limsup (|H_j|/(j!)^s)^{1/j} satisfies τ≤(ec)^s d√λ_m/m, where λ_m=d/(2m−1)·[4m/(2m−1)]^{m/d}. In fact the proof below permits a strictly larger positive type as indicated at the end.

The parameter s bounds the growth of Taylor coefficients, so the customary derivative Gevrey order is 1+s. These hypotheses do not purport to classify arbitrary divergent smooth jets. The analytic-reference special case covers every even order ≥6 and arbitrary real flat remainders. Quadratic and quartic cases are already covered in earlier rounds in their stated scopes.

**I. Real coefficient identity.** Fix a small input core |u|<r, set g(u)=p₁(x₀+u) and V=cu^{2m}H². Under exactness the core output admits a real analytic density A(w) at w=0, by subtracting the exterior mixture with mean separated by a fixed distance (uniformly bounded holomorphic Gaussian kernels; exterior zero-variance mass stays outside). Its jth Taylor coefficient equals the integral of the off-diagonal Gaussian coefficients of v, plus
[w^j] ∑_{0≤k≤j/(2d)} (2^k k!)^{-1} ∂_w^{2k}[g(w)V(w)^k].
For the smooth reference this follows by splitting at |u|=8|w|. On the outer part Cauchy bounds on the radius |u|/4 disk dominate every fixed Taylor remainder by a power of |u| times exp(−C/|u|^{2d}). On the inner part away from |u−w|<|w|/2, the unexpanded density is smaller than every fixed power of |w| by the same Gaussian separation. On |u−w|<|w|/2 substitute u=w+|w|^m t; the physical integrand becomes g(u)p_{V(u)/w^{2m}}(t)dt. Taylor-expand in u at w to fixed order L, then integrate t: on the retained segment the variance parameter is bounded above and below and its lth u derivative is O_l(|w|^{−l}). Hence the integrated remainder is O_L(|w|^{d(L+1)}). Gaussian moments times the explicit displacement powers give exactly the derivatives displayed, whose kth term starts at degree 2dk. The missing fixed polynomial tails are rapidly decaying. For the real graph, the inner unexpanded density differs from the smooth reference by a bound C|v−V||u|^{−3m}, by differentiating once in the variance. This is integrable and negligible to any desired finite order there. The outer part again uses ordinary Cauchy bounds, no spatial derivatives of v. This proves the identity by uniqueness of real asymptotic coefficients, allowing equality to the analytic density only almost everywhere.

**II. Weight making every off-diagonal term nonnegative.** For large even N set a=(cN)^−s and consider P_N=[W^N]e^{λNW²/2}A(aW). Cauchy on |W|=1/√λ gives |P_N|≤B e^{Nf₀}, f₀=(1+logλ)/2. For one off-diagonal input z=u/a, set S=Nv(x₀+az)/a²=z^{2m}h(az), where h(u)=v(x₀+u)/(cu^{2m})→1. Its weighted coefficient has the sign of
[W^N]exp[N zW/S+(N/2)(λ−1/S)W²].
When λS≥1 every even coefficient is nonnegative. Otherwise its sign is that of He_N(√N z/√[S(1−λS)]). The real symmetric Jacobi matrix of He_N has off-diagonals √j for j<N, so its largest absolute eigenvalue is <2√N. The monic even polynomial is positive outside that interval. For 0<S<1/λ,
S(1−λS)/z²=h(az)^{1/m} S^{d/m}(1−λS)
≤h(az)^{1/m} m/(2m−1) ·[d/((2m−1)λ)]^{d/m}.
This is <1/4 uniformly after shrinking the fixed core when λ>λ_m. The off-diagonal part of P_N is therefore nonnegative for every even N.

**III. The Wick groups with both positive and negative real saddles.** Put ν=k/N. By the identity in I, the kth diagonal group, including its weight, is
(2k−1)!! N^{−k} [W^N T^{2k}] e^{λNW²/2}(W+T)^{2mk}g(a(W+T))H_N(a(W+T))^{2k},             (1)
where 0≤k≤N/(2d) and H_N is the degree-N Taylor polynomial of H. Replacing H by H_N changes no coefficient requested. In (1), replacing the last two factors by 1 gives exactly
D_{N,k}=binom(2mk,2k)(2k−1)!!N^{−k}(λN/2)^{(N−2dk)/2}/[((N−2dk)/2)!].
Write Rmax=m/(d√λ). On |Z|≤Rmax+ε for some fixed ε>0, H_N(aZ)=1+O(a) uniformly, assuming τ<(ec)^s/Rmax. Indeed choose τ'>τ and this radius so that τ' a(Rmax+ε)(N/e)^s<1−η. From Stirling, (j!)^s≤C j^{3s/2}(j/e)^{s(j−1)}; summing j≥1 against (τ' a|Z|)^j gives O(a) after extracting the first power and bounding j≤N with a geometric majorant. The constant multiplier in the definition of τ has no effect. Cauchy yields analogous fixed-order derivatives on any slightly smaller radius. In the subcritical growth case τ=0. For all k, (1) is bounded in absolute value by C exp(CNa)D_{N,k}: group by the even power 2l from gH_N^{2k}; the ratio binom(2mk+2l,2k)/binom(2mk,2k)≤(m/d)^{2l}, by cancellation of falling factorials (at k=0 both binomials are 1). The ratio from the Gaussian multiplier is ≤λ^−l. Evaluate the absolute Taylor majorants at Rmax a.

Uniform Stirling with zero-factorial convention gives log D_{N,k}/N=f(ν)+O(log N/N), where
f(ν)=ν[2m log m−2d log d+log(2ν)−1]+(1/2−dν)[1+log(λ/(1−2dν))].
Endpoint values are understood continuously. For any compact I⊂(0,1/(2d)), the actual groups (1) are positive, and their logarithm divided by N is f(ν)+o(1), uniformly for ν∈I. To prove this, use the Cauchy torus of radii R=√[(1−2dν)/λ], U=R/d. Ignoring the small H_N phase perturbation, the modulus normalized at real positive radii attains its maximum only with both arguments 0 or both π. In real log radii x,y the phase is Φ₀=λe^{2x}/2+2mν log(e^x+e^y)−x−2νy. Its gradient vanishes at (log R,log U), its Hessian is diag(2λR²,0)+2mν RU/(R+U)² [[1,−1],[−1,1]], uniformly positive on I.

For each sign σ=±1 add 2ν log H_N(σ a(e^x+e^y)) to Φ₀; it is real at real radii and all fixed derivatives are O(a) perturbations on common neighborhoods. Uniformly in ν, the implicit function theorem (or uniform contraction from the bounded inverse reference Hessian) gives real stationary radii O(a) from the original. On two disjoint angular patches at (0,0) and (π,π) smoothly deform both log radii separately to the respective new real stationary radii, taking these new radii constant in a small subpatch and the original radii outside. This is a legal closed-torus deformation by Stokes, since the original coefficient integrand as a holomorphic two-form has no pole at W+T=0 and W,T stay nonzero. All transition areas and off-patch regions preserve a fixed normalized exponential gap by compactness (at W+T=0 use the product modulus directly). In the constant-radius patches expansion in angles gives minus the positive radius Hessian as the real angular Hessian. Gaussian domination and bounded third derivatives give at each saddle its positive leading contribution
exp[NΦ_σ]g(σa(e^x+e^y))/(2πN√det Hess Φ_σ) ·(1+o(1)).
All sign factors at the negative saddle equal +1 because N,2k and 2mk are even. The error is relative to each saddle separately and uniform. The remaining contour is exponentially smaller. Since Φ_σ=Φ₀+O(a), comparison to the identical unperturbed saddle calculation (or D_{N,k}) proves the claimed positivity and exponential rate.

**IV. Existence of a strict Wick exponential gain in every order.** We show that at λ=λ_m, one interior test fraction ν₀=1/(md) has f(ν₀)>f(0)=f₀. Direct substitution gives f(ν₀)−f₀=A(1/m)/d, with
A(x)=−(3−2x)log(1−x)+(2−x)log(1−x/2)−1−[(1−x)(1−2x)/(2x)]log(1−2x).
In the expansion of A the constant and linear terms vanish; a₂=1/12, a₃=1/24. For j≥4 its jth coefficient is
a_j=[(j−3)(1−2^{j−1}/(j+1))+2^{1−j}]/[j(j−1)].
It is negative and |a_j|≤2^{j−1}/[j(j−1)]. For 0<x≤1/4 the absolute tail divided by x² is ≤2x²/[3(1−2x)]≤1/12, giving A(x)>0. For m=3, 3A(1/3)=log(84375/4096)−3>0; use e<49/18 from the factorial series and (49/18)^3<84375/4096. Thus g_m=A(1/m)/d>0 for all m≥3. Choose λ=λ_m exp(mg_m/2): f(ν₀)−f₀=g_m/2>0 while the off-diagonal sign is strict and the critical type bound in the theorem has room.

Let f* be the global maximum. It strictly exceeds f(0) by the previous paragraph. At the other endpoint f'(ν)→−∞ from the left, so f* also strictly exceeds that endpoint. Choose small fixed endpoint neighborhoods where f≤f*−3δ and so small that at least one maximizing point remains in a compact interior interval. Every endpoint group contributes in absolute value ≤exp[N(f*−2δ)] for large N by III. The middle groups are individually positive, and one k nearest a maximizing νN contributes ≥exp[N(f*−δ)] for all sufficiently large even N (indeed exp[N(f*+o(1))]). Choose δ also below (f*−f₀)/4. Summing the groups and adding the nonnegative off-diagonal part contradicts the analytic Cauchy upper bound.

More precisely any τ<(ec)^s (d/m)√λ_m exp(mg_m/2) is admissible by choosing λ in (λ_m, λ_m exp(mg_m)) close enough to its upper endpoint; f(ν₀)>f₀ still holds. This is a sufficient critical type, not claimed optimal.

The same proof also works at m=2 if one uses ν₀=2/5 instead of 1/(md), which is then an endpoint. At λ₂=64/27 direct substitution gives 10(f(2/5)−f₀)=log(3^{12}/125)−8>0 (e<11/4 and (11/4)^8<3^{12}/125). Take any slightly larger λ preserving this gain. Sections I–III and the global-maximum-endpoint argument are unchanged; this gives a unified proof but does not replace the sharper independent quartic thresholds.

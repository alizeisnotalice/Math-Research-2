# Unequal tails, one analytic critical crossing and one regular crossing

Suppose the early R084 hypotheses and Brownian identification hold to T>0, the left far tail has limit 1+T, and the right tail has liminf strictly larger. The early symmetry theorem gives limiting interval [−R,R], R>0. Assume {f≤T}=[−R,R], ruling out simultaneous exterior contacts at T. Suppose the actual graph is real analytic across the left endpoint −R, and on a short outward collar at R has a strictly increasing continuous inverse r(t) locally Hölder with exponent β>1/2 **uniformly through T from above**, r(T)=R. For example C¹ regularity with positive continuous derivative near R suffices. No far-tail rate is required. Then the graph cannot be exact.

Use the entire heat function U built in r088-one-edge-entire.md from the left branch ℓ(t) and its positive weight w(t)=−g(ℓ(t))ℓ′(t) near T. The true initial surviving density equals U(T,y) on (−R,R), zero outside; it is positive throughout this open interval. Positivity follows, for example, by surviving a short initial slab with positive probability and using the positive transition density inside smaller static intervals during subsequent subslabs of the expanding domain. Since the initial survivor is even, U(T,y) is even on all of ℂ by spatial analytic continuation. All its time derivatives at T are even by U_t=U_yy/2, and hence the analyticity in t gives evenness also for t in a fixed small neighborhood of T. Therefore the positive synthetic right boundary is r₀(t)=−ℓ(t). The heat solution trimmed to (ℓ(t),r₀(t)) has precisely the true left graph source together with its reflected copy at r₀(t), of weight w(t)=g(r₀(t))r₀′(t). In fact the value is zero at both edges and the two spatial derivative jumps give these weights. The trimmed density, denoted Q⁰_t, is nonnegative in this short slab: it solves the heat equation inside its smooth moving interval with nonnegative initial value and zero lateral values, so the maximum principle applies.

Choose η>0 small. By compactness and the strict far-tail gaps above T, all newly activated sources through T+η are in the two prescribed collars. The true Q_t therefore has the same left source as Q⁰_t. Its right-only sources run on r(t), carrying the spatial Gaussian image measure. This measure assigns any time interval of length h mass ≤C h^β in the chosen slab. Consequently the true single-layer heat term is continuous up to this moving edge and through the initial time T: uniformly in y the contribution of ages ≤h is ≤C h^{β−1/2} by a dyadic sum; positive-age contributions are jointly continuous. The smooth left source and the continuous initial density cause no extra singularity. For every t≤T+η, Q_t is zero on y>r(t), by the one-sided R083 resolvent support (choose η below the gap of the high right tail above 1+T). Its trace on r(t) is therefore zero.

Take y₀<R inside and close to R. Since Q_T(y₀)>0, continuity gives Q_t(y₀)>0 after shrinking η. The true Q_t is caloric between y₀ and r(t), with nonnegative continuous initial and lateral data. It follows by the maximum principle on the continuous moving interval that Q_t(y)≥0 for y₀≤y≤r(t). We need this sign only near the high-side boundary; no positivity is assumed on the low exterior.

Write V(t)=∫_T^tQ_s ds and V₀(t)=∫_T^tQ⁰_s ds. Let W=V−V₀. Their initial heat laws and their left sinks are equal. Hence W is the zero-initial-data mild heat solution with bounded square-integrable forcing
−g(y)[1_{R<y<r(t)}−1_{R<y<r₀(t)}].
Values at the two moving endpoints are immaterial. In the strip of disagreement the potential of the solution with the smaller radius is zero (its boundary has been nondecreasing and its exterior empty), and the other potential is nonnegative by the two preceding comparisons. Therefore W times the displayed forcing is nonpositive everywhere. The parabolic L² energy identity gives W=0. It follows that Q_t=Q⁰_t in the slab, first for almost every t and then by weak continuity. In particular the true Q_t has compact support. But at t=T+η/2 its positive future negative-real transform grows at least as exp(cλ²) from the lower left tail, contradicting compact support, exactly as in r088-critical-level-continuation.md.

Relative to the two-analytic-crossing result this trades full local analyticity on the high side for the specified inverse modulus and the isolated critical sublevel. It also requires that the true low-side graph follow its actual analytic continuation, since its sources are cancelled exactly in the energy identity.

**A continuous strictly increasing outward high wing.** The same conclusion holds with only a continuous strictly increasing outward high branch (in place of the Hölder hypothesis on its inverse). Here are details of the extra argument for positivity in the high-side collar. For the small slab starting at T let r(s) be that continuous increasing right boundary. The known left analytic branch is separated from it. Take Brownian motion freely evolving from the positive bounded initial density Q_T, killed on first hitting r(s). Separately take the positive injections w(s)δ_{ℓ(s)}ds and similarly kill their propagated masses on first hitting r. Subtract the second surviving heat field from the first, obtaining a signed field q^K. The difference η of their two finite positive hitting measures is carried by (s,r(s)). Each hitting law has no time atom: at a fixed time hitting requires the unconstrained Brownian position to equal one fixed point, hence has probability zero, and the injected paths start at positive separation. The initial measure gives the edge itself zero mass. The true upper sources likewise define a finite nonatomic measure μ along the same graph since there is no plateau.

The true and killed exterior both vanish. Consequently, at any point y>r(t), their past Gaussian potentials from η and μ are equal, by subtracting the same free initial and left-injection fields. Taking y↓r(t) gives equality of their past traces at the graph. In detail each of the two positive hitting laws has a uniformly bounded trace: its exterior past potential equals its own free input density there, which is bounded by ∥Q_T∥∞ in the first case and by a constant in the second, as the left injections stay a fixed distance away. Use monotone convergence because every past upper-source location is ≤r(t). For the positive true μ the same bound follows from the true exterior expression, a difference of these two free inputs. Thus the total variation of σ=μ−η is dominated by a finite positive measure with bounded traces and finite bare spacetime heat energy. Time symmetry and equality of past traces imply that σ itself has zero bare energy with kernel p_{|t−s|}(r(t)−r(s)).

To use R083 Section 5's strict energy uniqueness, its same-wing steepness hypothesis must be checked; finite bare energy alone was not used there to pass to the spatial Fourier representation. At every positive time t strictly after T in this short slab, R083 Section 3's one-sided steepness argument applies at r(t): all past means are to its left, there is right-exterior vanishing at earlier times from the high-tail resolvent, and the crossing is monotone on both sides of r(t). The free minus true past trace at r(t) is zero by the exterior limit. A hypothetical bounded (time difference)/(space difference)² along a local sequence would yield the strictly negative short-time resolvent contradiction there, exactly as in R083. Hence that ratio tends to infinity. The two endpoints in time have zero mass and can be omitted. The good-set cutoff and Gaussian mollification proof in R083 Section 5, now for only one graph and bounded total energy, gives σ=0. Thus Q=q^K.

For clarity the killed reference has zero joint upper-boundary trace even with this rough r. For a path started at s<t, killing at the expanding r during [s,t] leaves a transition density dominated by the static half-line kernel with boundary r(t), by path inclusion. For the initial input the resulting bound is ≤∥Q_T∥∞[2Φ((r(t)−y)/√(t−T))−1], vanishing at the moving boundary uniformly away from time T. For the left-source contribution split the ages at a small h: ages ≥h obey the same static Dirichlet trace bound with the explicit pair of Gaussian kernels, while the ages <h are exponentially small due to the fixed separation. At time T itself the initial free heat convolution tends continuously to zero near R because Q_T vanishes at R; the separated recent term is negligible. Therefore q^K has continuous zero upper data, and is continuous and caloric in the near-right open collar by its distributional heat equation and local separation of the sources. The inner trace remains positive for a short time just as before. The maximum principle proves positivity on the strip needed for the energy comparison, completing the extension.

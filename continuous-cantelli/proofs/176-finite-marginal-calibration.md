# Simultaneous finite Gaussian marginal calibration and finite transforms with a continuous nonstopping graph

Fix N≥2, distinct nonzero real parameters z₁,…,z_m, and ε>0. There exist a centered variance-one probability ρ with density, dTV(ρ,γ)<ε, and a bounded smooth clock F with 1/2≤F≤3/2, F(0)−F(−1)=1/2, having all these properties:

* ∫x^j dρ=∫x^j dγ for j≤N.
* ∫exp(zx−z²F(x)/2)dρ=1 for every complex z. Its graph law cannot be realized by a bounded randomized Brownian stop.
* With the same spatial coefficient and the natural variance normalization under γ, the clock F_γ=F+1−∫F dγ satisfies ∫exp(z_i x−z_i² F_γ(x)/2)dγ=1 for every prescribed i. Alternatively the construction can impose these m tests on the raw F instead. No assertion that both choices are imposed simultaneously is required.

For instance with φ(x)=√(2−F(x)), input X∼ρ and independent Z∼γ give X+φ(X)Z exactly N(0,2), by the transform and uniqueness. Under X∼γ the normalized finite tests compare its transform to N(0,1+∫φ²dγ). This version keeps the spatial coefficient identical across the two input laws. The subscript on F_γ accounts for the variance change. Distinct N,ε,parameter sets may require different graphs; fixed anchors have no common local modulus in the construction.

**Calibration at the constant clock.** Start from F_a=1+Σ a_l h_l where the h_l are smooth compact unimodal bumps with disjoint supports away from −1,0,2,4. Use N−1+m columns. Let τ_a be first contact with F_a, and use outputs E[B_{τ_a}^j]−E[B_1^j] for j=2,…,N, and the m Gaussian-input transform discrepancies for F_{a,γ} above. At a=0 all vanish. The first rows of the derivative are
$$
 \tfrac12\int (x^j)''h_l(x)\,d\gamma .
$$
Indeed |τ_a−1|≤C|a| and τ_a−1=Σa_l h_l(B_{τ_a}). Apply the Brownian generator formula to the two bounded stopping times τ_a and 1, subtract expectations, and replace the integrand in the oriented time integral by its value at B₁. The error is o(|a|) by path continuity and integrable Brownian supremum moments; replacing h_l(B_{τ_a}) by h_l(B₁) costs o(|a|) as well. The Gaussian-input rows are −z_i²/2 times the integral of h_l(x)[exp(z_i x−z_i²/2)−1]dγ. For raw-clock tests omit the minus one in this bracket. These polynomials of degrees 0,…,N−2 and the distinct nonconstant exponentials (possibly minus constants) are linearly independent: differentiate N−1 times and use the Vandermonde identity for distinct exponents. Their value vectors span the row dimension on the complement of the four forbidden points. Choose that many distinct evaluation points with nonsingular matrix, then sufficiently narrow positive bumps there. By continuity the integrated matrix remains nonsingular after normalizing each column by ∫h_l dγ. The combined support widths may be arbitrarily small. At these fixed supports the map has invertible linear part and o(|a|) remainder, hence nonzero degree on all sufficiently small balls about zero.

**Adding tracks.** Put three spikes above time 1 centered at 0,2,4. Choose a smooth function b supported on [−1,1], positive inside, with maximum A=1/2 at zero, strictly monotone on each open side with nonzero derivative there, and exactly affine on an interior subinterval of its rising side. Such a smooth profile is obtained by integrating a positive smooth slope constant on a subinterval and flat at both endpoints of [−1,0], then reflecting and normalizing. Set the three profiles equal to b((x−2j)/v), where v↓0. Their supports avoid all calibrating bumps. Denote the new clock by F_{v,a}. It equals F_a off these supports. Since it only increases the clock, the first hits can differ only on paths where τ_a=1 and B₁ belongs to a base interval; on such paths the new terminal location lies in the closure of that same interval. Hence all polynomial rows change by O_N(v) uniformly in the calibration ball. For the Gaussian rows the two integrands differ only locally for the raw clock and the normalization constants change by O(v); the Gaussian rows change uniformly by O(v) in both versions. The maps are continuous in a. Indeed for uniform clock convergence the path has a positive gap on every compact time interval before first contact and strictly crosses immediately after contact at times when Brownian motion returns to that same position (for each fixed limiting clock almost surely). Thus the hitting times converge almost surely, with moment domination. Nonzero degree persists for v sufficiently small and yields a root a. The ball's radius can be made arbitrarily small before selecting v.

**Total variation and absolute continuity.** In the coupling with B₁, the raw calibrated first hit differs from B₁ only if a Brownian path visits a calibration support during [1−r,1+r], where r bounds |F_a−1|, or if B₁ lies in a spike not previously stopped. By conditioning at 1−r and reflection, the first event has probability ≤C(total support width + number of supports times √r) for r<1/2. The spike contribution is O(v). Choose successively the widths, r and v so this sum is <ε/2. The finitely many flat time levels give absolutely continuous spatial exit submeasures because B_t has a density. On each open nonstationary side of a bump, cover by countably many compact inverse-time segments on which the boundary is C¹ with bounded speed. First hits there have a time density: from an earlier deterministic time in this side's range, ignore the other barriers and use finite-horizon absolute continuity of Brownian motion with deterministic bounded drift to driftless Brownian motion in the moving frame, whose first hit of zero has a density away from the start time. Restriction to actually surviving paths is dominated by this unrestricted hitting measure. Spatial absolute continuity follows by the smooth invertible coordinate on the side. The finitely many endpoints and extrema have zero mass since B_t at each specified positive time has no atoms.

**Perturbation preserving all chosen moments.** Inside the common affine time window the three rising sides are parallel with equally spaced intercepts. Their first-hit law on a compact subwindow dominates a positive multiple of time Lebesgue measure on each track. To see this despite the calibrations, from time 1−r (before any earlier stop except a null boundary time for the selected paths) restrict paths to a fixed small buffer around the relevant spike until time 1, clear of calibration supports. Its killed Brownian density is strictly positive in the interior. From 1 to the beginning of an affine subwindow restrict further to a small closed interval inside the continuing spike core at that later time. This provides positive initial mass for a strip abutting the left moving edge: choose the preceding fixed interval strictly inside the moving strip at this initial time. On a sufficiently short affine time interval, that strip of fixed width in moving coordinates stays inside the actual continuing interval. Its Dirichlet heat kernel with constant drift has strictly positive exit flux at its left edge for every positive duration, uniformly on compact subwindows (equivalently use the reflection kernel or boundary point lemma). Comparison yields the asserted domination.

In round 173's smooth kernel take h=D^{N+1}ψ for a nonzero compact Gevrey bump ψ on that last subwindow. Every track density is the (N+1)st derivative of a compact smooth function, by convergence in each derivative order. Thus all its first N spatial moments vanish separately on each affine track by integration by parts. A sufficiently small nonzero multiple changes the calibrated first-hit measure into a different probability ρ, maintaining positivity, absolute continuity and dTV<ε. The complete transform remains exactly one, the input moments are unchanged, and the clock's Gaussian-input tests are unchanged. Only the first-hit law is stoppable on this continuous graph by round 174. The altered law therefore has no stopping realization. As v→0, the central spike has fixed height on shrinking width, which identifies the missing compactness explicitly.

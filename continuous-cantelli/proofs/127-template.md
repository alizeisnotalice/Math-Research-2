# Quantitative cost of approximating a separated measurable template at a gap

This is a statement about convergence to the actual template in space, distinct from convergence of the transform alone. Suppose a bounded measurable graph F equals s on a set K and is at least s+Δ on the gap (c,c+ℓ). Suppose for 0<r≤r₀ the interval [c−r,c] contains K of Lebesgue measure at least κr, κ>0. On the compact neighborhood fix the minimum Gaussian density g_*>0. For any continuous candidate v of modulus ω there,

    ∫ |v−F| dγ ≥ (Δ/4)g_* min(κ,1)r,

whenever r≤min(r₀,ℓ) and ω(2r)≤Δ/2. Otherwise the strict reverse bound would yield one point on K to the left with |v−s|<Δ/4 and one point in the gap to the right with v>s+3Δ/4, contradicting the modulus. In particular an L¹ error ε tending to zero forces some oscillation of size ≥Δ/2 at distances O(ε) (choose r slightly larger than 4ε/(Δg_*min(κ,1))). A Lipschitz family approximating this template requires its local Lipschitz constants to be at least c'/ε at small ε.

The one-sided density condition is explicit for the usual fat middle Cantor template. Start with a closed interval; at stage n remove its middle relative fraction η_n=2^(−n−2) in each surviving component. At an endpoint of the first central gap the nested adjacent intervals have length ratios in [3/8,1/2], and each retains at least ∏(1−η_n)>0 of its length in the limit. For a given r take the first adjacent descendant of length ≤r. Its length is at least 3r/8, giving a fixed κ. The early-versus-late time separation is the feature used in the measurable construction of Kleptsyn and Kurtzmann, Section 2 (https://arxiv.org/pdf/1202.2250). The elementary bound above requires only the stated values on the two sets and does not rely on constructing a new stopping time. It excludes approximating the separated template itself while retaining a common local modulus, but leaves room for a graph with different spatial assignments and the same output. In the directed-interval correction experiment archived in round 126 the compactness issue is instead tested on explicitly stored continuous graphs.

# Continuous calibration with the entire irregularity confined to one prescribed Cantor set

Fix the deterministic graph f and K of round 154. For any B_n<∞ and e_n>0 and any finite list per n of real or complex transform derivatives, there are bounded continuous g_n satisfying those exact equations, with transform residual ≤e_n uniformly on |Im z|≤B_n. They can have fixed values at two rational points with difference ≥1/8, the same strictly negative heat test at 7/8+10⁻¹² for all sufficiently large n, and converge pointwise everywhere to f. For every compact L disjoint from K, g_n equals f identically on L for all sufficiently large n, so the entire sequence has a common modulus there. On K itself it can also be taken equicontinuous. Any open neighborhood of any point of K fails equicontinuity, since the pointwise limit there is discontinuous. This localizes the compactness failure of round 145 to an arbitrarily small closed nowhere-dense set; for the explicit numerical heat witness use the unscaled K.

Write s=7/8 and t(x)=1+r(x) off K. For h↓0 define the preliminary graph equal to s on K and s+(t(x)−s)min(1,dist(x,K)/h) elsewhere. Boundedness of r gives continuity including at K, regardless of how the exterior branch behaves on approach. It equals f outside the h-collar of K and on K. Every strip kernel is uniformly bounded on the spatial compact containing the collars and on the fixed positive mark range, by quadratic damping in Re z. Thus the strip error is at most C_B γ(K_h∖K). For the explicit scaled Cantor set of diameter δ this collar mass is O(h+√(δ h)): the n-th generation has 2^(n−1) gaps of length at most Cδ4^(−n), each contributing at most twice h. Summing at the crossover 4^n≈δ/h gives the bound.

Fix the finite constraints for one stage. Choose a smooth time cutoff equal to 1 at s and zero on [1−1/32,∞). Correct the preliminary graph g by τ(g(x))Σ λ_i h_i(x) with fixed smooth compact bumps in x. At the limiting graph only K contributes to this derivative. For value equations at z the limiting spatial weight against γ|K is −z²exp(zx−z²s/2)/2. Its z derivatives give polynomial-exponential rows with independent leading coefficients (orders 0,1 at zero are automatic; order 2 at zero gives the mean). As in round 149, real and imaginary parts for nonredundant conjugate data give independent real rows; include lower derivative orders as necessary. Linear independence still holds on K minus a neighborhood of one prescribed endpoint, since it has positive measure and the functions are real analytic in x. Thus there exist fixed smooth test bumps supported away from that endpoint giving an invertible matrix, by testing linear combinations against the restricted measure. Use the rational left endpoint for the fixed early anchor, and 0 for the fixed late anchor.

For this finite system the preliminary Jacobians converge to the invertible one by bounded convergence. The base residual tends to zero and the second coefficient derivatives are uniformly bounded on a small ball. Inverse Jacobian contraction therefore supplies coefficients tending to zero as h↓0 and enforcing the exact equations. The corrections vanish outside K_h and at the anchors and can be kept within a fixed positive bounded mark range. Their strip error tends to zero, since all corrected x are in one compact. Choose h at stage n below 1/n and small enough for its specified strip tolerance; require also sup norms and spatial derivative norms of Σλ_i h_i below 1/n. This is possible for each fixed finite system and gives both pointwise convergence to f and equicontinuity on K, where τ(g)=1. Include the mean equation. At each fixed point outside K the equality g_n=f eventually holds. Weak convergence of graph laws and absence of a limiting atom at the test time in (s,1) give convergence of the smooth heat pairing. This yields a common strictly negative margin. For a scaled K one can instead choose a smooth negative test by approximation from the negative slice on K, with test and time allowed to depend on that fixed K.

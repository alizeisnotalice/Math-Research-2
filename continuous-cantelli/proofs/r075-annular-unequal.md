# Unequal contraction ratios, with a direct renewal proof

The annular lower bound and the local and circle exclusion in r075-annular.md hold for arbitrary finitely many strong-separated similarities, including reflections, with contraction ratios 0<r_i<1 and dimension 0<d<1 determined by ∑r_i^d=1. We prove the additional tube-mass fact self-containedly. Write D(x)=dist(x,E) on the whole line and Z(σ)=∫ exp[−(D(x)/σ)^α]dx; constants are absorbed into σ. Let μ be the self-similar probability with weights p_i=r_i^d. Given δ, stop at the first word w with contraction r_w≤δ. Then r_min δ<r_w≤δ, μ(E_w)=r_w^d, and two distinct stopping sets have separation ≥cδ, since their longest common prefix is strictly before stopping and the generator compact sets have positive separation. The proof of d-regularity and |E_σ|≍σ^{1−d} from the equal-ratio case therefore still applies. In particular F(t)=e^{(1−d)t} Z(e^{−t}) is bounded above and below for t≥0. All derivatives of fixed finite order are bounded on t≥0 by the same tubular estimates, since differentiating the weight in t introduces only a polynomial in (D/σ)^α.

**A scalar renewal asymptotic.** Write a_i=−log r_i. Then
F(t)=∑p_i F(t−a_i)+e(t),  with |e(t)|≤C exp(−c exp(αt)) for t sufficiently large.
Indeed the full-line tilt for E is the maximum of the individual tilts for its first-generation copies, whose integrals are r_i Z(σ/r_i). At any point each term other than a maximizer has D_i≥half the minimum distance between the copies. The integrated difference between sum and maximum is bounded by C exp(−c/σ^α), using half the exponent for integrability. Scaling gives the claimed bound.

Every translate sequence F(t_j+·), t_j→∞, has locally uniformly convergent subsequences by the derivative bounds. A limit f on ℝ is bounded and continuous and obeys f(x)=∑p_i f(x−a_i). We show that every a_i is a period of f. Iterate this identity n times and compare f(x−a_i) and f(x−a_j). The two shifted sums average f against the laws of ∑K_l a_l where the count vectors have laws Multinomial(n,p)+e_i and Multinomial(n,p)+e_j. Relative to Multinomial(n+1,p), the corresponding densities are K_i/((n+1)p_i) and K_j/((n+1)p_j). Each tends to 1 in L¹ with error O(n^{−1/2}) by its variance. Consequently f(x−a_i)=f(x−a_j); the harmonic identity then gives f(x)=f(x−a_i) for all i.

If the subgroup generated by the a_i is dense in ℝ, every such f is constant. Its value is unique: G(t)=∑p_i∫_{t−a_i}^t F(u)du has derivative e(t), hence a finite limit G∞ at infinity. Every constant translate limit must be G∞/∑p_i a_i. Thus F(t)→Q for a positive constant Q.
If the subgroup is hℤ, write a_i=k_i h. Every translate limit has period h. Hence F(t−lh)−F(t)→0 for each fixed integer l, by compactness. Put G_h(t)=∑p_i∑_{l=0}^{k_i−1} F(t−lh). Its h-step difference G_h(t)−G_h(t−h)=e(t). Summability of e gives uniform convergence of G_h(θ+nh) on θ∈[0,h] to a continuous h-periodic function (endpoint values agree by shifting n). The preceding translate property yields G_h(t)−(∑p_i k_i)F(t)→0. In either case there is a positive bounded continuous Q on ℝ such that F(t)=Q(t)+o(1), and Q is invariant under all shifts a_i, therefore under −log r_w for every word w. Positivity uniformly away from zero follows from the lower tubular bound.

**Uniform stopping-cell masses.** With δ≈ε/N fixed as in the annular proof and σ_s=(a(sN)^2)^{−1/α}, s∈[1/3,3C], one has δ/σ_s→∞ uniformly. The full-line tilt mass for a single stopping copy E_w is
r_w Z(σ_s/r_w)=r_w^d σ_s^{1−d} F(log(r_w/σ_s)).
Since the last argument as well as log(1/σ_s) tends uniformly to infinity, the ratio F(log(r_w/σ_s))/F(log(1/σ_s))=1+o(1) uniformly in w and s, using the exact invariance of Q. Restrict to the disjoint cδ-tubes U_w around E_w; there D equals the individual copy distance. The removed mass is exponentially small relative to r_w^d Z(σ_s), by using half the exponential for the tail and the full individual mass at the enlarged scale for integrability. Denoting the mass in U_w by W_w(s), we get ∥W_w/(r_w^d Z(σ_s))−1∥∞=o(1). Its derivatives through order three are uniformly bounded: for each numerator derivative use the integral at a fixed enlarged σ_s, bounded by C r_w^d σ_s^{1−d}; denominator derivatives obey the same estimate with weight 1. On a slightly smaller s interval the ratio consequently converges to 1 in C², by finite differences or elementary interpolation of the uniform bound on third derivatives and the vanishing sup norm.

Normalize by the full Z(σ_s). In the main proof's cell comparison, the unequal mass now produces the constant-phase term [W_w/Z−r_w^d]w₀(x_w), for the smooth spatial weight w₀; this term has C² norm r_w^d o(1). The remaining within-cell variation has C² norm O(ε r_w^d) by the same conditional-derivative argument. The large-sieve bound for the full squared error is therefore O((N+δ^(−1)) δ^d (ε+o(1))²), using ∑ r_w^{2d}≤δ^d. First fix a sufficiently small ε and then take N large, precisely as before. The endpoint and local-output arguments are unchanged. This proves the full claim without an arithmetic restriction on the ratios.

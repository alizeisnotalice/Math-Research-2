# First departure, competing wells and effective tail times

Write c(x)=F_*(|x|), H=sup f, and assume only that f is a bounded continuous nonnegative exact graph. Put E₊={f>c}, E₋={f<c}, m₊=inf_{E₊}c, m₋=inf_{E₋}f. Let C_σ be the effective tail onset of round 104 on side σ; these numbers are computed from tail sublevel masses there. Then

    m₊=min(m₋,C₊)=min(m₋,C₋).                                    (1)

Both discrepancy sets are nonempty. Indeed canonical radii reach arbitrarily large distances since the embedded normal has unbounded support, but remain finite at every finite time: survival inside a fixed small interior interval for a further finite time has positive probability; if the boundary went infinite at that time these survivors could never stop. Thus the inverse c is unbounded. E₋ contains both sufficiently remote tails. If E₊ were empty, round 137 would identify f with c on every finite interval, violating boundedness.

**Proof of (1).** Choose S>H+1 and R>0 with c(R)=S. The entire later-than-canonical set E₊ is inside (−R,R). Larger R may always be used. At signed tilt σz, z>0, q=z²/2, the exact transform and the canonical martingale truncated at S give

    P(z)+exp(−qS)∫exp(σzy)η_S(dy)=N_R(z)+∫_{|x|>R}exp(σzx−qf(x))γ(dx).

Here P integrates exp(σzx)(exp(−qc)−exp(−qf)) on E₊, and N_R integrates its opposite on E₋ inside the truncation. The rate −log P/q tends to m₊: compactness gives one bound; for the reverse bound fix a closed interval of strict discrepancy with c≤m₊+ε, then let ε↓0. The same argument works for N_R with infimum of f on the truncated open E₋ (empty mass has infinite rate). The canonical remainder costs at least S−o(1). The disfavored remote wing has rate equal to its infimum of f, since its linear term only reduces the integral and every fixed compact strict subinterval gives the lower bound. For the favored remote wing use its lower limiting rate as in round 104. In a sum of positive terms where the other rates are limits one takes the minimum with that lower limiting rate. As R increases, the compact infimum decreases to m₋, the disfavored wing infimum is always ≥m₋, and the favored rate increases to C_σ. This proves (1).

**A spatial crossing constraint.** Suppose T=m₊<min(C₊,C₋), so m₋=T. For each of E₊ and E₋ take the set K₊,K₋ of limits of sequences therein whose respective heights c or f tend to T. Both are nonempty compact sets: round 104 gives liminf of either remote f at least its corresponding C_σ>T. Their closed convex hulls intersect. Otherwise choose s strictly separating their hulls. For all sufficiently small ε>0 the two sets of discrepancy with height <T+ε are compactly localized on opposite sides of s, at a positive distance from s (take their closures). Use the previous finite truncation equality for both signs of z, multiply by exp(−σzs), and subtract. This replaces exp(σzx) by 2sinh(z(x−s)) and gives the two discrepancies opposite algebraic signs. In the low-height portions the separated supports make their contributions to this signed equation all of one sign. The rest has absolute size O(exp(−q(T+ε/2))) after shrinking ε and taking R sufficiently large, by the strict tail-rate gap, compact high-part gap and the canonical remainder. But one fixed positive-length interval of strict low discrepancy with height <T+ε/4 gives a contribution at least a constant times exp(−q(T+ε/4)−O(z)). This contradiction proves the intersection.

If b=inf{|x|:f(x)>c(x)}>0 then T=c(b), f=c on [−b,b] by round 137, and K₊⊂{−b,b}. If there is no exterior point of height T, also K₋⊂{−b,b}. Intersecting hulls of nonempty subsets of these two endpoints then implies a common endpoint. On that wing there are strict deviations from c in both directions arbitrarily close outside b. Otherwise a remote well of height exactly T occurs and it is subject to the budgets of rounds 138–139. This dichotomy does not assume a finite number of sign changes or branches.

# Reciprocal centers on a tail force an inverse-square pointwise rate

Let 0≤f≤H be continuous and exact, with ν_t(dx)=exp(tx−t² f(x)/2)γ(dx) a probability for all t. On the positive tail assume f>0 eventually, the reciprocal center m(x)=x/f(x) is nondecreasing eventually, and
                    ν_t([0,R])→0  for every finite R>0 as t→+∞.           (E)
There is no condition on the negative wing. Then
                    |f(x)−1|=O(x^{−2}) as x→+∞.                         (1)
Moreover for every bounded interval I the integral of exp(−x²(f(x)−1)/2) on t+I tends to |I|. If x²(f(x)−1) is uniformly continuous on the positive tail, it tends to zero. Reflection gives the independent negative-tail conclusion for r/f(−r), r>0, and negative parameter tilts.

Condition (E) holds if f is strictly positive on the whole closed ray [0,∞), since every [0,R] then has a positive minimum. Positivity on (0,∞) alone does not give that argument at zero. If f>0 on (0,∞) and f(x)≥c x² on a short [0,δ], (E) also holds: that part of the integral is O(1/t) by v=tx and ∫exp(v−cv²/2)dv<∞, and [δ,R] has a positive minimum. On the opposite half-line, ν_t(x<0)≤∫_{−∞}⁰ exp(tx)g(x)dx=O(1/t) always.

**First obtain f→1 without rates.** If f(x_j)≤1−a for x_j→∞ and fixed a>0, monotonicity of m gives f(y)≤(y/x_j)f(x_j)≤1−a/2 on [x_j,x_j+1] for large j. In ν_{x_j}=exp[−x_j²(f−1)/2]γ_{x_j} this contradicts normalization. Hence liminf f≥1. Conversely if f(x_j)≥1+a, set t_j=(m(x_j)+x_j)/2. On a fixed relative neighborhood |y−t_j|≤c_a t_j all points are below x_j and their m(y)≤m(x_j) implies f(y)≥1+c'_a; t_j and x_j are comparable by boundedness of f. This neighborhood contributes exponentially little to ν_{t_j}. On y≥R outside it, f≥1−c_a²/4 once R is chosen sufficiently large, and the Gaussian shift gives exponential decay again. Condition (E) and the negative half-line bound remove the remaining input. This contradicts total mass one. In particular ν_t(X<t/2)→0: on [R,t/2] use f≥1−1/16 and the shift tail bound, then use (E) and the negative-wing bound.

**A local estimate for shifted means.** Fix large t>0, K_t=[t/2,2t], η=sup_{K_t}|f−1|, M=t²η and r=√(1+M)/t. Let W=X−tf(X), and let μ_t denote the law of W under ν_t restricted to X∈K_t. For every bounded interval J on the W axis,
 μ_t(J)≤C(|J|+r).                                                      (2)
For every J⊂[−1,1],
 c|J|≤μ_t(J)+C r                                                      (3)
with absolute positive constants for all sufficiently large t (depending on the graph). Both estimates resolve intervals much smaller than unit scale.

Here are details. Choose a nonnegative Schwartz probability k whose Fourier transform is supported in [−1/2,1/2], for instance the normalized squared modulus of the inverse Fourier transform of a nonzero smooth bump supported in [−1/4,1/4]. Add independent noise of density t k(t·). On the retained set f≤1+η also add independent Gaussian noise of variance 1+η−f to W. The total smoothed subprobability has Fourier transform within 2exp(−c t²) on |u|≤t/2 of the transform of N(0,1+η) convolved with t k(t·), and both vanish outside this band. Indeed the exact transform at t+iu gives E_{ν_t} exp(iu W+u² f/2)=1. The absolute error from omission before multiplying by the characteristic function of k is bounded by
      ∫_{f>1+η} exp[−(t²−u²)(f−1)/2−ηu²/2] dγ_t ≤ γ_t(K_t^c).
Thus the smoothed density differs from the reference density uniformly by O(t exp(−c t²)), by Fourier inversion. The reference density is bounded everywhere and bounded below uniformly on [−1,1] as η→0.

Removing retained inputs outside K_t preserves the upper bound and costs o(1) in uniform density. For X<t/2, f≤1/2 the added variance is ≥1/2 and their ν_t mass tends to zero. On X≤t/8, f>1/2 the tilt is at most exp(−t²/8) relative to γ; on [t/8,t/2] and [2t,∞) use f→1 and the shifted Gaussian to get exponentially small mass. The smoothing kernel t k(t·) bounds their density by Ct times this mass. Write S_t for the remaining local smoothed law. Its density is therefore between fixed positive constants on [−1,1] and uniformly bounded above on the line. Its conditional noise about W has every fixed absolute moment of order j bounded by C_j r^j, because variance ≤2η locally and k is Schwartz. In particular a fixed enlargement J+ [−C'r,C'r] captures at least half the mass initially in J, proving (2). To compare S_t(J) with μ_t(J), divide W-space into strips nr≤dist(W,∂J)<(n+1)r. Each is covered by at most four intervals of length r, of total mass O(r) by (2). A status change costs at most min(1,C/(1+n)^2) by the noise moment bound. The sum gives |S_t(J)−μ_t(J)|≤Cr. The density lower bound proves (3).

**High and low deviations.** Suppose first that f(x)>1, put D=x−m(x)>0 and choose t=(x+m(x))/2. For large x one has t/x→1 and 1/2≤f≤2 on K_t. Put J=[−s,s] with s=min(D/8,1/2). For X∈K_t and W∈J, the identity W=f(X)(m(X)−t) implies m(x)<m(X)<x, since |m(X)−t|≤2s≤D/4. Monotonicity forces X≥x. On these sources f(X)−1≥(X−x)/x, so
 μ_t(J)≤C∫_x^∞ exp[−t²(X−x)/(2x)]dX≤C/t.
By (3) this bounds min(D,1)≤C(r+1/t). Since r→0, eventually D≤C√(1+M)/t. In particular x²(f(x)−1)=xf(x)D≤C√(1+M).

If f(x)<1 take t=x and set L=t²(1−f(t)). Monotonicity gives f(y)≤(y/t)f(t) for t≤y≤t+1/t, so t²(1−f(y))≥L−1 there. The ν_t mass of that interval is ≥c exp(L/2)/t. Its W values are all in [−(1+M)/t,(1+M)/t]. Bound (2) yields exp(L/2)≤C(1+M), hence L≤C+2log(1+M). Neither estimate uses a derivative of f or m.

**Closing the estimate on neighboring shells.** Let b_j=sup_{2^j≤x≤2^{j+1}} x²|f(x)−1|. In either test K_t⊂[x/4,4x] eventually, and M≤C max_{|i−j|≤3} b_i. Absorbing the logarithm into a square root gives b_j≤C[1+√max_{|i−j|≤3} b_i] for all j above a fixed threshold. If the b_j were unbounded, choose one exceeding a sufficiently large constant and all values on the finitely many initial shells and their three neighbors. Iteration then selects a neighbor satisfying b_{j_next}≥b_j²/(4C²), increasing at least doubly exponentially. The indices move by at most three per step, and cannot enter the finite initial shells since the values are increasing. This contradicts the a priori b_i≤C'4^i from boundedness of f. Equation (1) follows. In fact the asymptotic limsup is bounded by an absolute constant: after boundedness has been proved, take limsups in the recurrence to get L≤C(1+√L) for L=limsup b_j.

**Translated averages and rigidity.** Write a(x)=x²(f(x)−1). The positive-tail translates of e^{−a/2} are uniformly bounded, so any sequence t_j→∞ has a locally weak-star convergent subsequence with limit H. In the real identity at t=t_j+s for fixed s, restrict first to |X−t|≤K. There t²(f(X)−1)=a(X)+o(1) uniformly. The tails can be made uniformly negligible by K→∞: on X≥t/2 use (1) for a uniform multiplier bound, and X<t/2 has ν_t mass o(1) as above. Thus p₁*H=1 for every s. Gaussian convolution is injective on bounded tempered distributions, by local division by its nonzero Fourier multiplier, hence H=1. This proves all asserted interval averages. If a is uniformly continuous, each subsequence has locally uniformly convergent translates, which must therefore limit identically to zero for a.

If the hypotheses hold at both infinities, a nonconstant graph has an attained global maximum >1 since Eγf=1 and both tails tend to one. The round-23 analytic-maximum theorem rules this out if some attained maximum has a nonconstant analytic germ. Analyticity only inside an open plateau would not give that premise. For a globally real analytic exact graph (E) always holds on each wing: off-origin zeros contradict the real tilt by a 1/|t| window using the quadratic analytic upper bound; a zero at zero has order two since order four or more contradicts the tilt on [2log t/t,3log t/t]. This is also the compact-source check in rounds 25–26. Thus a globally real analytic f with both reciprocal centers eventually nondecreasing is constant, without the derivative-growth restrictions in R026. Eventual center monotonicity alone for an arbitrary continuous graph has not been shown to exclude a rough attained maximum. The proof's compact Fourier smoothing refines R026's truncated complex Esscher transform; R040 already contains the global reciprocal-center area identity, which is not claimed as new here.

## Quantitative relaxation to centers with backward excursions

Monotonicity can be replaced by bounds on
d(x)=sup{(m(y)−m(z))_+ : x/4≤y≤z≤4x}.
Assume (E), eventual f>0, and d(x)=o(x). Then f(x)→1 without a rate assumption. Indeed the preceding low-point proof now uses m(y)≥m(x)−d(x) over [x,x+1], which still preserves a fixed fraction of any macroscopic deficit in f. In the high-point proof, over the relative window around t=(x+m(x))/2 one uses m(y)≤m(x)+d(x); that window is in [x/4,x] after choosing its relative radius small. The resulting fixed high excess contradicts normalization exactly as before.

The smoothed estimates (2)–(3) only require f→1 and (E), hence also hold here. In the high test either D=x−m(x)≤4d(x), or the previous interval J (with |W|≤min(D/8,1/2)) forces m(X)≥m(x)+D/4>m(x)+d(x), so all its local inputs must satisfy X≥x. The previous proof gives
x²(f(x)−1)_+ ≤ C[1+x d(x)+√(1+M)].
For a low point repeat the unit-over-t interval at t=x with m(y)≥m(t)−d(t); since f→1 and d=o(t), this gives t²(1−f(y))≥t²(1−f(t))−C(1+t d(t)). The same local mass estimate yields
x²(1−f(x))_+ ≤ C[1+x d(x)+log(1+M)].
In each bound M is local at its own testing center t≍x. Therefore if d(x)=O(x^{−β}) for 0≤β≤1 then |f(x)−1|=O(x^{−1−β}). Precisely, divide the prior shell recurrence by 2^{(1−β)j}; the normalized shell supremum satisfies the same square-root neighboring-shell inequality with an adjusted constant, and the former at-most-exponential upper bound remains valid. If d(x)=o(1), then x|f(x)−1|→0 follows directly from the two pointwise bounds and M=o(x²). A finite total negative variation for m on a tail is a sufficient condition for d(x)=o(1). The analytic rigidity conclusion consequently holds already when both outward reciprocal centers have only sublinear backward excursions on proportional tail intervals and both compact-source escape hypotheses hold.

Without any ordering assumption, estimates (2)–(3) themselves give a quantitative count of the backward inputs when f→1 and (E) holds. At a high point x use D,t,J as in its proof. Inputs X≥x with W∈J still have mass ≤C/t. Thus at least c min(D,1)−C√(1+M)/t of ν_t mass must come from X∈K_t, X<x, and m(X)>m(x)+D/4. This is informative precisely when that expression is positive and supplies a spatially localized obstruction to isolated high points without an appreciable backward excursion.

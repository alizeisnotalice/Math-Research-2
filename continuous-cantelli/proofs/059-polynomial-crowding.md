# Quantitative escape permits polynomially decreasing separation

Use the paired-input distribution, normalized amplitude vector and notation of r052-separated-escaping-peaks.md. Assume 0<β<2 and −log Z(t)≤C t^{2−β} for all large t. Suppose the local mean-square and modulus conditions in the earlier appendix hold, namely E_Q|x−x_j|²=O(t^−2) and liminf ‖b(t)‖₂>0. If the local center separation satisfies Δ(R)≥c R^{-b} for large R with b<β/(2−β), exactness is impossible.

Indeed the Laplace action F(s)=−log Z(√s)^2 is nonnegative and concave, with derivative E_Q Y at s=t². Thus E_Q Y≤[F(t²)−F(0)]/t²≤C' t^−β. In a dyadic interval [T,2T] the energy ∫ t Var_Q(Y)dt equals half its endpoint mean drop, hence is O(T^−β). Keep only centers of modulus ≤R_T=K T^{1−β/2}, with K large enough that the input tail P(|X|>R_T−W) divided by Z(t) tends to zero exponentially, uniformly on this dyadic interval: more explicitly use Z(t)≥exp[−C 2^{2−β}T^{2−β}] and choose K²/2>C 2^{2−β} with slack. The discarded ℓ² tail is at most this same normalized input bound. A fixed large multiple L_T of R_T^b gives spaced-frequency frame constants comparable to √L_T for the retained centers. We have L_T=o(T) and L_T² T^−β→0 precisely under the stated strict bound. The averaging and freezing argument from the first appendix therefore works in every sufficiently large dyadic interval.

For example assume the power-shaped wells of the main theorem and that for some p>0, a≥2, K<∞ there is, for every sufficiently large R, at least one center of modulus in [R,KR] with ε_j≤K R^−p and w_j≥exp(−K R^a). Take R=t^{2/(p+a)}. Because a≥2 and p>0 this center is o(t), so the elementary inner-well lower bound gives
Z(t)≥A_j(t)≥c' t^−2/α w_j exp(−x_j²/2−t²ε_j/2)≥exp(−C t^{2a/(p+a)}).
Thus β=2p/(p+a) and every gap exponent b<p/a is allowed. Analogous concrete quadratic-well versions follow using the inner real Gaussian bound on |u|≤w_j/t. This conclusion uses a lower input mass on every large amplitude scale; an unspecified sparse sequence of records alone does not guarantee the displayed polynomial action.

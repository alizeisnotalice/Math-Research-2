# A one-sided fractional test and the linear opening alternative at a highest point

Translate an attained maximum a to the origin. Write w(u)=H−f(a+u), g_a(u)=p₁(a+u), and let φ=p₁. The left wing u<0 is unrestricted. For 0<κ<1 define the one-sided fractional difference functional
$$
 {\cal D}_\kappa h=\int_0^\infty [h(a+y)-h(a)]y^{-1-\kappa}\,dy,\qquad
 H_\kappa(z)=\int_0^\infty[\phi(t-z)-\phi(z)]t^{-1-\kappa}\,dt .
$$
For a smoothed source of variance b=w(u)+s>0 at a+u its test value is exactly b^{-(1+κ)/2}H_κ(u/√b). At fixed s>0 exchange of integrals in the smoothed density identity follows by the uniform boundedness of both the kernels and their first derivatives. The target D_κ p_{H+s} is bounded uniformly down to s=0. Every u≤0 gives a nonpositive contribution. The positive parts from u≥δ>0 have bounded integral uniformly in s: for y<δ/2 the off-diagonal variance-uniform derivative bound is C/δ², while the positive contribution for y≥δ/2 is bounded by (δ/2)^(-1−κ) times the kernel mass. Thus only the small right wing can compensate strongly negative sources on that wing.

**Sign threshold.** Set G(z)=∫₀∞ t^{-κ}exp(zt−t²/2)dt. Integration by parts in H_κ gives κ H_κ(z)=φ(z)[zG(z)−G′(z)]. A second integration by parts yields G″=zG′+(1−κ)G. Hence (zG−G′)′=κG>0, its value at 0 is negative, and it becomes positive at some positive z since G≥G(0) on the positive half-line. Denote the unique zero by z_κ. On [0,z_κ/2] there is a strictly negative uniform upper bound for H_κ. Also (H_κ)_+(z)≤C z^(-1−κ) on the positive axis. For z≥2 bound the difference at t<z/2 by t times the supremum of |φ′| at distances at least z/2, which gives an exponentially small integral. At t≥z/2 discard the subtraction and integrate φ(t−z) with weight at most (z/2)^(-1−κ); the compact z range follows by continuity.

Consequently, if
$$
 \int_{\{0<u<\delta:\ w(u)\le u^2/z_\kappa^2\}}u^{-1-\kappa}\,du<\infty ,
$$
then the integral of w(u)^(-(1+κ)/2) over 0<u<δ with w(u)≥4u²/z_κ² is finite. Indeed the positive parts for b=w+s can occur only on the first set and are ≤C u^(-1−κ) there. On the second set the test is ≤−c b^(-(1+κ)/2). Use the bounded target and the other-source bounds and then Fatou, noting that g_a is bounded below near zero. In particular if w(u)/u²→∞ and w>0 near zero, all one-sided inverse moments ∫₀^δ w^(-p)du are finite for 1/2<p<1, regardless of the other wing.

**Endpoint improvement.** Suppose in this last case also ∫₀^δ u w^(-3/2)du<∞. Then |{0<u<δ:w(u)≤s}|≤Cs for small s (decrease δ so w is strictly positive off zero). Indeed the unsmoothed positive-variance density J at a is ≤p_H(a). At a+y with 0<y<δ it equals p_H(a+y): the zero-variance output vanishes on this open side, and the positive-age integral is continuous locally there by separation from other sources and positivity of the local variance. Consequently J(a+y)−J(a)≥−Cy. All left sources decrease. Right sources with u≥δ have total positive difference O(y) by the off-diagonal derivative bound. For u>y/2 in the near wing the positive difference is at most C y u w^(-3/2), by comparing the two Gaussian exponentials. For u≤y/2 the difference is nonpositive. Every near-wing point with w≤y² has u≤y/4 for small y by w/u²→∞ and a positive minimum away from zero. On that set p_w(−u)≥c/√w and the ratio p_w(y−u)/p_w(−u)≤exp(−1/4). Therefore ∫_{w≤y²}w^(-1/2)du≤Cy, giving the stated estimate.

**A dichotomy with only one-sided comparison.** Suppose there is B≥1 such that w(v)≥w(u)/B whenever u≤v≤2u are small positive radii. This includes every nondecreasing wing. Then either w=o(u²) or w(u)≥c u throughout a sufficiently small right neighborhood. To see the dichotomy, if w(r_j)/r_j² stays between two strictly positive constants along r_j↓0, then w(u)/u² is bounded above on [r_j/2,r_j] and bounded away from zero on [r_j,2r_j]. Round 21 shows the relative measure of any fixed intermediate band of ratios tends to zero. Its common phase fraction of the small ratios on the first and second annuli would therefore be respectively one and zero, a contradiction. Continuity of the ratio then gives w/u²→0 or ∞ (including a constant zero neighborhood in the first case). In the latter case take 3/4<p<1; for u/2≤v≤u one has w(v)≤B w(u), so the finite pth inverse moment implies u≤C w(u)^p. Thus ∫u w^(-3/2)du is finite by the already proved inverse moment of order 3/2−p. The endpoint estimate and the same inward interval comparison imply u/2≤|{w≤B w(u)}|≤CB w(u), proving the linear lower bound. No regular variation, two-sided monotonicity, or hypothesis on other maximal points enters this conclusion.

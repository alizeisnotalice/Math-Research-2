# Quadratic and gradually crowding extensions

Use the notation of r052-separated-escaping-peaks.md.


For α=2 and only the comparability inequalities, the same conclusion holds if widths tend to zero as |x_j|→∞. Write u=w_j s/t. For |x_j|≤At the conditional density in s is bounded after normalization by C_A exp[A W |s|−c₁s²/2], and its denominator is uniformly bounded below on |s|≤1. Thus E u²≤C_A w_j²/t². The pair mass of any finite set of centers tends to zero when all ε_j>0, and the mass |x_j|>At is exponentially small. These facts give (1).

With w_j only bounded, a genuine quadratic expansion suffices instead of shrinking widths. Assume the same comparability with α=2 and uniformly in j and on each sign separately
v(x_j+u)−ε_j=(u/w_j)²(c_{j,±}+o(1)) as |u|/w_j→0,
where c_{j,±} lie in a common positive compact interval. Then E_Q u²=O(t^−2) by the previous Gaussian majorant. To obtain a lower bound on ‖b(t)‖₂, first restrict |x_j|≤η t with fixed small η>0. For bounded s=tu/w_j the source tilt and finite-end cutoff in s yield the two half-Gaussian densities e^{−c_{j,±}s²/2}, up to uniform additive O(η) on compact s and o(1) as t→∞; equivalently use dominated tails first, then let η↓0. Uniformly for w_j≤W their normalized cosine integral at frequency w_j is bounded below by exp[−W²/(2 min c_{j,±})]>0, since each half-Gaussian cosine integral is half its full Gaussian integral. More precisely multiply the half-Gaussians by exp[−(x_j/t)w_js−w_j²s²/(2t²)] in the rescaled integrals; the normalized expectations differ from zero tilt by O(η)+o(1) using the Gaussian majorant. The complement |x_j|>ηt has exponentially small pair mass for each fixed η. Choosing η small gives the required positive lower norm. This needs no common phases of the centers.

The fixed center spacing may be replaced by a quantitative local gap. For R≥1 let Δ(R) be the minimum of 1 and all distances between distinct centers of modulus ≤R. Require Δ(R)>0 for each R and ∫₁∞Δ(R)² dR/R=∞, with 1/Δ(R)=o(R) (the argument only needs this last condition on the selected dyadic blocks). In particular Δ(R)≳1/√log(R+2) suffices. Assume the concentration/moment estimates of the power-shaped or quadratic corollaries, in particular E_Q u²=O(t^−2) and liminf‖b(t)‖₂>0. Put T=2^l, L_l=K/Δ(3T), E_l=∫_T^{2T}t Var_Q(Y)dt, for a fixed large K. Monotonicity and the integral assumption imply ∑L_l^−2=∞, while ∑ E_l<∞, so some l→∞ have L_l²E_l→0. Partition [T,2T] into intervals of length between L_l and 2L_l (possible for large T since L_l=o(T)). One interval J obeys
|J| ∫_J‖b'‖₂² ≤ C[L_l² E_l + L_l²/T²]→0,
by averaging and ∫_T^{2T}t²Var_Q(Y)dt≤2T E_l. Freeze on J and keep only |x_j|≤3T. At all t∈J the discarded core transform and its ℓ² vector tail are exponentially small after division by Z (their unnormalized ℓ¹ prior mass is ≤P(|X|>3T−W)). The frame bounds on the remainder are uniform multiples of √|J|, since |J|Δ(3T)≥K. The frozen lower bound has this order, while the varying error is o(√|J|). The excluded complement of the core remains negligible in the normalized exact identity. Thus the same contradiction holds even for these gradually crowding centers.

# Temporal continuation at regular levels and one-sided uniqueness at Puiseux levels

Let y be an early spatial cut, f continuous with f(y)<T, and suppose q(t,y) is the signed heat density at that cut. Assume that, modulo Gaussian-null sets, the sources with T−2δ<f(x)<T are represented by finitely many one-to-one inverse branches x=X_j(s), T−2δ<s<T. Their time density is W_j(s)=g(X_j(s))·(±X'_j(s)) with the fixed sign of the real branch derivative. All sources at times ≤T−δ may instead be treated together by positive-age smoothing. No source at a time ≥T contributes before T. Finiteness and exhaustiveness of these branches, or suitable uniform replacements, matter if a level can also be approached at spatial infinity.

**Regular level.** If the branches extend real analytically through T with finite endpoints and represent all sources on both sides of T (no level atom there), then q(t,y) is real analytic across T. If only the lower-side branches extend analytically, this still gives a holomorphic extension of the left germ; it need not coincide with the actual q on the other side. Indeed split each recent contribution ∫_{T−δ}^t p_{t−s}(y−X_j(s))W_j(s)ds into the interval of ages 0<u=t−s<δ/2 and the remainder. At small age the positive real u can be fixed also for complex t near T. Re[(y−X_j(t−u))²]>c>0 uniformly for small δ and a small complex neighborhood, since each finite endpoint is separated from y. The holomorphic integrand is bounded by C u⁻¹ᐟ² exp(−c/(2u)). The remainder, with lower time endpoint T−δ and upper t−δ/2, is holomorphic by a short contour deformation or affine parametrization of this segment; its ages have positive real part bounded away from zero. The common earlier term is likewise holomorphic, by uniform Gaussian domination. In particular the earlier sources require no spatial regularity.

**One-sided critical level.** More generally suppose each branch has a convergent Puiseux expansion in v=T−s at v=0+, with finite real endpoint. The portion of q(t,y) for t<T has a unique germ among these functions with a prescribed infinite jet at T: if all derivatives at T from below are zero, then q(t,y)=0 on a left neighborhood of T. Atoms at T and arbitrary behavior above T are permitted. In particular such a T cannot be the late extinction time from R164–166 of a low component. No claim of ordinary analyticity at a ramified branch is required.

To prove uniqueness put h=T−t, and split the integral by age as above. Use the notation F_j(v)=X_j(T−v), B_j(v)=W_j(T−v). The only nonanalytic candidate is

    G(h)=∫₀^{δ/2} (2πu)^(-1/2) exp(−(y−F_j(h+u))²/(2u)) B_j(h+u)du.

The Puiseux expansions extend into a punctured slit disk in v with the positive real determination. With δ small, B_j(v)=O(|v|^(-p_j)) for some p_j<1 (increase p_j to 0 if necessary), and Re[(y−F_j(v))²]>c>0 on closed proper subsectors. Work on |arg h|≤θ=3π/4 with |h| small. There |h+u|≥cos(θ/2)(|h|+u), and the argument of h+u lies between 0 and arg h. A complex h-circle of radius c₀u remains in a slightly larger safe v-sector for the individual integrand, with c₀>0 uniform. Cauchy's estimate under the integral gives

    |G^(n)(h)| ≤ C n! c₀^(−n) ∫₀^{δ/2} u^(−n−p_j−1/2) exp(−c/(2u))du
                ≤ C A^n(n!)² .

The second estimate follows by substitution 1/u, absorbing fixed powers of n into A^n. Each derivative has a common limit as h→0 in the closed sector by uniform domination at fixed n; these limits are precisely the one-sided real jet. The other, analytic, pieces satisfy the same estimates. Thus if the real jet vanishes, Taylor remainder along every radial segment yields |q(T−h,y)|≤C (A|h|)^n n! for all n. Taking n=floor(1/(2A|h|)) gives a bound C' exp(−c'/|h|) uniform in the closed sector.

For the sector uniqueness step set V(w)=q(T−1/w,y). For large R the half-plane Re w≥R lies in the domain and |V(w)|≤C exp(−c'|w|). The vertical integral ∫_{R−i∞}^{R+i∞} exp(zw)V(w)dw is holomorphic in |Im z|<c'/2. For real z<c'/2, shifting the vertical integration line to +∞ gives zero, since |w|≥(Re w+|Im w|)/√2 on the right half-plane. The integral therefore vanishes for every real z; Fourier uniqueness on that vertical line implies V≡0. This proves the claim without an external summability theorem. At a proposed extinction time the real jet vanishes automatically because the locally smooth solution is zero just afterwards (also for T=H, by extending the signed flow by zero). Applying this argument at two distinct low cuts contradicts the temporal parity theorem R166.

**Optional escaping branches.** The same proof with Gevrey order one allows a branch with a convergent Laurent–Puiseux pole F_j(v)=c_j v^(−p_j)(1+o(1)), c_j real nonzero, 0<p_j<1/2. For the finitely many such branches choose π/2<θ<θ'<min(π,min_j π/(4p_j)). In the larger sector Re F_j²≥c |v|^(−2p_j) and Re(y−F_j)² has the same lower bound, while the differentiated branch contributes at most a fixed power of |v|⁻¹. The Gaussian factors decay there, including g(F_j). The same Cauchy integral and half-plane proof apply, together with any finite-endpoint branches.

## A criterion stated solely in terms of the graph

Assume that for every T in (1,sup f], the source in some one-sided band T−δ<f<T has the finite complete branch representation above with separated finite Puiseux endpoints or escaping poles of order less than 1/2. Empty bands are allowed. Then the only bounded continuous exact graph is f=1. Otherwise the mean gives a nonempty component of {f<1}; its common extinction time belongs to this range (use H=sup f). At that level apply the jet uniqueness at two distinct points of the component. Finite endpoints automatically have f=T by continuity, hence are separated from either point. This uses no local description of any submean germ, no regularity of the time-T level set, and no fixed bound on the number of branches as T varies. The finite-type hypothesis applies only to the past band of each possible extinction time. Round 61 already covered globally subanalytic graphs by a different argument.

More generally the finite-endpoint proof only requires sector holomorphy of the inverse and its density, a common finite limit distinct from the cut, and at most polynomial growth of the density on a sector wider than a half-plane. One substitutes an arbitrary fixed exponent M for p_j in the Cauchy integral, which still converges because of exp(−c/u). Thus the finite-endpoint test includes inverse germs X(T−v)=a+c/[log(B/v)]^α with α>0, B>1 and real nonzero c. On every closed proper subsector of the slit disk the principal logarithm has real part tending to infinity, the inverse tends uniformly to a and the derivative grows at most polynomially. The underlying graph has exponentially flat contact in this example.

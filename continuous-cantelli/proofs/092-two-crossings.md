# Analytic continuation at two finite critical crossings excludes an unequal far tail

Assume the early R084 hypotheses and conclusions through time T>0. For example assume f is bounded, continuous and nonnegative, the real Gaussian transform is identically one, the unique zero of f is the origin, both tail lower limits are at least 1+T, and every strict early sublevel {f≤t}, 0<t<T, is an interval. R087 supplies the interval conclusion under its local birth regularity instead. Let the endpoint limits be −A,B, with A,B>0. Suppose on at least one wing f has the far-tail limit L=1+T. The other wing may have any larger lower limit. We prove exclusion if f is real analytic **in neighborhoods of the two finite points −A,B only**. The proof also permits one-sided analytic germs continued as upper bounds on the outward collars.

**Endpoint speed.** Write A(t), B(t) for the R084 boundary distances. They are uniformly Lipschitz on [T/2,T), and hence up to T by passage to the limit. In fact the Brownian surviving measure Q_t is bounded above by the killed half-line heat law started from zero at the static lower barrier −A(t), because all earlier boundaries were more restrictive. In the coordinate x=−A(t)+z, z>0, its density is ≤p_t(z−A(t))−p_t(z+A(t))≤Cz for t≥T/2. The last inequality follows by the mean value theorem in z and a uniform derivative bound on p_t. A left exit during the next h units must hit the old static barrier −A(t). The reflection principle bounds its probability by
∫₀∞ Cz·2barΦ(z/√h)dz = Ch/2.
On the other hand the exact boundary measure of such exits is ∫_{A(t)}^{A(t+h)}g(x)dx, by the R084 Brownian identification. The range of A(t) is compact, so its density lower bound gives the asserted speed estimate; reflect for B. In particular the analytic germs at the endpoints have nonzero outward derivatives: T−f(−A+v)≥c v and T−f(B−v)≥c v for small v>0, using continuity and strict increase of the early boundary levels. Flat finite critical crossings are already excluded just by the speed estimate.

**Synthetic continuation.** Both inward analytic germs have analytic inverse time branches ℓ(s),r(s) through T with ℓ′(T)<0<r′(T). On the two true early boundaries near T the spatial Gaussian source has time weights w_ℓ(s)=−g(ℓ(s))ℓ′(s) and w_r(s)=g(r(s))r′(s), both positive and analytic there. Fix t₀<T sufficiently close to T and use their real analytic extensions on [t₀,T+η]. Construct a signed family Q̃ by retaining all true sources f≤t₀ and, for later times, subtracting only these two prescribed moving sources from free heat propagation. Equivalently
Q̃_t=P_{t−t₀} Q_{t₀}−∑_{j=ℓ,r}∫_{t₀}^t p_{t−s}(·−j(s)) w_j(s)ds.
It agrees with Q_t for t₀<t<T because the two branches supply every such early source. All measures have finite variation; in this slab they have densities. Choose R larger than the orbit of both branches and the full early hull by a fixed margin. For every fixed |y|≥R, t↦Q̃_t(y) is real analytic near T with a common time radius for all such y. Here is an explicit check. Choose s₁∈(t₀,T) fixed close to T; the first past part P_{t−s₁}Q̃_{s₁} is holomorphic in a common neighborhood of T by its positive time gap. For the layer over [s₁,t] change variables u=t−s and split at a fixed d>0 less than half T−s₁. On 0<u<d use
p_u(y−j(t−u)) w_j(t−u).
For t in a small complex disk around T, the complex spatial displacement of the branch from its real range is uniformly small. Thus Re[(y−j(t−u))²]≥c>0 for all |y|≥R. The integrable bound C u^{−1/2} exp(−c/(2u)) proves holomorphy by dominated integration. The remaining integral d≤u≤t−s₁ has positive lower time bound and a variable holomorphic endpoint. Parametrize its straight path by u=d+v(t−s₁−d), 0≤v≤1; after shrinking the complex disk the integrand is holomorphic with Re u≥d/2. This gives a common radius, though bounds on this last part may depend on y. Since Q̃=Q=0 throughout that exterior at all t<T close to T, the identity theorem gives Q̃_t=0 there also for t>T sufficiently close to T. Hence Q̃_t has compact support [−R,R] and its real transform is at most C_t exp(R|λ|).

**Comparison and contradiction.** If the true f is analytic on both sides of the finite endpoints, the true post-T graph contains these two continued branches. Omitted sources, including any extra births or flat regions exactly at T outside the early interval, give only nonnegative heat laws, so Q_t≤Q̃_t in measure for t>T sufficiently close to T. If the actual outer f is only bounded above by the outward analytic continuation on each small collar, compare real transforms instead. The outward graph values satisfy f≥T there since every earlier sublevel is contained in the limiting interval. Each x on a synthetic branch with predicted level f_syn(x)≤t therefore has true T≤f(x)≤f_syn(x)≤t. Its actual deducted transform integrand exp(λx+(t−f(x))λ²/2) is at least the predicted one. The same real-transform upper bound follows even though the two time-space measures need not coincide. No assumptions on any other sources after time T are required.

For any fixed t=T+η' in the resulting slab, the exact remaining real transform is positive:
∫e^{λy}Q_t(dy)=∫_{f(x)>t}exp(λx−(f(x)−t)λ²/2)g(x)dx.
Use the sign of λ favoring the wing where f(x)→1+T. For |λ| large the interval |x−λ|≤1 satisfies f(x)>t (take η'<1/2) and f(x)≤1+T+η'/2. Completing the square gives the lower bound P(|G|≤1)exp(η'λ²/4). This contradicts C_t exp(R|λ|). Thus the two analytic critical crossings are impossible. In particular, for a nonconstant exact candidate with different finite limits both above one, early connectedness or its R087 sufficient test forces at least one of the two finite critical crossings at level min(L_−,L_+)−1 to fail this analytic continuation/comparison condition. A global analyticity assumption at unrelated points is unnecessary.

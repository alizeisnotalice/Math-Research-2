# Continuous exact nonstopping graphs with standardized spatial laws converging to the Gaussian

For arbitrarily small total-variation error there exists a centered variance-one probability law ρ, a bounded continuous graph f, and a fixed separation between f(0) and f(−1), such that the transform ∫exp(zx−z²f(x)/2)ρ(dx) is exactly one for all complex z, but the spacetime graph measure admits no bounded randomized Brownian stopping realization. The laws in the construction can be chosen to have densities. This relaxes only the exact Gaussian spatial marginal of the normalized transform problem. The graphs fail a common local modulus.

**Three shrinking intervals.** Fix a height increment A=1/2, fix L=2 and take a small v>0. At centers 0,L,2L put tents of radius d/2=vA and height A above the constant time 1. Write F for the resulting continuous graph. For t=1+u, 0<u<A, the three continuing intervals {F>t} shrink linearly, with their left endpoints on parallel tracks x=jL−vA+vu. Stop Brownian motion from zero at τ=inf{t≥0:t≥F(B_t)}. Continuity, 1≤F≤1+A and F(0)>0 give τ=F(B_τ) almost surely. Its position law ν has the exact exponential transform by optional sampling. At time 1 all paths outside the three open base intervals stop; every other path stops in its original closed interval. In particular ν equals γ off their union (endpoints have zero mass), and ν assigns the union exactly its Gaussian mass m_v=O(v). The closing time produces no atom at a tip since B_{1+A} has a density. For a null set on either sloping side its inverse time image is null and the exit law is absolutely continuous in time: hitting a particular moving side there is dominated by the unrestricted first-passage law to its straight boundary for Brownian initial points in the given interval. The latter has a density by the Brownian reflection formula with constant drift, or by finite-horizon absolute continuity to driftless Brownian motion in the moving frame. This proves absolute continuity of ν.

**Room for a signed perturbation.** Choose the fixed window A/4<u<A/2. The spatial law ν on each left track, parameterized by u, dominates c_v du for some c_v>0 throughout that window. In the coordinate y measured from its moving left endpoint, Brownian motion has drift −v. Choose 0<ℓ<vA and compare with killing on the smaller fixed strip 0<y<ℓ for 0<u<A/2; the actual right endpoint is at distance 2v(A−u)≥vA. At u=0 the initial density dominates a positive multiple of exp(−vy)sin(πy/ℓ) in that strip. Its killed evolution is this same function times exp(−(v²+π²/ℓ²)u/2), by direct differentiation of the forward equation and zero boundary data. The positive outward flux at zero, one half its inward derivative, gives the required lower bound. Each left exit of this subpopulation before its right exit is an actual stop at that left boundary. The constants may become very small with v.

Use the nonzero signed kernel η of round 173 on these three subsegments, with compact time support strictly inside the window. Its three bounded time densities and the preceding domination allow any sufficiently small nonzero real multiple ε_vη to be added to ν while retaining positivity. The zero transform includes zero total mass and zero first spatial moment. The resulting probability ρ₀≠ν has the same transform and still coincides with γ outside the base intervals, assigning them mass m_v. Thus its total variation distance from γ (supremum over events) is ≤m_v.

**Why no other stopping realization exists.** For any continuous bounded clock F, a bounded stopping time S on its graph must equal the first hit τ, including under independent initial randomization. Path continuity gives τ≤S. For each rational t, P(S>t,F(B_t)<t)=0: given such a live position, Brownian motion has strictly positive probability to remain up to the common bound inside an interval where F<t, which precludes a future graph stop (use countably many rational intervals). If τ<S, Brownian motion returns to B_τ arbitrarily soon strictly after τ, by strong Markov and one-dimensional recurrence at zero on every initial interval. Some such return precedes S and has F(B_t)=τ<t; continuity gives a rational witnessing the prohibited event. Hence τ=S. As ρ₀ differs from ν, it cannot be a graph stop. Its signed heat remainder must be negative on some test by round 3's exact positivity–realization equivalence.

**Normalization and fixed separation.** Write b=∫x²dρ₀=∫F dρ₀ (by the transform at order two). Then 1≤b≤1+A m_v, and the first moment is zero. Send x to x/√b, t to t/b and use f(x)=F(√b x)/b with input law ρ the scaled ρ₀. This preserves the exact transform and nonrealizability by Brownian scaling. The scaled γ converges in total variation to γ by its explicit density; hence so does ρ. The variance is one and 0≤f≤3/2. For v small, f(0)−f(−1)=A/b≥1/3. Points within O(v) of zero outside the central tent differ from its tip by the same amount. Consequently there is no common modulus on a neighborhood of zero. This construction does not yield an exact continuous graph with input γ.

# Directed enthalpy comparison with the continuing canonical Gaussian boundary

Assume the R084 Brownian early band to T>0. By R088 the early interval is [−b(t),b(t)] and agrees with the canonical Gaussian Brownian first-exit interval; write b=b(T)>0, R(t) for its later radius and F_* for its spatial inverse. Only a fixed short canonical continuation is used, as in R094. Assume the tail limit f(−r)→L=1+T and liminf f(r)>L on the right. The low wing may be nonmonotone, may approach its limit from either side at any speed, and may have simultaneous or subsequent births. On the high wing suppose that for every t in a small slab [T,T+h₀] each inactive high point y∈(b,R(t)) with f(y)>t lies outside the entire rightmost true past, so that Q_s vanishes there for T<s≤t. A sufficient assumption is that the right sublevels are intervals on [0,∞) through this slab: the high right-tail gap above 1+t makes the one-sided R083 resolvent support applicable. Membership at endpoints need only hold almost everywhere in space-time. We use cumulative source indicators, so no moving-boundary smoothness on the high side is needed.

For any fixed sufficiently small 0<h<h₀, take q=λ²/2 and σ(x)=f(x)−T, h_*(r)=F_*(r)−T, Δ(r)=(f(−r)−F_*(r))_+. Let I_far be the whole low-wing quantity ∫_{r≥R₀} exp(λr−q(f(−r)−T)) g(r)dr for a fixed large R₀ on which f(−r)>T+h₀ (shrink h₀<1/2). Then
(1−e^{−qh}) I_far(λ) ≤ O(qe^{−qh+Cλ})
 +∫_b^{R(T+h)} exp(λr−q h_*(r)) [1−exp(−q Δ(r))]g(r)dr.              (1)
There is no additive term on a merely linear exponential scale from the opposite side.

**Proof.** Write V(t)=∫_T^t Q_s ds, V_*(t)=∫_T^t Q_s^* ds and W=V−V_*. Use the left weak value at T as common initial reference if there are new time-T sources; their contribution is counted among the new source indicators from the start. Both initial signed heat laws before these births are the same. The bounded L² forcing in (∂_t−∂²/2)W is g(I_*−I), where I,I_* are the spatial indicators of the post-T sources already activated by t, counting new time-T points and excluding the identical early open core. The sign is unchanged by any common endpoint convention. Let E_s=g1_{y<0}(I_*−I)_+, Z(t)=∫_T^t P_{t−s} E_s ds. The forcing of W−Z is nonpositive on the low side. On the high side any positive forcing requires I_*=1 and I=0, and there the true V vanishes by the exterior hypothesis while V_*≥0 and Z≥0. Hence (W−Z)_+ times the forcing is nonpositive almost everywhere. The standard positive-part L² energy identity yields W≤Z (prove the identity by a smooth convex approximation of the squared positive part for the mild heat equation). In particular V≤V_*+Z.

Take the transform with e^{−λy} at T+h. The canonical integrated survivor is bounded in magnitude by C exp(Cλ) by compact support. For Z the semigroup contributes exp(q(T+h−s)); multiplying by q exp(−qh) and integrating in s gives precisely the collar bound in (1), possibly smaller since the delay is cut off at time T+h. On the true side all future-transform integrands are nonnegative. The far low sources are in the future at every s∈[T,T+h], hence their integrated contribution is (exp(qh)−1)I_far/q. This proves (1).

**Consequences.** In particular Δ must be positive arbitrarily close to b even if f(−r) approaches L from above more slowly than every inverse power. Indeed after writing the far integral as ∫p₁(r−λ)exp(q[L−f(−r)])dr, the window |r−λ|≤1 supplies exp(−o(λ²)), larger than the first error in (1). If Δ vanishes on a collar, take a smaller fixed h so R(T+h) lies inside it.

If f(−r)≤L−D/r+o(1/r) for a real D of either sign, the same restricted window gives a lower logarithmic rate D/2 on the λ scale. If k<liminf_{r↓b}h_*(r)log(1/Δ(r)), then Δ≤exp(−k/h_*) on a sufficiently small collar. Bound 1−exp(−qΔ)≤qΔ, use q h_*+k/h_*≥√(2k)λ, and send h↓0. For nonnegative liminf this yields
√(2 liminf h_* log(1/Δ))+D/2≤b.
The case of zero liminf follows directly by dropping the bracket. This extends the finite linear rate to arbitrarily negative D because (1) avoids an opposite-wing exp(−bλ) error.

Suppose instead f(−r)≤L+B/r^p+o(r^{−p}) with B>0, 0<p<1. The far lower log rate on scale λ^{2−p} is at least −B/2 by the same fixed window. A bound Δ≤exp(−k/h_*^γ), k,γ>0, gives an upper log bound (b+o_h(1))λ−C_γ k^{1/(γ+1)} q^{γ/(γ+1)}+O(log q), where C_γ=(γ+1)γ^{−γ/(γ+1)}, by minimizing qv+k/v^γ. Consequently γ>(2−p)/p is impossible, and at γ=(2−p)/p it is necessary that
k≤B^{1+γ} γ^γ/[2(1+γ)^{1+γ}].
This conclusion uses no rate or regularity of the high tail beyond its strict lower limit and the stated local exterior condition.

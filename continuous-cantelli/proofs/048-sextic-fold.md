# A sextic jet with a sectorially flat perturbation

Let x₀∈ℝ, c>0. Suppose v(x₀+u)=cu⁶H(u)² near zero, H real and positive, H(0)=1. On either real ray require that H extend holomorphically to a punctured sector about that ray, and that H−1=O_J(|u|^J) there for every J≥1 on each closed subsector. The two extensions need not agree. The half opening on each side is assumed greater than θ₀=½arcsin(2/3) (about 0.365 radians). Under these hypotheses the output cannot have a Gaussian law. No bounds are needed away from the fixed input core. This covers e.g. H=1+exp[−d/|u|^γ] for every d>0 and 0<γ≤4, since θ₀<π/8; γ>4 is covered by the earlier supercritical real-error theorem. It also covers H=1+exp[−d(log(1/|u|))^p] for every d>0,p>1, since Re[(log(1/u))^p]∼(log(1/|u|))^p on any fixed narrow sector, with separate branches on the two sides. A variance cu⁶+exp[−d/|u|^γ] also meets these assumptions by dividing by cu⁶ and taking the positive local analytic square root.

**Set-up.** The law from a fixed input core |u|<r would have an analytic density at the translated y=0 by exterior localization: source means away from y=0 give locally bounded holomorphic Gaussian kernels by distance; zero exterior variances give no local mass. The local analytic density would imply an analytic cumulative mass increment from 0 to w. Condition on b∼N(0,c) in w=u+b u³H(u), which realizes the desired variance also for u<0 by symmetry. Write g(u)=p₁(x₀+u). On sufficiently small real intervals all fixed derivatives of H−1 are flat by Cauchy on disks with radius proportional to |u|. In particular 3H+uH'>0 and u²(3H+uH') is strictly increasing in |u| along both rays after decreasing r. All local critical points and real branches discussed below are therefore the only ones in the core for large b and suitably small scaled w.

**Large negative b and the two outer branches.** Put e=|b|^{-1/2}, U=u/e, W=w/e. Then W=F_e(U)=U−U³H(eU), F₀(U)=U−U³. Write s=1/√3 and ρ=2/(3√3). For small |W| the outer positive branch starts at the unique positive nonzero root of F_e, close to 1. In the unperturbed case it is holomorphic for |W|<ρ, and through the circle |W|=ρ except at W=ρ. Indeed implicit continuation cannot encounter a cubic critical value except ±ρ. On Re U=s one has Re F₀(U)=ρ+3s(Im U)², so this branch remains in Re U>s on the open disk. At W=−ρ its root is on the positive real axis strictly larger than 1 and is simple. The closure of its image satisfies |1−U²|≤ρ/|U|≤2/3 and hence |arg U|≤θ₀; it stays bounded and away from 0.

Here are details furnishing a uniform cut disk for perturbations, to avoid presuming global holomorphy in U. Choose a small disk D centered at W=ρ. On the closed disk |W|≤ρ minus a smaller concentric disk at ρ, the positive outer branch extends to an open neighborhood and has simple roots. This follows by compactness, uniqueness and the implicit function theorem; at W=−ρ this chosen root is simple. Its image stays in a compact subset of the assumed open sector and is bounded away from zero, including a small neighborhood of the corresponding root U=s. Thus the simple branch, as well as a small fixed disk about s, is covered by compact sets on which F_e→F₀ with every fixed derivative, with O_J(e^J) error. Enlarging the W disk by a sufficiently small fixed η>0 stays in these neighborhoods outside D. Uniform Rouché on small root circles, patched on overlaps by uniqueness, gives a single perturbed outer branch there. Near W=ρ the implicit theorem for the simple zero of F'_e provides a real h_e=s+O_J(e^J), with simple maximum ρ_e=F_e(h_e)=ρ+O_J(e^J). In a fixed disk about h_e write F_e(U)=ρ_e−(U−h_e)²D_e(U), D_e(h_e)→3s>0. The inverse of the coordinate (U−h_e)√D_e(U) exists on a fixed disk with uniformly bounded derivatives. The branch with larger real root is obtained by taking the positive square root √(ρ_e−W); it agrees with the simple continuation, and its domain is the disk |W|<ρ_e+η' cut along the real ray from ρ_e to the positive boundary. The overlap with the outer patch may be taken to be a connected crescent by choosing η' small relative to the radius of the fold disk. This construction uses only the sector near the positive outer root. The negative outer root is reduced to the identical positive-root construction by reflecting u,w; its signed Nth coefficient picks up (−1)^N.

On the positive outer branch the conditional real density as a function of W, in physical w-density units, is −g(eU)dU/dW, because du/dw=dU/dW and F'_e<0. It has the uniform singular expansion
K_e(W)=L_e(1−W/ρ_e)^−1/2+O(1),   L_e=g(e h_e)/(2√(ρ_e D_e(h_e)))→g(0)/(2√(3sρ))>0.
The O(1) term is bounded in the slit disk near the fold by the square-root coordinate, and is bounded on compact portions away from it by simple continuation. Therefore
[W^N]K_e=ρ_e^{−N}(L_e C_N+O(1/N)),   C_N=4^{−N}binom(2N,N),
uniformly for 0<e<e₀. For completeness, subtract the displayed inverse square root and integrate the Cauchy formula around the fixed-fraction larger circle with a keyhole at ρ_e. The outside contour gives exponentially small error after normalization; on the straight cut lips, the bounded remainder and ∫_{ρ_e}^{ρ_e+η'} x^{−N−1}dx≤ρ_e^{−N}/N suffice, and a small indentation of radius ρ_e/N about the branchpoint contributes the same order. Since C_N∼1/√(π N), for all large even N both outer conditional coefficients, after restoring w=eW, are strictly positive for every large negative b.

**The central branch.** For each fixed b its C∞ germ at zero has exactly the pure sextic inverse jet (implicit differentiation and H−1 flat). Its formal Nth density coefficient is
a_N(b)=[u^N]g(u)(1+bu²)^{−N−1}.
This follows by a change-of-contour residue for the analytic pure central branch w=u+bu³. When N=4l, the leading term is g(0)binom(3N/2,N/2)|b|^{N/2}. Dividing by this binomial times (−b)^{N/2}, the remaining even source coefficients g_{2j} have multipliers b^{−j} up to sign times (N/2)_j/(3N/2)_j≤3^{−j}. The unit-disk Cauchy bound on g therefore bounds the normalized remainder by C/|b| for all |b|≥B. Choose B sufficiently large so every central coefficient for these b and N has a common positive lower bound in this normalization. For b>0 there are no outer real branches near zero.

**Both cutoffs and integration of asymptotic coefficients.** Work with the cumulative increment rather than its derivative so that the tail in b needs only the trivial uniform bound on a probability mass. For |b|≤B take a smaller near core where all conditional maps have derivative ≥1/2; the central branch contributes, to every finite order in the increment, exactly the (integrated) pure analytic central coefficients. Uniformity follows from the real bounds on H−1 and its first derivative or directly by comparing the inverse root with that for u+bu³. The true annulus away from 0 gives an analytic increment for |b|≤B: after b integration its w-density is the integral in u of the real analytic integrand g(u)ρ_c((w−u)/(u³H(u)))/|u³H(u)| cut at |(w−u)/(u³H(u))|≤B. At w=0 its crossing locations satisfy u²H(u)=1/B, with a simple crossing on each of the two rays. Choose the outer and inner spatial endpoints off equality, with the crossing interior when relevant (enlarge B and then shrink the inner cutoff). Compactness and the implicit theorem give analytic varying endpoints. All bounded-b terms have at most geometric asymptotic coefficients.

For B<|b|≤|w|^{−1/2}, |W|=√|b|·|w|≤|w|^{3/4}. The two possible outer branches are uniformly holomorphic on |W|≤ρ/2, so their cumulative contributions (integrated from 0 to w, in physical units) have degree-(N+1) remainders bounded by C_N |b|^{(N+1)/2}|w|^{N+2}, using uniform Cauchy estimates on a smaller disk if necessary. The central branch output increment is ∫₀^{u_b(w)}g(u)du there. Its inverse root differs from the pure root by at most C_J |b||w|^{J+3} since both roots are O(|w|), the pure derivative is ≥1/2 in this range, and |H−1|≤C_J|u|^J. The pure central branch enjoys the same Taylor remainder estimate by its analytic scaled inverse around zero. Full Gaussian b moments integrate each such remainder. The true increment on |b|>|w|^{-1/2} is dominated in integral by the Gaussian tail probability; it is negligible to every finite order. Likewise the finitely many Taylor coefficients in the discarded b tail have at most a fixed polynomial in |b| and are negligible. This justifies termwise identification of real asymptotic coefficients of the full cumulative increment. Its (N+1)st coefficient equals the b-integral of the conditional Nth density coefficient divided by N+1.

Finally for N=4l large all the negative-b outer coefficients and both-sign large-b central coefficients are positive. Already the latter, integrated, contribute ≥c₀ binom(3N/2,N/2) c^{N/4} E|Z|^{N/2}/(N+1) up to a geometric error from |b|≤B. Its (N+1)st root diverges. Bounded-b and spatial exterior analytic increments have only geometrically growing coefficients, so no exact Gaussian-output graph has this sextic contact.

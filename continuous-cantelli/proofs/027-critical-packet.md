# A neighboring-frequency Gaussian packet at the critical exponential-flat scale

Let the analytic real reference V(u)=cuⁿH(u), c>0, n≥4 even, H(0)=1, be nonnegative on a sufficiently small core (−r,r). Let actual v(x₀+u) be continuous nonnegative and |v(x₀+u)−V(u)|≤C exp(−d/|u|ᵏ), k=n−2. Write N=n−1, θ=π/(2N), b=sinθ, g=cosθ, A=N b/n, B=N g/n. A normalized critical coefficient is D*=t^{2N}/k, where t=(kb/2)^{1/n}, and d*=2D*/(nc).

**Theorem.** For each n as above there is ε_n>0 such that all d≥(1−ε_n)d* force incompatibility with exact Gaussian output. The amount ε_n is existential. The normalizations below show it can be chosen independently of c,x₀,H and C. Only the local radius and sufficiently large frequencies may depend on them.

Set ξ>0, a=(ncξ/2)^{−1/N}, λ=ξa=2/(nc aᵏ), D=ncd/2 and w*= (k/n)i e^{iθ}=−(k/n)b+i(k/n)g. Write q=−Re w*>0. For a source-restricted output measure μ on |u|<r use its centered transform T(s)=∫ e^{isu−s²v(x₀+u)/2}ρ(x₀+u)du, ρ=p₁. For sufficiently small fixed δ>0 test by the packet
P(T)=(2πδλ)^−½ ∫ℝ exp[−τ²/(2δλ)−w*τ] T(ξ+τ/a)dτ.
For an exact graph the localization lemma gives |T(s)|≤C exp(−δ₀|s|). In the interval |τ|≤λ/2 this bounds |P(T)| by C exp[−δ₀ ξ/2+q²δλ/2]. Outside this interval the unweighted transform is bounded by 1 and the tilted normalized Gaussian tail is ≤2 exp[q²δλ/2−(1/2−qδ)²λ/(2δ)]. Thus for suitably small δ, |P(T)|=O(exp[−(A+2)λ]). Actual/ref model core transforms need only have bounded mass to get the same exterior-frequency bound.

**Removing the large real-input region.** Shrink r so both v and V ≥c|u|ⁿ/2 in the core. Take fixed L large; for L<|z|<r/a, z=u/a, |τ|≤λ/2 and s=ξ+τ/a≥ξ/2, both kernels have modulus ≤exp[−λ Lⁿ/(8n)]. The normalized packet absolute weight integrates to exp[q²δλ/2]. This region contributes O(exp[−(A+2)λ]) for L chosen large and δ sufficiently small. The same bound on |τ|>λ/2 applies globally. Hence only |z|≤L remains; do not truncate the τ integral there.

**Exact integration in τ.** For deterministic variance s at input u=az put Y=ξ²s/(2λ). For |z|≤L, the averaged single-source kernel is exactly
K(z,Y)=(1+2δY)^−½ exp{λ Gδ(z,Y)},
Gδ(z,Y)=iz−Y+δ(iz−2Y−w*)²/[2(1+2δY)].
Indeed ξ+τ/a=ξ(1+τ/λ) and the normalized Gaussian integral completes a square. For real Y≥0 this is valid for every δ>0. For bounded complex z,Y it extends holomorphically for δ sufficiently small using the root continued from δ=0. On bounded real z,Y ranges, differentiating gives |∂_Y K|≤C(λ+1)exp[λ Re Gδ(z,Y)].

**Actual-model error.** For each real |z|≤L, write Y between ξ²v(x₀+az)/(2λ) and ξ²V(az)/(2λ). Uniformly on this bounded range, Y=zⁿ/n+o(1) as a↓0 (use H(az)→1 and an exponential amplitude times a^−n). Therefore the difference in the packet over this input range is bounded by C a ξ² times the integral of
exp{ λ[−D/|z|ᵏ + Re Gδ(z,zⁿ/n) + o(1)]}dz.
The point z=0 gives zero error. At D=D*, δ=0 the real exponent is −D*/|z|ᵏ−zⁿ/n, with exactly two global maxima ±t, value −A. At either such point, 2tⁿ/n=kb/n=q and
Re[ (i(±t)−2tⁿ/n−w*)²/2 ]=−(±t−kg/n)²/2 <0.
Strictness follows from tⁿ=kb/2≥k/(2N)≥1/3 and (k/n)^n<e^{−2}<1/4. On compact neighborhoods of ±t the partial derivative of the exponent with respect to δ remains strictly negative for 0≤δ≤δ₁. On the remaining compact set away from zero and these maxima there is a uniform gap at zero filter width that persists for small δ. Near z=0 the term −D/|z|ᵏ ensures a gap uniformly for all D≥D*/2 (the packet correction is bounded). Consequently for suitably small fixed δ there is η>0 such that the whole |z|≤L error exponent is ≤−A−η. Continuity on the compact set away from 0 preserves this bound with η/2 for D in [D*(1−ε_n),D*], ε_n>0. Larger D decrease the error. Polynomial prefactors and the uniform o(1) cost a further arbitrarily small constant in this exponential rate.

**Analytic model.** On fixed |Re z|≤L, 0≤Im z≤2 insert Y_a(z)=zⁿH(az)/n. Write F_{δ,a}(z)=Gδ(z,Y_a(z)). All needed derivatives converge uniformly to their values at a=0. The unfiltered F_{0,0} on Im z=b has exactly two isolated maxima z₊=g+ib and z₋=−g+ib, which are complex stationary points with Re F''<0 and Re F=−A there. On this line set w(z)=iz−2zⁿ/n. At z₊, w(z₊)=w*, so F_{δ,0}(z₊)=−A+iB AND F'_{δ,0}(z₊)=0 for every small δ; its quadratic real curvature stays strictly negative. At z₋, w(z₋)−w*=−2i kg/n, and ∂δ Re F_{δ,0}(z₋)|δ=0=−2(kg/n)²<0. The strict negative δ derivative extends over a fixed neighborhood of z₋. Off fixed neighborhoods of z_± the initial phase gap persists on the bounded horizontal contour for small δ. The verticals over ±L have a strict gap along heights [0,b] if L was chosen sufficiently large: n even and Re[(±L+iy)^n] ≥ Lⁿ/2 for L sufficiently large uniformly in 0≤y≤b. Thus for small δ the shifted contour has the sole dominant maximum at z₊. These choices only involve n,L. At fixed small δ and small a, introduce the branch z₊(a) solving F'_{δ,a}=0 by the implicit function theorem. The horizontal line over a fixed neighborhood of g can be shifted to Im z₊(a) with short connectors in the strict gap. The other contour pieces all have Re F≤−A−η' for some η'>0, while Re F_{δ,a}(z₊(a))→−A. On the moving neighborhood the Gaussian saddle lemma, with horizontal quadratic domination and bounded third derivatives, yields
a λ^−½ e^{λ F_{δ,a}(z₊(a))} [√(2π)ρ(x₀+az₊(a))/√{(1+2δY_a(z₊(a)))(−F''_{δ,a}(z₊(a)))}+o(1)].
Square roots are assigned continuously by the actual Gaussian integral and the horizontal saddle. The coefficient has a nonzero limit. Thus the reference packet has modulus a λ^−½ exp[(−A+o(1))λ] times a factor bounded away from zero, with no subsequence needed. The exact graph's exponentially smaller packet plus the exponentially smaller actual-reference error contradict this.

This proof does not assume analyticity or regularity for the actual perturbation beyond continuity (measurable perturbations with the core localization hypotheses also work). The initial analytic-strip argument uses actual variances: away from the central zero on a compact subannulus they are uniformly positive by comparability. The method supplies a strict improvement on the direct absolute-error coefficient but does not find the optimal critical coefficient.

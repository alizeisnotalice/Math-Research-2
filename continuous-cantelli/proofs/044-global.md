# Gaussian transform at a finite set of parameters and complete derivative matching up to a constant

Fix b>0, and any finite set of real parameters. There is an even bounded smooth nonnegative variance v, equal to b outside a compact set and positive except for a quartic zero at zero, such that for independent standard normal X,Z the random variable Y=X+√v(X)Z has variance 1+b and E exp(tY)=exp((1+b)t²/2) at every prescribed t. Its smooth density D satisfies D^(n)(0)=p_(1+b)^(n)(0) for every n≥1, but D(0)>p_(1+b)(0). The function D−p_(1+b)−[D(0)−p_(1+b)(0)] is flat at zero and nonzero on every one-sided neighborhood. In the normalized formulation f=1+b−v, the construction may be taken to have f≥0 and E f=1.

## Finite transform constraints

By symmetry retain just the distinct positive absolute parameters t_1,...,t_m. The zero test is automatic. Write g=p_1. Start at v=b, and use disjoint fixed even smooth bumps ψ_j supported around ±x_j in 1<|x|<2 for j=0,...,m. In the exterior set v=b+Ση_jψ_j. The map from η to the changes of E v and of the m real output transforms has derivative at zero equal, up to nonzero row factors, to the matrix with entries ∫g ψ_j cosh(t_i x)dx, including t_0=0 for the first row. The functions 1,cosh(t_i x) are independent on (1,2): analytic continuation and their first m+1 even Taylor coefficients give a Vandermonde matrix in t_i². Hence evaluation vectors span, distinct suitable nodes exist, and by normalizing ∫gψ_j=1 and shrinking their supports the derivative is invertible. The finite-dimensional inverse function theorem gives a continuous local inverse on a full neighborhood of the zero residual.

Take R>0 small and use v=u⁴w(u) on |u|≤R, interpolating smoothly to b only on R<|u|<R+R⁵ by a fixed convex cutoff between b and c u⁴, c=w(0). The realization below has w=c for |u|≥R/2 on the core. Uniformly for c∈[1/24,1/8] and c/2≤w≤3c/2, the changes in all finite constraints due to this replacement are O(R), because the kernels on this bounded source set and bounded variance range are bounded. Apply the exterior inverse to the negative residual. This enforces every constraint, with |η|=O(R), continuously in w in the uniform topology. Choose R small enough for v≤b+1 and v>0 outside zero, uniformly for this parameter family. Thus every graph in what follows already has all required real-parameter identities and the required variance.

## The second derivative

The local output density D_core defined by |u|≤R is smooth near zero by the Fourier argument in round 43. In distributions S_R''=D_core−g there. With S_R's order-four coefficient written as I_4+g(0)c/2, round 43 gives
    D_core''(0)=g''(0)+12g(0)c+24I_4.
The off-diagonal coefficient I_4 is uniformly exponentially small as R↓0 by the off-diagonal Gaussian estimate, regardless of the derivatives of w. Away from this core compare with the baseline v=b. Removing the baseline on |u|≤R costs O(R) in its second density derivative. The transition layer costs O(R²) since sup_(s>0)|p_s''(u)|≤C|u|⁻³ and its total length is 2R⁵. The fixed exterior bumps cost O(R) because |η|=O(R) and the variance remains bounded away from zero there. Consequently
    D''(0)−p_(1+b)''(0)=g(0)(12c−1)+E,    |E|≤C R.
E depends continuously on all coordinates for fixed R. These are ordinary density derivatives of smooth representatives. Directly substituting y=0 into the positive-variance integrand alone would omit the diagonal g(0) term.

## Joint infinite-dimensional fixed point

Use the even cutoff realization of round 43, w(u)=c+Σ a_k u^kχ(u/ε_k) over even k≥2, with coordinate intervals |a_k|≤M_k. After M_k is chosen take ε_k<R/2 small to bound the sum of the zeroth-order perturbations by 1/48 and ensure uniform convergence of every derivative as in that round. Thus c/2≤w≤3c/2. For fixed R all exterior density coefficients are bounded at each order, uniformly over the box. Indeed outside |u|≤R the variances have a uniform positive lower bound for this fixed R, including on the transition. They are continuous in the product topology by dominated convergence. For the core the true order k+4 coefficient of S_R is g(0)a_k/2 plus a polynomial in earlier coordinates and the bounded continuous off-diagonal term I_(k+4). Since D_core=g+S_R'', the order k+2 coefficient of the full density is affine in this displayed diagonal term with nonzero multiplier (k+4)(k+3). The off-diagonal and exterior terms can still depend on every coordinate. Set the new a_k equal to the value obtained by subtracting all these terms from the target density coefficient and dividing by (k+4)(k+3)g(0)/2. Uniform bounds and the finite bounds on earlier coordinates give a finite M_k large enough for the resulting self-map, before ε_k is chosen.

Update c to 1/12−E/(12g(0)). The uniform error bound keeps this in [1/24,1/8] for sufficiently small R, independent of all M_k. These simultaneous updates give a coordinatewise continuous self-map of the compact product of intervals. Finite Brouwer fixed points with the remaining coordinates fixed at zero, followed by diagonal subsequence extraction, give a common fixed point. The second derivative and every even derivative of order ≥4 are now exactly as prescribed. All odd derivatives vanish by symmetry.

## The discrepancy persists in the actual densities

Round 43 gives D_core(0)=g(0)+∫_(|u|≤R)g(u)p_(v(u))(−u)du, by the order-two coefficient of S_R; the input zero has measure zero. The exterior adds positive mass density. Thus D(0)>g(0)>p_(1+b)(0). For y≠0, complexify the heat kernel in a small disc: inputs close to the unique zero are separated from that disc, and all remaining inputs have variance bounded below. This gives real analyticity on each half-axis. If the density difference equaled its positive constant Taylor series on any one-sided neighborhood, it would equal this constant on the whole half-axis, contrary to integrability. The matched transforms form a finite set chosen before constructing v. The resulting law is demonstrably non-Gaussian.

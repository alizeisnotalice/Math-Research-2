# The ordered squared-gap estimate in the presence of accumulation points

Let A={H−f=0} be any nonempty closed maximum set. Denote its derived set by P (its non-isolated points). For any b∈A define C_b as the supremum of positive c for which v^(-1/2)exp(c/v) is integrable on some neighborhood of b, interpreting the integrand as +∞ on v=0; null zero sets do not affect Lebesgue integration. The empty supremum is 0. This gives the round-179 definition at isolated points. The function C is lower semicontinuous on A: integrability on a ball also holds on a smaller ball near each sufficiently nearby point. Use K_b=π²/(8L_b²) at isolated points with L_b as in round 179. At b∈P set the terminal bound K_b=C_b.

For any a,b∈A let D(a,b) be one half of the sum of squared lengths of the complementary gaps of A between them. When A is unbounded on a wing define D(a,±∞) by the sum over gaps on that wing; otherwise assign infinite cost in that direction. Then for each isolated a,
$$
 C_a\ge\min\big(\inf_{b\in A}[D(a,b)+K_b],\,D(a,-\infty),\,D(a,+\infty)\big).
$$
The bounds K_b on P can always be replaced by zero, so the result remains informative even when nothing is known about integrability at a cluster. For a sequence a_n increasing to a finite cluster b and with no intervening maxima, the cost from a_n to b is precisely half the sum of the remaining squared spacings, not half the square of the whole distance.

**Proof.** The local inequality of round 179 remains valid at an isolated a with the infimum over all other b∈A. In its complex displacement argument the compact range away from a can now contain infinitely many zeros. For each such b with |b−a|≤d, the strict inequality d²/2<C_b+|a−b|²/2 gives a positive exponent c_b<C_b which strictly dominates the nearby numerator (d²−|x−a|²)/2. This supplies an integrable majorant in a neighborhood of b even if there are additional zeros there: the zero set has measure zero on that neighborhood by definition of C_b>0. For |b−a|>d take a neighborhood where the numerator is strictly negative; the kernels there are bounded even at v=0. Compactness gives a finite subcover, allowing exactly the same uniform estimate and Fatou limit as before. The rest of the compact region has v bounded away from zero. No integrability assumption on an entire infinite set has been used implicitly.

Start at a point of finite C_a below its local cap. The infimum of C_b+|a−b|²/2 is attained on A excluding a by lower semicontinuity, isolation of a and boundedness of minimizing positions. Go to such a b with C_b+|a−b|²/2≤C_a. At a cluster point stop. Otherwise continue until a cap is reached or the chain is infinite. The C values decrease strictly. Any finite path pays at least D for the gaps it crosses. For an infinite path with a bounded subsequence, choose a subsequential limit b. It is in P since the vertices are distinct. Lower semicontinuity gives C_b≤lim C along the path. Its total cost is at least D(a,b): each finite collection of gaps strictly before b is crossed, and a gap ending at b is either crossed or approached by steps crossing its full length in the limit. Equivalently D(a,b_j)→D(a,b) for b_j→b in A since the intervening squared-gap sum is bounded by |b_j−b|²/2. Summing the descent inequalities proves the target estimate in this case as well. Unbounded escape supplies the corresponding infinite-wing gap sum. The bound makes no claim that capacity at an uncontrolled accumulation point is positive.

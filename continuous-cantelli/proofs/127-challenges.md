# Two fixed continuous challenge families and a remote holdout that separates them

Both examples below can satisfy the first four Gaussian moment equations exactly. They illustrate the two extremum configurations requested in §10. They fail the full transform by a tail estimate which applies to every graph with the stated sparsity and surplus bounds.

**A test with explicit asymptotic margin.** Suppose f=1+h≥δ>0, with h bounded. Off finitely many compact pieces, suppose the set h<0 is contained in intervals at radii n or n+1/2 of total length at most C exp(−n⁴) on each wing. Then, as |z|→∞, the positive part of the transform difference from one is at most exp(−δ z²/2+O(1+|z|^{4/3})). Indeed its integral against γ_z is bounded by ∫_{h<0}exp(zx−z²f/2)dγ, and the compact part costs exp(O(|z|)), the other part at most C Σ exp((n+1)|z|−n⁴). Use n|z|−n⁴/2≤C'|z|^{4/3} and sum exp(−n⁴/2). On the other hand, if on a set of Lebesgue length at least c₀ in [z−1,z+1] one has h≥c₁/(1+|z|)^3, the negative part has magnitude at least c₂/(1+|z|) for large |z|. This follows from 1−exp(−v)≥min(v/2,1/2) for v≥0 and the lower bound of the translated Gaussian density on that unit window. Consequently the transform is strictly below its required value on either wing satisfying the surplus condition. This argument uses the sign budget of round 1 and an explicit estimate on sparse exceptional intervals.

**Profiles before moment adjustment.** Use the radial coordinate r=|x|. Fix a sufficiently large integer N≥20. Set a continuous baseline ψ(r)=χ(r)/(8(1+r)^3), where χ=0 on [0,N], χ=1 on [N+1,∞), linear between. For every integer n≥2N place disjoint tents of radii exp(−n⁴)/8 at n and n+1/2, interpolating from the baseline to their target heights by the convex combination (1−tent)ψ+tent·target, with tent=(1−distance/radius)_+.
* In the escaping example take the two targets +(1−1/n) and −(1−1/n), respectively. Its supremum and infimum are approached in both tails and attained at no finite point.
* In the attained example halve both targets and add a separate bump around r=N−2. With u=r−(N−2), take the bump to be θ(u)(1−exp(−1/u²)), with the value 1 at u=0; θ is an even cutoff equal to one on |u|≤1/4 and zero on |u|≥1/2, linear in between. Its global maximum is attained and the deficit there equals exp(−1/u²) on a neighborhood. No fixed positive distance power is comparable to this deficit. Both examples have infinitely many tail extrema.

**Exact low moments.** Here is a constructive way to adjust either profile without changing its two stated extremum properties. Write P for the profile and take two fixed radial tents supported respectively on [0,1/2] and [2,5/2], positive in the interiors. Their two moment columns (E tent,E X² tent) are independent by their separated squared radii. Add a linear combination to P to obtain P₀ with E P₀=E X²P₀=0. Since P is uniformly bounded and supported on |x|≥N−5/2, both adjustment coefficients tend to zero as N→∞. Fix the first integer N≥20 for which the adjustment in sup norm is <1/8. Take V in the span of the same tents with EV=0, E X²V=1. For e>0 small put P_e=P₀+e V, α=2e/E P_e², and f=1+α P_e. Take e=2^(−j) at the first j≥1 for which e‖V‖∞<1/8 and α‖P_e‖∞<1/2. These choices exist since E P₀²>0. Thus f≥1/2, Ef=1, E Xf=0 by evenness, and E X²(f−1)=E(f−1)²/2. These are precisely the nontrivial conditions in the first four transform derivatives. Compact adjustment on |x|≤5/2 neither changes the true extremum(s) nor removes the remote positive baseline. The explicit large-tilt holdout above therefore excludes both. They are diagnostics for proofs requiring power contact, finite extrema or tail limits, not examples of exact continuous counterexamples.

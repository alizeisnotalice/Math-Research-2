# Relative perturbations at one point of the Cantor minimum set

Under the finite SSC hypotheses of r075-annular-unequal.md (equal ratios included), it already suffices that at **one** point x_* of E,
v(x)=[c+o(1)] dist(x,E)^α,  x→x_*, x∉E,   c>0, 0<α<2,
and v is continuous with v=0 on E near x_*. No derivative estimates on the coefficient and no rate of convergence are needed. This excludes the circle stationary graph as well as exact Gaussian output on the line.

Normalize the original similarity hull to unit length; a fixed initial spatial scaling only changes constants. Pick a cylinder E_u in an arbitrarily small given neighborhood of x_*, with contraction r_u arbitrarily small. Its distance from all other cylinders outside its word is ≥c₀r_u, by maximal common prefix and SSC. If circle hull endpoints are identified, choose an interior point of E arbitrarily near x_* first and then a sufficiently small cylinder through that point with distance to the seam ≥c₀r_u; perfection allows this. Let H be a fixed smooth cutoff on the normalized line, equal to one on E, nonnegative and supported in the c₀/4-neighborhood of E. In physical coordinates use the cutoff h=H∘S_u^{-1}. On its support D is exactly the distance to E_u and the relative perturbation of c is bounded by η, where η can be prescribed arbitrarily small before making this choice. In particular v=0 at the points of E_u. A reflection in S_u causes no change.

Use integer frequencies qn with q=floor(1/(3r_u)) for r_u≤1/12 (for a circle of period P use the initial fixed period scaling). The coordinates y=q(x−x_u) around one chosen representative identify the selected cylinder with a translated or reflected copy of E at scale λ=q r_u∈[1/4,1/3]. After division by the fixed Jacobian, the model integral is at frequencies n and with variance coefficient a equal to the fixed Fourier-normalization constant times c q^{2−α}. The unperturbed natural probability is supported on a proper arc in these coordinates. It is uniformly d-regular because λ ranges over a compact positive interval. The spatial weights H and the positive Gaussian input density (or constant circle input density) in these coordinates have uniformly bounded C¹ norms and the weights are uniformly bounded below on E_u. The same fixed constants therefore suffice in the Fejér annulus lower bound and in the large-sieve comparison. Constants depending on a are avoided by always writing σ=(aN²)^{−1/α}; N can be taken arbitrarily large after fixing the chosen cylinder. The required stopping cell mass approximations in the unequal-ratio case have error o(1) for each fixed cylinder, which is sufficient.

Here is the only new large-sieve estimate. Use the same unperturbed Z(s), disjoint stopping tubes, center phases, and δ≈ε/N as in the preceding proofs. On the fixed support, write the rescaled actual tilt as exp(−s²A(x)(1+θ(x))), with A(x)=aN²D_y(x)^α and |θ|≤η≤1/2. For 1/2≤s≤2C and j≤2 the mean value theorem yields
|∂_s^j [exp(−s²A(1+θ))−exp(−s²A)]|≤C_j η exp(−c₁ A).
Indeed differentiation introduces a fixed polynomial in A and differentiation with respect to θ contributes another A; using part of the exponent absorbs all powers. No x derivatives enter. The normalized integral of this bound over any one tube, including the bounded weight, is ≤Cη r_w^d, where w denotes a stopping word of the selected, rescaled Cantor set. This follows from the individual full-line enlarged-tilt mass bound and Z(s)≍σ^{1−d}. The first two derivatives of the centered Fourier phase are uniformly bounded as Nδ≤ε, and the derivatives of 1/Z have the earlier relative bounds. The tube complement in the support remains exponentially small because v≥cD^α/2 there. The extra perturbation coefficient at each center thus has C² norm O(η r_w^d).

It follows that the total normalized squared error on the annulus is at most
K ε^{d−1}(ε+η+o(1))² N^{1−d} +o(N^{1−d}),
where K is independent of η, ε, and the selected sufficiently small cylinder. First fix C then ε using the lower bound for the unperturbed natural measure, then prescribe η≤ε and choose a cylinder satisfying it, and finally take N→∞. This retains a strictly positive annular lower bound after multiplying by the unperturbed Z. For the upper bound use the same localized output test at physical frequencies qn; on the cutoff support the actual variance is comparable to cD^α, as needed to integrate all derivative remainders. On the circle expand the smooth periodic cutoff into its rapidly decaying Fourier series in place of the line Fourier inversion. Since qn is an integer, the Gaussian multipliers at the shifted integer frequencies are exact, and all lower order terms again contain spatial derivatives of the cutoff, supported in the patch. The constants in this upper bound may depend on the fixed q and cutoff; sending N to infinity gives the contradiction.

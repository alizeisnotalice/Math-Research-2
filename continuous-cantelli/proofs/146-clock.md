# Conditional Gaussian scores on the zero set in the natural parameter clock

Let f be a bounded nonnegative continuous exact graph, ν_t its positive real tilt, W_t=X−t f(X), and U_t=√t W_t. For any compactly supported continuous g and fixed real h there is an exact parameter change

    E_t[g(X) exp(hU_t)] = E_{t+s}[g(X) exp(c_h X)],
    s=−t+√(t²+2h t^(3/2)),    c_h=s²/(2t)→h²/2.                    (1)

Use t sufficiently large for the square root. Indeed the likelihood to t+s has exponent (s+s²/(2t))W_t−s²X/(2t). Identity (1) needs no derivative of the graph.

If the spatial marginals ν_t converge vaguely to π as t→∞, the local joint limit of (X,U_t) is π(dx)N(0,x)(du), interpreting zero variance as a point mass. Without convergence, the same assertion holds along **any subsequential limit of spatial averages** taken uniformly in the clock w=2√t over intervals [B,B+L] with B,L→∞, using the same average for the joint law. The measure π may be a subprobability; escaped mass is outside this local assertion.

To prove the averaged statement, under (1) the clock becomes w'=w(1+4h/w)^(1/4)=w+h+O_h(1/w), with derivative 1+O_h(w⁻²). Changing variables on the entire averaging interval changes the average of any bounded function of w by O_h(1/L+1/B²). On the support of g the multiplier exp(c_h X) may be replaced uniformly by exp(h²X/2)+o(1). Thus local averaged Laplace moments converge to ∫g(x)exp(h²x/2)π(dx). For a convergent unaveraged spatial law use (1) directly. Both limits charge only x≥0 with f(x)=0: negative compact intervals have exponentially vanishing mass by the tilt and so do compact sets where f has a positive lower bound. For any fixed spatial compact, (1) with bounded compact cutoffs and both signs of arbitrarily large h gives uniform exponential score moments there. This supplies tightness in the score and uniform integrability of each local Laplace test. Disintegration and uniqueness of moment generating functions identify its conditional Gaussian law (one may first use rational h and a countable determining family of g). No decomposition into finitely many wells is needed.

In particular, suppose f(0)>0, there is exactly one positive zero a, and liminf at positive infinity is greater than one. Then ν_t→δ_a as spatial probabilities. Indeed the whole negative half-axis has mass at most g(0)/t, compact sets separated from a and zero on the positive side have a positive height gap, and far positive mass is O(exp(−ct²)) by Gaussian completion. Continuity handles the origin. Consequently √t(X−tf(X)) converges in law to N(0,a). Every score moment also converges: use (1) without a cutoff for both signs of h; the additional weight exp(c_hX) stays bounded on the negative wing, and on the remote positive wing its at most fixed exponential growth costs only O(t) in the log of the tail bound. This conclusion places a fluctuation constraint on a hypothetical exact graph with an isolated favored zero, without prescribing its cusp shape.

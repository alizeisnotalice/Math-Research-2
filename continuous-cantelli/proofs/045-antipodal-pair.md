# Distinct smooth graph laws with the same complete density jet

For every b>0 and finite set of real transform parameters there exist two bounded smooth even variance functions v_+,v_− as in the global construction, with Ev_±(X)=b, such that their output laws are different. Their moment generating functions both match N(0,1+b) on the prescribed set. Their output densities have identical Taylor jets at the origin; at all positive orders this common jet is that of N(0,1+b). Their density difference is nonzero on every one-sided neighborhood of the origin. This assertion compares two non-Gaussian probability laws.

## Continuous family of constrained graphs

Use the global construction in 044-global.md, including the even local profile, the thin transition and the fixed finite collection of exterior controls in 1<|x|<2. Pick 0<δ<min(1,b)/4. Additional even nonnegative smooth functions φ_1,φ_2, each at most one, have disjoint supports in two pairs of intervals ±J_j, with J_2 entirely to the right of J_1 on the positive ray. Both have a nonempty open plateau of height one in J_j. For θ on the real unit circle, prescribe v=b+δΣθ_jφ_j on these new intervals. Translate the two pairs sufficiently far into the tails, keeping their widths fixed. The perturbations in the prescribed transforms, Ev, and the second density derivative at zero then tend uniformly to zero: the first group is bounded by constants times the Gaussian integral weighted by exp(max|t_i||x|); the second density derivative is evaluated at variances in a fixed positive compact interval. Fix these support locations, then choose small R. The exterior inverse used in 044-global.md applies uniformly to all residuals, and the resulting bump corrections in 1<|x|<2 may be made smaller than δ/10 in supremum norm. Thus the quadratic-growth rate of their MGF contribution will be strictly below that of a largest new bump. The previous curvature error E is now O(R) plus the uniformly small error due to the new bumps and their compensations.

Here the global fixed point can be chosen continuously in θ. For completeness, use the scaled coordinates z_k=a_k/M_k in the unit sup-norm cube and the extra coordinate c∈[1/24,1/8]. Choose all cutoff radii so that ||Δw||∞≤|Δc|+ε||Δz||∞ with fixed small ε. The exterior parameters are Lipschitz in this norm for fixed θ with a Lipschitz constant O(R⁵): the core changes by u⁴Δw and the transition changes by O(R⁴)|Δc|. The finite inverse has a bounded derivative. In the curvature update the core I_4 has exponentially small Lipschitz norm. The transition has length O(R⁵) and sup_s|∂_s p_s''(u)|≤C|u|⁻⁵ there, giving a Lipschitz bound O(R⁴). The effect of the finite exterior controls is O(R⁵). The new distant bumps are fixed while comparing inputs at fixed θ. Hence c↦1/12−E/(12g(0)), as a row of the full update, has Lipschitz norm <1/2 once R is small. Its values remain within the chosen interval by the preceding size estimates. In each remaining row, the numerator's sup and Lipschitz bounds are finite uniformly in θ before choosing M_k, by the fixed positive exterior variance bound, the off-diagonal Lipschitz estimate and the polynomial bounds from previous rows. Enlarge M_k before choosing its support scale, so every such full normalized row is also a contraction with constant at most 1/2. This gives a unique fixed point for each θ in this family with these cutoffs.

In the compact product topology the equations and the final density value at zero depend continuously on θ and the coordinates. This uses uniform convergence of the cutoff series and dominated convergence in the off-diagonal integrals. Any product limit of fixed points is a fixed point at the limiting θ; uniqueness makes the product-topology solution continuous. In particular d(θ)=D_θ(0) is continuous. The function d(θ)−d(−θ) changes sign at antipodes, so some antipodal pair has equal value. All other density derivatives and finite real transforms already match by construction.

## Why their output laws differ

For this pair let B=max_j|θ_j|≥1/√2, and take the rightmost positive interval J_* among those attaining B. Choose a closed subinterval in its plateau with left endpoint l strictly larger than every right endpoint of the other maximal bumps (including the reflected negative copies). For t→+∞ consider the difference of the two real output transforms. The contribution of J_* has a fixed sign. Its magnitude is at least a fixed positive multiple of exp((b+δB)t²/2+lt) by integrating on this plateau and using 1−exp(−δB t²)→1. The other maximal bump intervals, if present, have strictly smaller upper endpoints and their contributions are exponentially smaller on the linear scale. Each nonmaximal new bump has a strictly smaller quadratic rate. All remaining differences are supported on a compact set and have variance at most b+δ/10 there, taking R smaller if necessary. The identical exterior baseline cancels exactly. Thus the sign of the difference for large t is that of J_* and the laws differ. Evenness and analyticity off the origin imply the two densities cannot agree on any open interval of either half-axis. Their difference has an identically zero Taylor jet at zero.

This is a finite-transform information obstruction. It does not match the unspecified transforms to the Gaussian, and it does not produce two different graphs with the same output law.

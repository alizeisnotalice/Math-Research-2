# Cantor minimum sets on the circle at lattice frequencies

Use the period-one circle, N=b^k (k≥0), integer b≥3, and the equal-contraction Cantor set E with digits {0,b−1}. Set L=(b−2)/b, s=log(2)/log(b), D(x)=dist(x,E) on the circle. For fixed a>0 and α>0 put

I_N=∫₀¹ cos(2πNx) exp(−a N²D(x)^α)dx,    Z_N=∫₀¹ exp(−a N²D(x)^α)dx.

By symmetry the complex coefficient equals I_N. For every a>0 and every α>0, I_{b^k} is nonzero for all sufficiently large k, as well as at k=0. At 0<α<2 the asymptotic ratio to Z_N is a strictly positive Cantor Fourier coefficient. At α>2 the eventual sign is positive for odd b and negative for even b. At α=2 the renormalized coefficients are eventually strictly increasing. In particular V=constant+cD^α, c>0, cannot preserve the uniform circle measure under the wrapped Gaussian kernel with position-dependent variance V. That would require I_N=0 for every positive integer N with a=2π²c. None of the conclusions require an assumption on the smoothness of D.

**1. Subquadratic case.** Write μ for the uniform digit product measure. Let J(t)=∫ exp(−t D^α)dx, and η_t(dx)=J(t)^{−1}exp(−tD^α)dx. Then J(t)≳t^{−(1−s)/α} for t≥1. Indeed, choose j with b^{−j} comparable to t^{−1/α}; on each level-j cylinder D≤b^{−j}, giving the bound by its length times 2^j. For each fixed depth l all 2^l retained intervals have identical unnormalized mass by exact rescaling. The finitely many exterior gaps have combined mass O_l(t^{−1/α}), since in each gap the mass is 2∫₀^{length/2}exp(−tu^α)du. Therefore η_t gives asymptotically 2^{−l} to every level-l interval and its total exterior mass tends to zero. By uniform continuity of continuous test functions this proves η_t⇒μ.

At N=b^k, the retained k-cylinders are [j/N,(j+1)/N]. Writing x=(j+y)/N gives ND(x)=D(y) and cos(2πNx)=cos(2πy). Thus retained mass is (2/b)^k J(t), with t=aN^{2−α}→∞, and its normalized Fourier coefficient tends to μ̂(1). The ≤2^k old gaps have mass O(2^k N^{−2/α}), at most O(N^{−(2−α)s/α}) times the retained mass. Consequently I_N/Z_N→μ̂(1), where
μ̂(1)=−∏_{j≥1}cos(π(b−1)/b^j)>0.
The first cosine is negative, every remaining cosine is positive, and the tail product is nonzero since its angle squares are summable.

**2. Critical case.** Set A_k=(b/2)^k I_{b^k}. The retained k-cylinders contribute A_0 after this rescaling. Each old gap of depth j≤k has integer N times length (b−2)b^{k−j} and both endpoints on the N grid. Its oscillatory integral equals 2N^{−1}∫₀^{(b−2)b^{k−j}/2} exp(−a y²)cos(2πy)dy. There are 2^{j−1} such gaps. Summing gives the exact formula
A_k=A_0+∑_{h=1}^k 2^{1−h}∫₀^{(b−2)b^{h−1}/2} exp(−a y²)cos(2πy)dy.
The integral in the last difference tends to (√π/(2√a))exp(−π²/a)>0, so A_k is eventually strictly increasing. It can vanish at most once in this final range. The absolutely convergent sum may in principle have zero limit; the difference argument deals with this without assuming a sign for the limit.

**3. Superquadratic case.** Put δ=(aN²)^{−1/α}, f(u)=exp(−(u/δ)^α), ω=2πN and H_j=(b−2)/(2b^j), the half-length of a depth-j gap. Then Nδ→∞. Denote B_N=N^{−2}δ^{−1−s}. In Fourier integration replace f by f−1, since the nonzero global harmonic of 1 vanishes. On old gaps j≤k both endpoints have phase one and the integrated constant is zero. Two integrations by parts and one more for the error give
2∫₀^{H_j} f(u)cos(ωu)du = 2f′(H_j)cos(ωH_j)/ω² + O(ω^{−3}∫₀^{H_j}|f‴(u)|du).
Here sin(ωH_j)=0, f′(0)=0, f″ is absolutely continuous even for 2<α<3, and the sine boundary term in the third integration vanishes at both endpoints. For every old gap cos(ωH_j)=(−1)^{(b−2)b^{k−j}} equals −1 for odd b and +1 for even b. All principal terms therefore have a common sign. Choosing the single generation with δ/b<H_j≤δ shows the total absolute principal contribution is ≳B_N, since |f′(H_j)|≍δ^{−1} and 2^{j−1}≍δ^{−s}. Such a generation exists with j≤k for all sufficiently large k.

By scaling, ∫₀^{H}|f‴|≤Cδ^{−2}min(1,(H/δ)^{α−2}). For H_j≥δ the total old-gap remainder is O(N^{−3}δ^{−2−s})=o(B_N). For H_j<δ and j≤k, multiplying the bound by the number O(H_j^{−s}) of such gaps gives an error at most
C N^{−3}δ^{−α} ∑_{H_k≤H_j<δ} H_j^{α−2−s}.
If α−2−s>0 its ratio to B_N is O((Nδ)^{−1}); at equality the ratio is O((1+log(Nδ))/(Nδ)). For α−2−s<0 the ratio is O((Nδ)^{1+s−α}). These all tend to zero because α>2 and s<1. Finally all fine gaps j>k have |f(u)−1|≤(u/δ)^α, so their absolute sum is O(δ^{−α}N^{−(1+α−s)}), whose ratio to B_N is O((Nδ)^{1+s−α})→0. The Cantor set itself has zero Lebesgue measure. This proves the stated eventual sign, with |I_N|≳B_N. Summing |f′(H_j)|H_j^{−s} over the two geometric tails H_j≤δ and H_j≥δ also gives |I_N|≲B_N.

**4. A fixed positive harmonic for every radial decreasing profile.** For 0<ε<L/2 let E_ε={D≤ε}. Its first cosine coefficient is strictly positive. If bε≥L/2 then only the central gap remains outside E_ε, and the coefficient equals sin(π(L−2ε))/π>0. If bε<L/2, the two retained edge cylinders contribute, by reflection symmetry, at least (2/b)cos²(π/b)|E_{bε}|. In detail their exact coefficient equals (2/b)cos(π/b) ∫_{E_{bε}} cos((2π/b)(y−1/2))dy. The central gap strips add at worst −2ε. In each complementary gap of length l≤L, the fraction inside E_t is min(1,2t/l)≥2t/L for t≤L/2; summing gives |E_{bε}|≥2bε/L. Hence the total is at least ε(4cos²(π/b)/L−2)>0. For b=3 use 2cos²(π/3)=1/2>L; for larger b use 2cos²(π/b)≥1>L. Integrating this statement in ε with the positive Stieltjes measure of any continuous, nonconstant nonincreasing weight on [0,L/2] proves positivity of its first cosine coefficient. This gives I_1>0 independently of high-frequency estimates.

**5. Arbitrary deleted-digit sets.** Let the digit set A contain 0 and b−1 with 2≤m=|A|<b. Adjacent retained children may meet at endpoints; integrals in each interval still scale exactly since E contains its endpoints. The gaps between consecutive retained digit runs in the unit generator have lengths l_i/b with integers l_i≥1. An old gap of type i at depth j has length l_i/b^j and multiplicity m^{j−1}. With s=log_b m, every proof above except the first-harmonic positivity proof adapts, as follows. For some even integer h>0, μ̂(h)≠0: the measure pushed forward by x↦2x mod 1 has Lebesgue-null support (the Lipschitz image of E), hence is not uniform, and some nonzero Fourier coefficient of this pushforward is nonzero. Use the sequence n=h b^k with this fixed h. In the subquadratic case the full complex normalized Fourier integral tends to μ̂(h) using t=ah²N^{2−α}. In the critical recursion, the normalized difference at step k is the positive real prefactor 2m^{−k} times the sum over types i of ∫₀^{l_i b^{k−1}/2} exp(−ah²y²)cos(2πh y)dy. Each of these approaches a positive real number. The baseline complex Fourier coefficient may be nonreal, but the real parts are eventually strictly increasing. In the superquadratic case all old endpoints have phase one and all midpoint cosines equal +1 because h is even. The principal terms are negative. The previous estimates, summed over finitely many types with δ=(a h²N²)^{−1/α}, give a strictly negative real part for large k. Thus one fixed lattice sequence h b^k detects all exponents for each missing-digit set.

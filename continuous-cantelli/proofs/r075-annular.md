# Annular Fourier energy for homogeneous Cantor minima

Let E in the line be the attractor of m≥2 contracting similarities, all with contraction ratio ρ, satisfying strong separation of the first-generation compact sets. Reflections are allowed. Assume 0<d=log(m)/log(1/ρ)<1. Let μ be its natural probability. In a circle E is embedded in an arc of length at most the period, possibly identifying the two hull endpoints. Fix 0<α<2 and a>0.

**Claim.** For constant variance coefficient a dist(·,E)^α the circle uniform-stationarity identities fail. More locally, a Gaussian-output graph on the line cannot agree with this variance on an open interval meeting E, provided that interval contains a point of E in its interior and the local variance is measured relative to this E. The variance outside the interval is arbitrary. Both conclusions hold for every such ρ, with no condition on decay of the Fourier transform of μ.

We use period one and frequencies 2πn. For the local line result one may use any fixed larger period containing the compact hull in a proper arc, with frequencies 2πn/P; equivalently rescale space. Write D=dist(·,E), σ=N^(−2/α). Fix any smooth real nonnegative weight w with compact support and ∫w dμ>0. Define T_n=∫w(x)exp(2πinx−a n² D(x)^α)dx. Constants in the variance and Fourier normalization can be absorbed in a. We prove for some C>1 and all large N,

    ∑_{N≤n≤CN} |T_n|² ≳ σ^{2(1−d)} N^{1−d}.                         (1)

On the circle take w=1. Endpoints and local outputs are addressed below.

**The natural measure.** Level-j cylinder sets have diameter comparable to ρ^j and separation ≥cρ^j, by distinct first digits after their longest common prefix. Each has mass m^(−j). Choosing ρ^j comparable to r proves μ(B(x,r))≍r^d for x∈E at small r: a small cylinder through x is inside the ball, and at most a fixed number of level-j cylinders can meet it by separation. Thus |E_r|≍r^{1−d}, where E_r denotes the r-neighborhood, by a maximal r-separated set. These bounds persist under endpoint identification, with constants modified.

For ν=wμ let S(K)=∑_{|n|≤K}|ν̂(n)|². The Fejér kernel of order K, with coefficients (1−|n|/K)_+, is ≥cK at circle distances ≤1/(4K), ≤CK/(1+K²dist²) everywhere, and nonnegative. Testing it against ν⊗ν gives S(K)≳K^{1−d}, using a fixed smaller open subset of positive μ mass on whose neighborhood w has a positive lower bound. Testing the order-2K kernel, whose coefficients dominate 1/2 on |n|≤K, and summing distance annuli using μ(B)≲r^d yields S(K)≲K^{1−d}. Choose a fixed C sufficiently large that the lower bound at CN exceeds twice the upper bound at N. Since ν is real, ∑_{N≤n≤CN}|ν̂(n)|²≥c₀ N^{1−d}.

**Transfer to the tilted variance.** Fix a small ε>0, choose a level j with δ=ρ^j comparable to ε/N (specifically ρε/N<δ≤ε/N), and set M=m^j=δ^(−d). The open cδ-neighborhoods U_i of the M level-j compact sets E_i are disjoint for a fixed sufficiently small c>0. Each is a translated, possibly reflected, copy of the same neighborhood; D on it is distance to its own E_i. Thus for every real s in [1/2,2C], the unweighted mass of exp(−a(sN)²D^α) in each U_i is identical. Put Z(s) equal to the **sum over these neighborhoods** of these tilt masses. Uniformly in s, Z(s)≍σ^{1−d}. Indeed the lower bound follows from E_σ⊂∪U_i eventually; the upper bound follows by integrating |E_r|≲r^{1−d} against the stretched-exponential weight (use a polynomial bound for large r). Off the union, on bounded sets, D≥cδ, giving an exponentially small error O(exp(−c'(δ/σ)^α)). We keep ε fixed before N→∞. All constants in Z(s)≍σ^{1−d} can be taken independent of ε once N is sufficiently large depending on ε.

Let π_{i,s} be the conditional tilt probability in U_i and μ_i the natural conditional probability on E_i. The derivatives in s up to order two of π_{i,s} as signed measures have total variation bounded by a constant independent of i,ε,N for s∈[1/2,2C]. In fact the unnormalized derivatives are polynomials of bounded degree in N²D^α times the tilt, so their total variations are bounded by the same constant times the tilt mass with a smaller fixed coefficient. The latter is at most Cσ^{1−d}/M by congruence and the upper tubular bound, while the original mass is ≥cσ^{1−d}/M. The quotient rule gives the assertion.

Pick representatives x_i∈E_i. Directly subtract the two conditional integrals in the expression for T_n/Z(n/N)−ν̂(−n). The cell difference is exp(2πinx_i) b_i(n/N), where

    b_i(s)= M^(−1) ∫ [w(x)exp(2πisN(x−x_i))−w(x_i)] d(π_{i,s}−μ_i)(x).

The subtracted constant integrates to zero. Diameters of U_i are O(δ), so the bracket and its first two derivatives in s are O(Nδ)=O(ε). It follows that ∥b_i∥_{C²[1/2,2C]}≤C₁ε/M with a uniform C₁. This uses no common orientation.

**The variable-amplitude large sieve.** For points x_i on the line separated by ≳δ in an interval of length at most one, and C² coefficients b_i on [1/2,2C],

    ∑_{N≤n≤CN}|∑_i exp(2πin x_i)b_i(n/N)|²
       ≤ C₂(N+δ^(−1))∑_i∥b_i∥²_{C²}.

To see it insert a fixed smooth squared cutoff equal to 1 on [1,C] and supported in (1/2,2C). The (i,j) expanded sum is bounded by C N∥b_i∥_{C²}∥b_j∥_{C²}/(1+N²∥x_i−x_j∥²), using the trivial estimate and twice summation by parts, with ∥·∥ the circle distance. The maximum row sum of the distance kernel is O(1+(Nδ)^(−1)) by separation. This also holds if the interval endpoints are identified because any periodic interval of length δ lifts to at most two pieces inside a unit interval. Use 2ab≤a²+b². Consequently the squared error summed on the annulus is at most
C₃(N+δ^(−1)) ε²/M + o(N^{1−d}) ≤ C₄ ε^{1+d} N^{1−d}+o(N^{1−d}).
Here the little-o includes the discarded exterior and C₄ is independent of ε. Choose ε so small that the error norm is less than half the above main norm, using first the choice of C and then N sufficiently large. The lower bound on Z gives (1).

For a full-period hull with both ends identified, compute the union of tubes on the lifted line. On the hull the circle distance to E equals the line distance, since both endpoints belong to E. The added exterior pieces when integrating the line version of the tube union have total absolute tilt mass O(σ) from the two outer endpoints. After normalization the error per frequency is O(σ^d), and σ^d=o(N^{−d/2}) since α<2. This suffices in the squared annular bound. For a proper arc one can cut the circle inside the complementary gap and use the exponentially small exterior estimate (or the same endpoint bound).

**Localization for exact Gaussian output.** Let Y conditional on X=x have mean x and variance v(x), X have smooth positive Gaussian density g, and Y have Gaussian density of positive variance. Choose 0≤h∈C_c^∞ in the stated contact interval with ∫h g dμ>0, and put w=hg. Then for ξ=2πn the exact ∣E[h(Y)e^{iξY}]∣ is O_K(n^(−K)) for every fixed K by smoothness of the target. Expand h by Fourier inversion. The conditional multiplier in the inner integral is exp(−(ξ+t)²v(x)/2). Taylor-expand it in t at 0 to order K−1 on |t|≤ξ/2: for every l, the lth derivative is bounded by C_l n^(−l) exp(−c n² v(x)), uniformly in x, since Hermite polynomials times a Gaussian are bounded, including v=0. The remainder is O_K(n^(−K)|t|^K), and the |t|>ξ/2 tail and all tails of finite Taylor moments are O_K(n^(−K)) by rapid decay of the Fourier transform of h. Upon inversion the lth Taylor terms contain h^(l)(x), supported in the contact interval. The l=0 term is T_n with coefficient a for v/2 in this normalization, and all l≥1 together are O(n^(−1)σ^{1−d}) by the upper tube estimate on the compact support. Thus |T_n|≤C n^(−1)σ^{1−d}+O_K(n^(−K)) uniformly in the annulus. Taking K sufficiently large contradicts (1) since d<1. This proves the local assertion.

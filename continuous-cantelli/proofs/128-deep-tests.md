# No fixed number of time-difference orders detects all negative relaxed survivors

Here a relaxed graph is a conditional probability law for a bounded mark F≥0 given the Gaussian X, with the full normalization E exp(zX−z²F/2)=1. Fix any derivative order N and finite Q. There exists δ(N,Q)>0 such that every exact relaxed graph with F≤1+δ satisfies

    (−1)^n ∂_q^n R(q,y)>0,  0≤n≤N, 0≤q≤Q, y on either wing.

This covers every nonzero nonnegative spatial test. In particular the integer Hausdorff differences with j+k≤M are all positive if δ is sufficiently small depending on M. Nevertheless there are three-time exact relaxed graphs with arbitrarily small sup|F−1| whose survivors are negative.

**Uniform positive signs.** Since EF=1 and 0≤F≤1+δ≤2, E(F−1)²≤δ (use F²≤(1+δ)F). On each compact range |y|≤Y, the resolvent formula and the mean value theorem in F bound the difference of every specified derivative from the constant graph by C(N,Q,Y)√δ, using Cauchy–Schwarz. Indeed each derivative kernel and its derivative in F are dominated, for y on the right and u=x−y>0, by C(1+u^{2N+1})exp(√(2Q)u); the reflected bound is the same. This majorant has a bounded Gaussian second moment on the stated compact. At the constant graph the target equals ∫₀¹t^n exp(−qt)p_t(y)dt, bounded below uniformly by its integral on [1/2,1]. For |y|≥Y, take the uniform exterior estimates of round 122 with H=2 and sufficiently large Y for the finitely many n,Q. Their proof uses only the spatial Gaussian marginal, real normalization and the bound H, all valid with a conditional distribution of marks (the rare-set estimate becomes a joint probability). Zeroth order follows directly from the positive resolvent. Hence δ can be taken strictly positive independently of the graph. Finally D_{j,k} is the integral of (−1)^k R^{(k)}(j+t₁+…+t_k) over [0,1]^k, with evaluation at j for k=0. This proves the integer claim.

**Explicit negative family.** Fix 0<ε≤1/4, s=1−ε, J=[2,2+ε^{3/2}/100]. Put η(dx)=(1−ε/2)1_J(x)g(x)dx. Assign mark s to η, mark 1+ε to P_εη, and mark 1 to the residual γ−η−P_εη. All three measures are nonnegative. In fact, uniformly in y and x∈J,
p_ε(y−x)/g(y) ≤ ε^{−1/2} exp(x²/[2(1−ε)]),
by completing the square in y. Since |x|≤2.1, η(J)≤g(0)|J| and g(0)exp(2.1²/(2(1−ε)))<8, we have P_εη≤0.08 ε γ. This leaves a positive middle density even on J.

The spatial marginal is γ by construction. If q=z²/2 and M_η=∫exp(zx)dη then the change from the constant graph's transform is M_η[e^{−qs}+e^{−q(1+ε)}e^{qε}−e^{−q}(1+e^{qε})]=0 for all real z. At the platform itself Q_s has density p_s−dη/dx. On J, p_s/g≤exp(−3ε/(2(1−ε)))≤1−ε, using −log(1−ε)≤ε/(1−ε). Thus Q_s≤−ε γ/2 on J. A smooth nonnegative test inside J has strictly negative mass, persisting immediately to the right by right continuity. The Hausdorff equivalence produces a negative integer difference. By the first part its least possible total order, even when the spatial test can vary with ε, must tend to infinity as ε↓0. This gives an analytic family where the finite-order obstruction is arbitrarily deep. The conditional time distribution is genuinely mixed on J; the family gives no deterministic counterexample.

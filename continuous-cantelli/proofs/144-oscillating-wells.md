# Two oscillating continuous wells with constant leading total flux

Fix a positive canonical time T with radius b and edge slope J. Choose distinct a₁,a₂>b and set d_i=a_i−b. Take A_i>0 with A₁+A₂=J and nonzero B_i with |B_i|<A_i such that ΣB_i exp(−1/(2d_i))=0. This is possible by scaling either signed coefficient; ΣB_i is then nonzero. There exists an even bounded continuous graph, canonical throughout [−b,b], whose normalized exterior edge output has the correct limit J on both wings, although its individual well outputs oscillate, and even their unmarked center density has a persistent relative oscillation. The graph can satisfy Ef=1 while violating a higher exact transform identity.

**Realizing prescribed center measures on a graph.** For m≥M prescribe ρ_i(m)=m⁻²[A_i+B_i cos(2√m)]. On the right flank of a_i solve the integral equation

    x(m)=a_i+∫_m^∞ ρ_i(s) √((x(s)−b)/(2πs)) exp(−s(x(s)−b)/2)/g(x(s)) ds.

On bounded continuous functions valued in [a_i,a_i+ε] this maps the set into itself for M sufficiently large. Its Lipschitz constant in the sup norm is bounded by C∫_M^∞ (1+s)s^(−5/2)exp(−d_i s/2)ds<1, by the ordinary derivative with respect to x of the integrand. The unique solution is strictly decreasing and x(m)−a_i=O(m^(−5/2)exp(−d_i m/2)). Define v(x(m))=(x(m)−b)/m, f=T+v and f(a_i)=T. This realizes exactly the proposed center density by ordinary change of variables in round 143. The underlying graph on each flank is continuous, monotone and single-valued.

Its output equals ∫_M^∞ p_{m/(x(m)−b)}(t−m)ρ_i(m)dm. After m=t+√t w, the quantity t² times this integral equals

    A_i+B_i exp(−1/(2d_i))cos(2√t)+o(1).

Indeed on bounded w the Gaussian tends to p_{1/d_i}(w) in the scaled variable and 2√m=2√t+w+o(1). On t/2≤m≤2t the uniform integrable majorant is C exp(−c w²); outside this range the Gaussian tail is exponentially small after polynomial scaling. Integration of the cosine uses the Gaussian characteristic function. Adding the two expressions cancels the leading oscillation, whereas the sum of the input densities is exactly m⁻²[J+(B₁+B₂)cos(2√m)] for m≥M. In particular the combined unmarked center measure has different subsequential limits on the √t scale even though the graph has constant limiting total flux.

**Completion away from these two flanks.** On the other flank near each a_i set v(a_i−u)=1/√log(1/u) for small u>0. Its output is negligible: substituting L=log(1/u), the exponent is at most t d_i−q/√L−L, with q=t²/2. The latter two losses bound the integral by C exp(−c t^(4/3)+t d_i), on keeping half of L for integration. Near b use v(b+u)=√u. Its output is O(t⁻⁴) by using tu≤q√u/2 on a fixed small interval and substituting w=q√u. Connect the finitely many pieces on compact intervals with strictly positive v. Set v equal to a constant >1 beyond a fixed large radius. The remaining compact parts are exponentially negligible in t², and the far tail is bounded by Gaussian completion of the square. Reflect all pieces. At positive tilt the opposite exterior wing has exponentially small contribution after normalization by exp(−bt), since v≥0 there. Hence the difference of the full real transform from 1 is o(t⁻²exp(b|t|−Tt²/2)) at both infinities by the canonical core identity.

To arrange the mean, choose b small so T<1/4, take the two wells sufficiently far out and start the constant tail still farther out. Keep the edge collars and the compact transition away from a central plateau short. Set f=T+1/4 on most of the remaining Gaussian mass, and interpolate as above; the heights can all be bounded by 3, and the resulting mean is below 1. Raising this plateau with a compact even nonnegative bump outside the core makes the mean exceed 1; interpolate to equality. This modifies only compact regions with v bounded below. Finally vary by λ times a nonzero continuous even bump of zero Gaussian mean, supported in the same positive-gap region. For sufficiently small λ every earlier property is preserved. The fourth transform derivative EX⁴−6EX²f+3Ef² is a quadratic in λ with strictly positive leading coefficient. Choosing one of any three distinct small λ away from its at most two roots ensures this derivative is nonzero. Thus this construction explicitly distinguishes compatibility with the high-tilt core budget from exactness at all parameters. The accompanying quadrature checks the single-flank transfer coefficient for d=2; the construction and error statements above are analytic.

# Intrinsic-noise scale periodic profiles at a flat contact

Suppose v≥0 is a bounded continuous graph with Gaussian output, so D_e(y)=∫p_{v(x)+e}(y−x)g(x)dx=p_{M+e}(y), e>0, g=p₁. Let r_j→0 from the positive side, h_j→0 and a_j=x₀+r_j. If v(a_j+h_j t)/h_j²→V(t) locally in measure for a bounded nonnegative profile V, then for every δ>0 and s∈ℝ,
∫_ℝ p'_{δ+V(t)}(s−t)dt=0.                                      (1)
Indeed h_j D'_{δh_j²}(a_j+h_js) is the integral of the analogous rescaled kernel against g(a_j+h_jt), and tends to zero by exactness. On bounded intervals there is convergence of integrals by continuity and uniform boundedness; on the tails use |p'_T(y)|≤C|y|^−2 independent of T>0. Since g(x₀)>0, (1) follows. For bounded V the integral of the undifferentiated kernel is finite for every s, and differentiation under the integral is justified. Thus this output density is constant in s. If V is L-periodic its average over one period equals one, by Gaussian mass and unfolding. Equivalently the wrapped Gaussian kernel of variance V(t) sends uniform circle measure to uniform measure: for n∈ℤ\{0}, κ=2π/L,
∫₀ᴸ exp[−inκt−(nκ)²V(t)/2]dt=0.                                  (2)
The Gaussian δ factor cancels; also subtracting the essential minimum from V preserves (2). All these statements concern the scaled tangent profile and do not assume anything on the opposite side of x₀.

**Nonconstant profiles impossible in the following classes.**

1. Suppose V has an attained minimum V₀, and V(t₀+u)−V₀≤C u² in a two-sided neighborhood. Put W=V−V₀. If W is nonzero on a set of positive measure, the stationary circle density of W has a strictly positive contribution from sources bounded away from t₀ with variances bounded below. Such sources exist unless W=0 almost everywhere. For c>0, Fatou on the smoothed identity at t₀ gives ∫_{c u²≤W(t₀+u)≤C u²}du/|u|<∞ near zero: the Gaussian kernel at difference −u on this set is ≥C_c/|u|. Therefore for every fixed K the exceptional set W(t₀+u)≥c u² inside |u|≤Kh has measure o(h). To count output probability in |y−t₀|≤h, restrict to local sources |u|≤(1−ε)h outside this exception. On |Z|≤ε/√c their outputs stay in the interval. Thus their normalized mass has liminf at least (1−ε)P(|Z|≤ε/√c) times the uniform-density unit. Let c↓0, ε↓0 to obtain at least the full uniform unit. The disjoint positive contribution noted above then contradicts stationarity. This includes C^{1,1} at one minimum and arbitrary C² periodic profiles. If W=0 on a positive-measure set the zero-variance sources already fill that set's uniform output mass, also immediately excluding W>0 elsewhere.
2. A bounded profile taking finitely many distinct values of positive measure is constant almost everywhere if (2) holds. After subtracting the lowest value, the Fourier coefficients of its level-set indicator 1_E for n≠0 are bounded by C exp(−c n²), owing to the gap to the remaining finite levels. These give an analytic representative of 1_E on the connected circle. Its values lie in {0,1} everywhere by continuity and almost-everywhere equality, so it is constant, contradicting a nontrivial minimum level set.
3. Let A(t)=exp(−κ²V(t)/2). If {A>mean(A)} is a single true arc up to null sets and A is nonconstant, stationarity is impossible. An affine cosine having zeros at the two endpoints is positive precisely on that arc and negative on its complement. Its integral against A−mean(A) is strictly positive, contradicting the vanishing zeroth and first Fourier coefficients. This covers single-trough/single-crest profiles of any regularity without a quadratic contact hypothesis.
4. For any fixed bounded nonconstant periodic H the profiles V=c+ηH cannot satisfy (2) for all sufficiently small nonzero |η| (assume V nonnegative). Choose n with Ĥ(n)≠0. For Q=(nκ)²/2, the nth Fourier coefficient of e^−QηH is −Qη Ĥ(n)+O(η²) by a uniform Taylor bound, so it cannot vanish. This bound depends on H and its chosen harmonic.

For nonperiodic isolated local defects, if V equals a constant c off a compact set K, then (1) forces V=c almost everywhere. The density differs integrably from uniform and has zero integrated difference, so its constant is 1. Fourier transformation of the compact-input difference gives ∫_K e^{iξt}[e^{−ξ²V/2}−e^{−ξ²c/2}]dt=0. The identity extends to complex ξ. At ξ=iT a positive-measure set V≥c+δ contributes exp[(c+δ)T²/2−O(T)] whereas the total negative part is at most O(exp[cT²/2+O(T)]); thus V≤c almost everywhere. The quadratic term at ξ=0 then forces ∫_K(V−c)=0.

For the contact v(x₀+u)=u^q[H(ωu^{1−q/2})+o(1)] with q>2, h=r^{q/2} and ω≠0, the phase expands along a subsequence as θ+ω(1−q/2)t+o(1) on compact t-sets. Therefore any bounded continuous, nonconstant H in the excluded classes gives a contradiction, with no condition on the opposite side. Values of H with jump discontinuities on a null set work as well whenever such asymptotics are compatible with continuity of the actual graph. For general bounded nonconstant periodic profiles the multiplier conditions (2) are necessary; this result does not claim a universal classification.

**Separated cusps at finitely many minima.** Suppose the continuous, nonconstant profile W=V−min V has finitely many zeros S={z₁,…,z_m} on its circle. Suppose for fixed c,α>0 with α<2,
W(t)≥c dist(t,S)^α in a neighborhood of S. Suppose also at one zero z₁ there exist C,β>0 with W(z₁+u)≤C|u|^β for sufficiently small |u|. Then (2) is impossible, including when the several cusps have different subquadratic powers and oscillate between the bounds at each minimum. By compactness the lower estimate extends over the whole circle with a smaller constant. For arbitrarily large positive integers n, the phases nκ(z_j−z₁) are all within π/12 of multiples of 2π. Indeed compactness of their rotation orbit gives pairs of iterates arbitrarily close with arbitrarily large index gap (or simply use recurrence of a rotation of a finite torus). Fix σ>0 with κσ≤π/12. On dist(t,S)≤σ/n the real part of exp(−inκ(t−z₁)) is ≥1/2 for those n. The integrated positive weight there is ≥c' n^(−2/β) due to the interval around z₁ of radius ε n^(−2/β); consistency of the two cusp bounds gives β≤α<2, so this interval is eventually inside σ/n. On the rest of the circle the full absolute contribution is ≤L exp(−c''n^(2−α)). The selected Fourier coefficients are nonzero for large n. One-sided W(z₁+u)≤C|u|^β on an interval 0≤u≤u₀ suffices. The single-minimum quadratic and smooth-profile criterion above does not require finiteness of S.

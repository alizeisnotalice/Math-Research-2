# Gaussian rigidity for two-sided convergent Puiseux germs

Let X,Y be independent standard normals and φ be continuous and nonnegative. If φ is globally subanalytic and X+φ(X)Y has a Gaussian law, then φ is constant. In particular the assertion covers all continuous semialgebraic φ, without restrictions on degree or complexity of its graph.

More generally let f be the normalized exact coefficient, so f is nonnegative bounded, E f(X)=1 and ∫ exp(tx−t²f(x)/2)dγ(x)=1. It suffices that f have finite limits at both ends and, at every finite point a, both germs f(a±u), u>0, be represented by convergent power series in u^(1/q) for some integer q≥1 (allowing different denominators and constant germs). In the original problem f=C−φ² with C the output variance, so global subanalyticity passes to f. A bounded globally subanalytic function has finite tail limits by applying the one-variable Puiseux theorem also to f(±1/u). For a primary source use [van den Dries and Miller, §1.1 and §3.1(4)](https://www.numdam.org/article/AIF_1994__44_5_1367_0.pdf) with field of exponents Q, taking a common denominator. See also the Puiseux development in §2 of [Bolte, Daniilidis and Lewis](https://people.orie.cornell.edu/aslewis/publications/07-tame.pdf).

**Compact mass and attainment.** A finite nonzero zero of f is impossible: its local Puiseux expansion bounds f by C|x−a|^ε, contradicted by the exponentially small sublevel mass bound of round 6. A zero side interval would also violate the bound on any subinterval away from zero. If f(0)=0, a leading order p>2 on either side is impossible: apply the same sublevel inequality to that side's interval at distances [r,2r] from 0. Its Gaussian mass is comparable to r, while the upper bound is exp(−c r^(2−p)). Thus f≥c x² near 0. For each fixed compact interval its tilted mass ∫exp(tx−t²f/2)dγ tends to zero as |t|→∞: away from 0 there is a positive lower bound, and near a zero at 0 complete the square to obtain O(1/|t|).

A right tail limit less than 1 makes the tilted mass of [t−1,t+1] diverge as t→+∞, using the shifted Gaussian density times exp(t²(1−f)/2). If the limit exceeds 1, the mass on [R,∞) tends to zero by the same formula; on (−∞,−R] it is at most exp(−Rt), and the compact remainder tends to zero. Both contradict normalization. Reflection gives the left limit. Thus both limits are 1. A nonconstant continuous f of mean 1 must exceed 1 somewhere; its maximum M>1 is attained on a nonempty compact set. Choose its rightmost point a, so the right branch of v=M−f is positive immediately to the right, and its Puiseux series there is nonzero.

**Orders up to two.** Give an identically zero side order +∞. If either side has order two, round 16 applies directly on that side. If both orders are less than two, v/u² tends to infinity on both sides, yet some local negative moment diverges, contrary to round 17. If one order is less than two and the other exceeds two (possibly infinity), round 20 excludes the mixed contact. These cover all cases except when both orders exceed two.

**Reading the series with the interval mass formula.** Choose a common denominator for the two series. Proceed through their ordered union of exponents. After all preceding nonzero terms match a common integer-power polynomial P in the signed coordinate, let α>2 be the next exponent and write the two residuals as (c_±+o(1))u^α, one coefficient possibly zero. Use P=0 initially. At later steps P has a strictly positive even leading term: this follows at the first step from nonnegativity and the parity conclusion below. Hence P is nonnegative on a neighborhood and extends using a nonnegative smooth cutoff. Both v and P satisfy some common superquadratic upper bound. Round 52 implies that the primitive of g(v−P) agrees with a single polynomial to error o(|h|^(α+1)). But direct integration for ±h gives right coefficient g(0)c₊/(α+1) and left coefficient −g(0)c₋/(α+1) in powers of |h|. At least one is nonzero. Therefore α is an integer and c₋=(−1)^α c₊. This proves the inductive step, including the impossibility of a one-sided plateau. If the union terminates, both series are the same polynomial. Otherwise induction on the common discrete exponent lattice fixes every coefficient. Convergence in u^(1/q) implies convergence in the ordinary signed coordinate of the resulting integer-power series, since it is a subseries at positive radius. Thus v has a nonzero two-sided real analytic germ at its attained zero a, excluded by round 23.

The argument only invokes Puiseux expansions at possible zeros and at the selected maximum; the tail limits are a separate hypothesis for the stated broader class. Continuity alone ensures neither these expansions nor the compact tilted-mass estimates used here.

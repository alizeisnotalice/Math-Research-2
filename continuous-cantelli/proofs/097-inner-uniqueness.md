# A fixed high wing determines the other wing under a small heat-potential condition

In the unequal-tail single-well setting of round 94 suppose the high wing on a short outward collar agrees with the genuine Gaussian first-exit continuation beyond b. Write A(t) for the low-wing radius at times just above T and R(t) for the canonical radius. Assume A is continuous there and its increments obey

    lim_(h↓0) sup_(T<t≤T+h) ∫_(T,t) (t−s)^(−1/2) dA(s)=0.

For example a local Lipschitz inverse, or a uniform Hölder exponent >1/2, suffices. Then no such exact graph exists. The low wing need not be analytic. For single-well candidates a C¹ low inverse with nonzero finite speed suffices; if the actual spatial graph is C¹ through b with positive outward derivative, its inverse meets this condition too.

**Proof.** Shrink the time window so the two high branches are identical, the actual high exterior heat remainder is zero there (the high tail is strictly above 1+T), and all new spatial sources are on the two outward wings. Let D=q_actual−q_canonical and W(t)=∫_T^t D(s)ds. The two distributions agree at T. The new high sources cancel. The canonical and actual low source measures are supported at −R(s) and −A(s), respectively, with spatial density g, and contribute with opposite signs. The kernel bound p_v≤(2πv)^(−1/2) and the hypothesis imply sup|D|=o(1) uniformly as the slab shrinks; the canonical radius has bounded speed. The measures have no time atoms under this assumption (or by inverse continuity). At each time t the function D(t,x) is spatially analytic on x>−min(A(t),R(t)): recent sources are separated from every compact interior neighborhood, and sources at older times have a positive heat age. It vanishes sufficiently far to the right since both remainders vanish there. Hence it vanishes throughout this half-line.

Let S=1_{I_actual}−1_{I_canonical}, the difference of the swept-interval indicators. In distributions W_xx=2(D+g S). On the preceding half-line S also vanishes, so W is affine there. It belongs to L² in space by the heat representation (bounded source measures and square-integrable heat kernel in spacetime suffice); thus W and its derivative there vanish. In the strip between the low boundaries, S has a single sign for this fixed time. There g is bounded uniformly away from zero. By choosing the short time slab such that |D|<g/2 in that region, W_xx has the sign of S. Integrating twice from the zero data at the right edge of the strip shows W S≥0. No assumption on how often the sign changes as time varies was used. The bounded right side for W_xx guarantees continuity of its primitive, so there is no boundary derivative atom.

Now W_t−W_xx/2=−g S with zero initial data. Testing against W gives a nonincreasing squared L² norm because the source term is −∫g SW≤0. The usual heat-energy identity applies to this bounded compact spatial forcing. Consequently W=0, D=0, and both graphs give the same compactly supported Q on some time interval beyond T. This is impossible: at any such time t the true positive future transform directed toward the low wing has a contribution ≥c exp[(t−T−δ)z²/2] from radii in [z,z+1] for each fixed 0<δ<t−T and all large z, by the low tail limit and completing the Gaussian square. Compact support only permits exponential growth linear in z in its logarithm.

By positive-time analyticity of the canonical boundary, a high wing analytic through b coincides with the canonical continuation on a collar, since the two agree inward of b. The same implication holds if the difference belongs to a quasianalytic germ class there. This application uses local equality, and the low-side heat-potential condition remains in force.

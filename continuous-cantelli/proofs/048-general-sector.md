# All analytic sextic reference germs, with a sectorially flat perturbation

With the two sectors and notation of r043-sextic-sector-fold.md replace its pure reference 1 by any real analytic germ H₀ with H₀(0)=1. Assume H−H₀=O_J(|u|^J) on each closed subsector for every J. Then the same exclusion holds.

**Terms already dealt with.** The sectorial Cauchy bounds give real flatness with all derivatives. In the real conditional map w=u+bu³H(u) all large negative-b outer coefficients are eventually strictly positive for all even N, uniformly for b<−B, by the far-branch argument already given. There only convergence in a complex neighborhood of the scaled outer branch matters, and H(eU)→1 there with all fixed derivatives. With H₀≠1 the simple real fold has ρ_e=ρ+O(e), still uniformly bounded above and below; the relative O(1/N) Darboux remainder and sign are unchanged. For the central branch the cumulative increments for H and H₀ have the same asymptotic Taylor expansions to every fixed order after b integration. Exactly as in the earlier proof, on |b|≤|w|^−1/2 both central roots are O(|w|), their maps differ there by O_J(|b||w|^{J+3}), and the reference derivative is ≥1/2. The central analytic reference inverse in |W|<η, W=|b|^{1/2}w, gives uniform Taylor remainders because its normalized map U±U³H₀(eU) is uniformly close to U±U³. For bounded b the reference central inverse has a fixed physical neighborhood of analyticity. All other cutoffs and the discard of the extreme b tail in the cumulative increment are unchanged. We need the cumulative integral of the analytic-reference central density coefficients, and a lower bound large enough to dominate the bounded-b geometric terms.

**Uniform two-fold estimate for the analytic central reference.** Set S=sign b, e=|b|^−1/2 and F_e(U)=U+S U³H₀(eU). Its central inverse at W=0 carries density K_e(W)=g(eU)dU/dW in physical density units. For S=−1 the unperturbed central branch has two simple real folds W=±ρ at U=±s, s=1/√3; for S=+1 they occur at W=±iρ, U=±is. The central cubic inverse takes bounded values on |W|≤ρ (polynomial root bound) and is simple except at these two points. Patch its extension on a compact neighborhood of the closed disk minus small fold neighborhoods by uniform Rouché. Near both folds the local square-root coordinates are available simultaneously since H₀ analytic at zero permits all compact scaled U. The patching works on a fixed slightly enlarged disk |W|<ρ+η cut along the two outward radial slits starting at the perturbed critical values W_j, j=1,2. The cuts may rotate O(e); a fixed small neighborhood of each fold is disjoint from the other and its complement connects on an interior crescent, as in the previous proof. At each fold subtract L_j(e)(1−W/W_j)^−1/2, with the branch equal to one at W=0, using the local central inverse. The residual is uniformly bounded on the slit domain, including at both branchpoints. By the Cauchy keyhole estimate,
[W^N]K_e(W) = C_N ∑_j L_j(e)W_j^−N + O((|W_1|^−N+|W_2|^−N)/N).
All L_j are continuous near e=0 and have the same strictly positive real limit L₀=g(0)/(2√(3sρ)). For S=− this follows from the positive real derivative into each real fold, or reflection at the negative fold; for S=+ first rotate W=iT, U=iV on the unperturbed cubic, which gives the former real configuration with derivative dU/dW=dV/dT. For S=+ the two W_j and L_j are conjugates; for S=− both amplitudes are real positive for small e.

Consequently for b<−B and N even all central coefficients are positive and bounded below by c₀ C_N |b|^{N/2}(min_j |W_j|)^−N. On |b|=b_*+O(1), b_*=√(cN/2), the N/2th Gaussian moment carries fixed relative mass by the log-density curvature estimate. Since W_j=±ρ+O(e), these terms alone grow supergeometrically even allowing exp(−CN^{3/4}) losses.

**H₀'(0)=a≠0.** For S=− the fold values are ±ρ−ae/9+O(e²) because the unperturbed critical derivative in U vanishes, and for S=+ they are ±iρ+ae/9+O(e²). Thus min_j |W_j|≤ρ−|a|e/10 after shrinking e in the real case, whereas |W_j|≥ρ−C e² in the imaginary case. Integrated over the favorable large negative-b window |b|∈[b_*,b_*+1], the real pair gives ≥c₀ C_N ρ^−N E|b|^{N/2} exp(c₁ N^{3/4}). For b≥N^{1/3} the absolute imaginary-pair contribution is at most C C_N ρ^−N E|b|^{N/2} exp(C N^{2/3}), directly from the uniform two-fold formula. On B≤b<N^{1/3} a fixed smaller scaled disk bounds all coefficients by (C√b)^N, hence O(C^N N^{N/6}); the moment has Nth root comparable to N^{1/4}. The real term therefore dominates along all large even N.

**H₀'(0)=0.** For positive b the critical value W₊(e) near iρ and its critical point are analytic in e near zero. Put W₊(e)=iρ exp[L(e)+iP(e)] on the real e-axis. Since F_{−e}(−U)=−F_e(U) and F_e is real on the real axis, W₊(−e)=−conjugate(W₊(e)). Thus L is even and P odd, both real analytic and zero at e=0. The vanished linear coefficient gives L(e)=O(e²), P(e)=p₃ e³+O(e⁵). Take an unbounded sequence N∈4ℕ for which p₃(2/c)^{3/4}N^{1/4} tends to zero modulo 2π: if p₃≠0 take nearest multiples of four to (2πj/(|p₃|(2/c)^{3/4}))^4; otherwise use all such N. On |b−b_*|≤N^η, 0<η<1/4, we then have NP(b^−1/2)→0 modulo 2π uniformly because the cubic term varies from its central value by O(N^{η−1/4}) and N b^−5/2=O(N^−1/4). Including L_j(e)→L₀>0, the pair in the coefficient formula on this window is positive and ≥c₀ C_N |W₊|^−N b^{N/2}.

The normalized positive weight in this last integral including the Gaussian density is proportional to exp[f_N(b)−N L(b^−1/2)], f_N(b)=(N/2)log b−b²/(2c). On b≥b_*/2 the derivative of N L(b^−1/2) has absolute value ≤C N/b²≤C by analyticity and evenness; f_N''≤−1/c everywhere and ≥−2/c on [b_*,b_*+1]. Thus the integral on |b−b_*|>N^η in this region is o(1) times its mass on that unit window, by exp[−(b−b_*)²/(2c)+C|b−b_*|]. The pair coefficients on this exterior window have absolute value at most C C_N ρ^−N times the same weight. On N^{1/3}≤b<b_*/2, loss from f_N(b_*) is ≥c₂N whereas both the correction and its central value are O(N^{2/3}); the interval length is polynomial. The range B≤b<N^{1/3} is geometrically negligible relative to the central window exactly as before (the latter loses at most exp[−C√N] from L). Hence the positive-b integrated pair is positive on the selected N. Adding the negative-b positive central and outer terms now contradicts analyticity. The factor 1/(N+1) in passing to cumulative increments has no effect.

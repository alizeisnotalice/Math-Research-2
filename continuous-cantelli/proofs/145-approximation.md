# Uniform real-transform approximation by bounded continuous graphs with a negative survivor

Fix the three-layer relaxed law of round 128 at ε=1/8, with s=1−ε, J=[2,2+ε^(3/2)/100], early measure η and late measure P_εη. All temporal marks lie in {s,1,1+ε}. The following is a deterministic approximation theorem. For any enumeration of distinct nonzero rational tilts z_j there are smooth graphs f_n such that

* 1−2ε≤f_n≤1+2ε, with f_n identically one outside a compact set depending on n;
* f_n(2)=1−ε and f_n(3)=1+ε, and E f_n=1;
* the real-transform residual is zero at z₁,…,z_n and its supremum over the **entire real line** is at most 2^(−n);
* the graph laws converge weakly to the fixed three-layer joint law, the transforms converge locally uniformly in the complex plane, and at one common fixed time a fixed nonnegative smooth spatial test detects a strictly negative survivor with a uniform margin for all large n.

This sequence fails local equicontinuity on every open interval. The result identifies a concrete failure of the compactness hypothesis in the proposed counterexample route even with exact growing finite constraints and successful held-out tests.

**Uniform approximants before calibration.** Truncate the target joint law outside [−R,R] by returning its marks to 1. For R>3 this only changes the late layer. The real-transform error at every real z has absolute value at most ∫_{|x|>R}exp(x²/2)(P_εη)(dx): optimize exp(zx−z²/2) in z. This tail tends to zero since η has compact support and the convolution variance ε<1. On the compact interval partition into small cells and subdivide each into up to three intervals whose Gaussian masses equal the cell's three target layer masses. Assign the three constant marks there. The resulting marked integrals converge uniformly over the entire real family: on this compact set and mark range the kernels exp(zx−z²F/2) have a uniform bound and a uniform modulus in x, by Gaussian damping in z. Replacing x by a representative of each cell bounds the error uniformly. The same construction gives weak convergence by uniform continuity for compactly supported continuous tests. Smooth the finitely many interfaces, including at the truncation endpoints, over arbitrarily small total Gaussian measure using smooth cutoffs and values between the extreme marks. In two additional tiny neighborhoods impose the specified anchor values by smooth convex interpolation. The compact-domain uniform bound controls all modifications. Taking successive R and finer partitions gives smooth baseline approximants g_k with real residual supremum tending to zero, correct anchors, and weak convergence. All can be chosen equal to 1 outside a compact set. Uniform convergence on compact complex-parameter sets follows by Gaussian tail domination exp(C|x|+C') and equicontinuity in the parameter.

**Exact finite calibration.** For a fixed prefix z₁,…,z_n include the mean as an extra constraint. Under a perturbation by a compact smooth h, the limiting derivative functionals have spatial weights 1 and

    w_j(x)=−q_j exp(z_jx) E[exp(−q_j F)|X=x],    q_j=z_j²/2.

These weights are linearly independent on the negative half-line. There the early layer vanishes; the conditional late probability (P_εη)(x)/g(x) tends to zero as x→−∞ since ε<1. Thus each w_j(x) is a nonzero constant times exp(z_jx)(1+o(1)). In a putative linear relation discard zero coefficients, order the remaining distinct exponents including zero for the mean, and divide by the dominant exponential at −∞. This contradicts its nonzero coefficient. The weights are continuous there. Consequently one can choose n+1 smooth compactly supported bumps h_i on that half-line giving an invertible matrix of integrals (otherwise the range on all test functions would have a nonzero annihilator).

For g_k+Σλ_i h_i the constraint residual at λ=0 tends to zero and the Jacobians tend to the invertible limiting matrix by weak convergence, including the mean by boundedness. On a fixed small coefficient ball the second derivatives in λ are uniformly bounded by compact support and bounded marks. Hence for all sufficiently large k there is λ_k→0 giving zero residual: multiply by the inverse base Jacobian and use the contraction mapping on a ball of twice the resulting base residual. For this fixed prefix the calibrated graphs still converge weakly and preserve their anchors. On the supports of the bumps, positivity F≥1−2ε bounds sup_z q exp(zx−qF) by a finite constant, so even the uniform real-transform error still tends to zero. Diagonalize, choosing the k for each prefix sufficiently large to give 2^(−n), keep the correction sup norm below min(ε,1/n), and take k increasing. This yields all stated convergence and range assertions.

**Heat and compactness checks.** Choose ψ≥0 smooth, nonzero and supported inside the interval J on which the round 128 negative estimate holds. Against this test the survivor is right-continuous at s, so choose one t strictly between s and 1 with a negative test value. The trace kernel 1_{F≤t}P_{t−F}ψ(x) is bounded and continuous except possibly on {F=t}; the limiting joint law gives that set zero mass. Thus weak convergence gives a strict uniform negative margin for the eventual graphs. The compact value range allows a locally equicontinuous subsequence on any positive-length compact interval to converge there along a further subsequence uniformly to a single graph. But on every open subinterval the target has both late and middle conditional masses strictly positive at almost every x (including on J by the bound in round 128). Its restriction cannot be a graph law. This contradicts the already identified weak limit and rules out such subsequences. No exact continuous nonconstant limiting graph follows.

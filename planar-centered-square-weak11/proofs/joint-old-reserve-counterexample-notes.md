# Joint old-source reserve: a minimal failed auxiliary claim and bounded tests

Date: 2026-11-15. New draft after the finite atomic platform files were frozen. No changes to the frozen proof/script/result. No commit or push.

The new candidate is |A(μ,εδ_p)|≤M+4ε, or the stronger |A|≤M, for every finite positive atomic μ of total mass M. Here A is the strict-threshold-1 pure mixed halo, common-radius centered axis-parallel squares and all r>0. This note records the initial exploration; the stronger candidate |A|≤M has since been proved sharply for arbitrary finite positive measures in `point-insertion-shell-budget.md`. The exploratory computation below is historical evidence and is not used to justify that proof.

## The auxiliary claim |H(μ)|≥M is false for two atoms

Take μ=δ_(−1/10,0)+δ_(1/10,0), total mass M=2. The complete halo is the union of the singleton rectangles and the two-source rectangle. The singleton rectangles have side 1 and their union lies in

    (−3/5,3/5)×(−1/2,1/2).

The two-source rectangle is

    E=(−(sqrt(2)−1/5)/2,(sqrt(2)−1/5)/2)
        ×(−sqrt(2)/2,sqrt(2)/2).

Indeed it is the set of centers whose maximum distance to the two atoms is strictly less than sqrt(2)/2. Since sqrt(2)>7/5, this E contains both singleton halo rectangles. Therefore the exact complete halo is E, and

    |H(μ)|=sqrt(2)(sqrt(2)−1/5)=2−sqrt(2)/5<2=M.

This is a genuine two-dimensional area computation, using common-radius squares. The rational root comparison follows from 2>49/25. One atom always has halo area equal to its mass, so two atoms are minimal for this failure. P(μ)=M, even if available independently for this input, does not establish a lower bound on receiver area.

## Exact one-old-atom insertion bound

For μ=mδ_q, ν=εδ_p, any mixed witness square contains p and q and has 4r²<m+ε. Consequently its center lies in the singleton halo H((m+ε)δ_p). Removing H(εδ_p) gives

    A(μ,ν) ⊂ H((m+ε)δ_p) \ H(εδ_p),
    |A(μ,ν)|≤m.

This includes p=q and all positive masses and distances. It is sharp if p=q and ε≥m, because then H(μ)⊂H(ν) and the annular square area is exactly m. Thus a universal bound |A|≤M, if true, would have best coefficient 1. This argument cannot be summed over source subsets without paying the same old source repeatedly.

## Bounded exploratory computation

The new script `computation/joint_old_reserve_small_probe.py` enumerates all nonempty subsets for at most five source atoms, builds every valid receiver rectangle with its exact subset mass (converted to floating square roots only for union-area exploration), and computes union area by a planar rectangle sweep. Testing each subset is complete: every strict witness captures one such subset, and every receiver rectangle built from any subset supplies a strict witness capturing at least that subset. Degenerate rectangles are omitted.

The script tests exactly 100 seeded small inputs (seed 20261115), cycling over two to five distinct old atoms and ε/M∈{1/100,1/10,1,10}. It uses exact rational input coordinates and masses; square roots and areas are floating point for exploration only. Its floating output is exploratory evidence for those inputs, not a certificate of either general candidate. A numerical violation would need an independent exact geometric proof. The final JSON records the actual menu, numerical maxima, arithmetic, version, and limitations. The sample is deterministically generated by a seeded pseudorandom generator (seed 20261115); no optimization solver is used.
